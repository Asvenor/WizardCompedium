# High-Level Information Warfare
<!-- wizard-upgrade:start -->
[[Home]] · [[Combat Dashboard]] · [[Problem Navigator]]

> [!note] Scope
> Information-gathering priorities are tactical analysis. No personal enemy profile or campaign log is required in this reference.

<!-- wizard-upgrade:end -->


> **At high levels, information can decide encounters before Initiative is rolled.**
>
> A prepared Wizard should attempt to:
>
> **Find → Identify → Verify → Predict → Conceal → Deceive → Exploit**
>
> The goal is not simply knowing more.
>
> The goal is entering situations with **asymmetric information**.

# Information Advantage

## Choose the tool by its actual limit

| Tool | Useful job | Operating limit / counter |
|---|---|---|
| Find Familiar | Low-cost mobile reconnaissance | Telepathy within 100 feet; shared senses use a Bonus Action and last until your next turn. A distant scout must bring information back rather than provide unlimited-range telepathy. |
| Arcane Eye | Invisible, invulnerable visual scout | Concentration up to 1 hour; Bonus Action moves it 30 feet. Needs a gap at least 1 inch wide; provides vision, not hearing. |
| Scrying | Observe a creature or a previously seen location | Creature target must be on the same plane and gets a Wisdom save modified by familiarity/connections. A successful save prevents your retry against it for 24 hours. The sensor does not supply map coordinates. |
| Contact Other Plane | Up to five concise questions | DC 15 Intelligence save on casting; failure deals 6d6 Psychic damage and Incapacitates until a Long Rest or Greater Restoration. Successful contact is not omniscience: the entity may not know. |
| Divination | Advice about one goal/event within seven days | 25+ GP incense consumed. Repeating before a Long Rest adds a cumulative 25% no-answer chance per casting after the first. Circumstances can change the answer. |
| Sending | Brief communication without line of sight | 25 words and an immediate same-length reply; cross-plane delivery has a 5% failure chance you recognize. A recipient can block you for 8 hours. |
| Nondetection | Protect against Divination and scrying sensors | 8 hours and 25+ GP dust consumed; not invisibility or protection from mundane witnesses. |
| Mind Blank | Strong personal mental/information protection | 24 hours, no ongoing Concentration; protects the target, not every ally, room, document, or inference about their activities. |

**Optimization:** use the cheapest source that answers the actual question, then distinguish observation from inference. An enemy being absent from a sensor's view is not proof that the location is empty.

Source: the named entries in the [2024 PHB spell descriptions](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/phb-2024/spell-descriptions). Access, unreliable witnesses, and what information an NPC knows remain campaign-dependent.

Information changes spell selection.

Knowing that an enemy:

- Flies
- Teleports
- Uses Counterspell
- Has Legendary Resistance
- Has Truesight
- Resists Fire
- Is immune to Charm
- Has poor Dexterity
- Relies on Concentration
- Can Plane Shift

can completely change the correct strategy.

Therefore:

> **Reconnaissance is part of combat optimization.**

---

# Intelligence Cycle

Use a simple cycle:

**Question**

↓

**Collect**

↓

**Verify**

↓

**Analyze**

↓

**Prepare**

↓

**Act**

↓

**Update**

Do not collect information without knowing what decision it is supposed to improve.

---

# Priority Questions

Before a dangerous encounter, determine as many of these as practical.

## Enemy

- What creature is it?
- What creature type?
- Approximate CR / threat level?
- Spellcaster?
- Legendary Resistance?
- Magic Resistance?
- Important immunities?
- Important resistances?
- Special senses?
- Teleportation?
- Flight?
- Regeneration?
- Concentration-dependent abilities?
- Dangerous reactions?
- Escape abilities?

## Environment

- Room dimensions?
- Ceiling height?
- Entrances?
- Exits?
- Chokepoints?
- Cover?
- Difficult terrain?
- Environmental hazards?
- Civilians?
- Reinforcements?
- Escape routes?

## Strategic

- What does the enemy want?
- Will they fight?
- Can they retreat?
- Do they know we're coming?
- Are they expecting magic?
- Do they have allies nearby?
- What happens if they escape?

# Reconnaissance

Start with the cheapest information source.

Do not cast powerful divination magic when mundane observation solves the problem.

Information sources can include:

- [[Find Familiar]]
- Party scouts
- NPC contacts
- Prisoners
- Witnesses
- Documents
- Maps
- Research
- Libraries
- Divination
- Scrying
- Interrogation
- Observation
- Previous encounters

### Principle

> **Use low-cost intelligence first. Escalate to magic when necessary.**

---

# Remote Scouting

[[Find Familiar]] is one of the Wizard's best low-cost intelligence tools.

Potential uses:

- Scout corridors
- Look through windows
- Inspect rooftops
- Observe guards
- Find alternate entrances
- Identify room layouts
- Check terrain
- Watch enemy movement
- Search inaccessible spaces

The familiar can answer questions before the party exposes itself.

---

# Familiar Reconnaissance Pattern

**Familiar scouts**

↓

**Map enemy position**

↓

**Identify terrain**

↓

**Locate entrances / exits**

↓

**Return information**

↓

**Select spells**

↓

**Engage**

This can turn:

> **Unknown encounter**

into:

> **Prepared encounter**

without spending a meaningful combat resource.

---

# Divination

High-level Wizards have access to numerous ways of obtaining information.

Think about divination by **question type**, not merely spell name.

## Location

> Where is something?

Potential tools:

- Locate effects
- Scrying
- Contact-based information
- Tracking
- Familiar reconnaissance

## Identity

> What is this?

Potential tools:

- Arcana
- Identify
- Legend Lore
- Research

## Future

> What is likely to happen?

Potential tools:

- Divination effects
- Contact Other Plane
- Allied divine magic
- [[Wish]] access to appropriate spells

## Capability

> What can this enemy do?

Potential tools:

- Research
- Previous encounters
- Observation
- Creature knowledge
- Divination
- Captured documents

---

# Scrying

Scrying allows remote observation of a creature.

Its value is not merely:

> **"I can see the target."**

Its value is potentially learning:

- Current location
- Allies
- Environment
- Security
- Activity
- Vulnerabilities
- Travel patterns

One successful observation can substantially change encounter planning.

---

# Scrying Questions

Before Scrying, decide what you actually want to learn.

Examples:

> Is the target alone?

> Where are they sleeping?

> What defenses surround them?

> Are they carrying the object?

> Who are they meeting?

> What room are they currently in?

> Are they preparing to leave?

Do not waste magical intelligence by observing without an objective.

---

# Scrying Preparation

The more information and connection you possess about a target, the better your position may be depending on the exact spell rules.

Therefore collect:

- Name
- Description
- Portrait
- Personal knowledge
- Possessions
- Physical samples
- Location history

when strategically useful.

Check the exact current [[Spells|Scrying]] rules before applying modifiers.

---

# Contact Other Plane

Contact Other Plane can provide information unavailable through conventional investigation.

Potential uses:

- Hidden locations
- Enemy intentions
- Historical events
- Magical mysteries
- Planar information
- Unknown threats

However, answers may be limited.

Therefore:

> **Ask high-value questions.**

---

# Question Design

Bad:

> "Tell me everything about Strahd."

Better:

> "Does Strahd possess an item capable of preventing teleportation?"

> "Is the missing artifact currently inside Castle Ravenloft?"

> "Is the creature we encountered a Fiend?"

Questions should produce **actionable information**.

---

# Legend Lore

Legend Lore is valuable for important:

- Creatures
- Objects
- Places
- Historical mysteries

Use it when conventional research cannot reveal enough.

Its value increases when the subject is important enough that even partial information could change strategy.

---

# Wish as Intelligence Access

[[Wish]] gives access to eligible level 1–8 spells from outside the Wizard list through spell duplication.

This massively expands the Wizard's potential information toolkit.

Before attempting a risky custom Wish for information:

> **Check whether an existing level ≤8 spell can answer the question.**

Safe spell replication is preferable.

---

# Information Verification

Never trust one source automatically.

Information can be:

- Wrong
- Outdated
- Incomplete
- Manipulated
- Illusory
- Deliberately planted

When the stakes are high:

> **Verify important intelligence through an independent source.**

---

# Two-Source Rule

For campaign-critical information, try to obtain:

**Source A**

+

**Independent Source B**

Example:

**Scrying**

+

**Familiar reconnaissance**

or:

**NPC testimony**

+

**Divination**

or:

**Captured document**

+

**Direct observation**

Agreement increases confidence.

Disagreement tells you something is wrong.

---

# Information Confidence

You can mentally classify intelligence as:

| Confidence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Confirmed** | Directly verified |
| **High** | Multiple reliable sources |
| **Medium** | Credible but unverified |
| **Low** | Single questionable source |
| **Unknown** | Assumption |

Do not build a complex plan around **Low Confidence** information without acknowledging the risk.

---

# Target Profiling

For major enemies, compare the facts that can change the plan.

| Information | Tactical decision |
|---|---|
| Identity, creature type, location | Establish eligible spells and reachable targets |
| Confirmed facts versus unknowns | Avoid treating guesses as reliable defenses or weaknesses |
| Resistances and immunities | Choose useful damage and conditions |
| Saves and Legendary Resistance | Decide between save pressure, ability checks, and terrain |
| Movement and escape abilities | Test whether containment or kiting can work |
| Senses | Evaluate concealment, invisibility, and scouting routes |
| Spellcasting and reactions | Plan range, sight denial, interruption, and reaction baiting |
| Exploitable weaknesses | Select the lowest-cost reliable answer |
This should be concise enough to use during play.

---

# Spellcaster Profiling

Against enemy casters, prioritize discovering:

- Counterspell access
- Dispel Magic
- Concentration spells
- Teleportation
- Defensive reactions
- Flight
- Summoning
- Save DC
- Spellcasting ability
- Range
- Escape options

See your anti-caster tactics.

---

# Pre-Combat Intelligence

Before a planned encounter, information should influence preparation.

Example:

**Enemy cannot fly**

→ vertical control becomes stronger.

**Enemy has poor Dexterity**

→ Dexterity-save spells gain value.

**Enemy has Magic Resistance**

→ attacks / no-save effects become more attractive.

**Enemy has teleportation**

→ ordinary containment becomes unreliable.

**Enemy has Truesight**

→ some illusion strategies lose value.

**Enemy relies on Concentration**

→ repeated damage / disruption becomes valuable.

---

# Preparation Conversion

The intelligence loop should end with:

**Information**

↓

**Prepared spells**

↓

**Positioning**

↓

**Equipment**

↓

**Tactics**

If nothing changes after gathering intelligence, ask whether the information was actually useful.

---

# Counterintelligence

Information warfare works both ways.

Assume intelligent enemies may attempt to learn:

- Your location
- Spell selection
- Allies
- Bastion
- Clone location
- Demiplanes
- Teleportation Circle sigils
- Magic items
- Plans
- Weaknesses

A high-level Wizard should deliberately control what enemies can discover.

---

# Anti-Divination

Potential tools include:

- [[Long-Term Magical Defenses#Private Sanctum|Private Sanctum]]
- Nondetection
- Mind Blank
- Physical secrecy
- Demiplanes
- Compartmentalized information

The goal isn't necessarily permanent invisibility.

It is making enemy intelligence:

**Harder**

**Less reliable**

**More expensive**

---

# Private Sanctum

[[Long-Term Magical Defenses#Private Sanctum|Private Sanctum]] is especially valuable for sensitive locations.

Potential uses:

- Planning rooms
- Laboratories
- Clone infrastructure
- Spell archives
- Planar Binding chambers
- Diplomatic meetings
- Bastion command areas

Depending on selected effects, it can interfere with:

- Observation
- Divination
- Teleportation
- Planar access
- Communication

See [[Long-Term Magical Defenses]].

---

# Nondetection

Nondetection is useful when a particular:

- Creature
- Object
- Location

must resist divination.

Potential applications:

- Important party member
- Critical object
- Secret mission
- Sensitive cargo

Its duration and component requirements make it more tactical than permanent infrastructure.

---

# Mind Blank

At high levels, Mind Blank becomes an extremely powerful information-security tool.

It protects against major forms of:

- Psychic interference
- Information extraction
- Divination
- Mental intrusion

For a Wizard expecting high-level magical opposition, this can be as strategically important as conventional defensive magic.

---

# Mind Protection

Information can also be extracted from people.

Potential threats include:

- Charm
- Domination
- Telepathy
- Detect Thoughts
- Interrogation
- Magical coercion
- Memory manipulation

Therefore:

> **A secret is only as secure as every person who knows it.**

---

# Information Compartmentalization

Do not give everyone every secret.

Example:

### Party

Knows you maintain emergency infrastructure.

### Trusted Ally

Knows how to contact you.

### Wizard

Knows exact Clone location.

### Nobody Else

Knows the full network.

This reduces the damage caused by:

- Capture
- Charm
- Betrayal
- Mind reading
- Interrogation

---

# Need-to-Know Principle

For sensitive infrastructure:

> **Give each person only the information necessary for their role.**

Especially protect:

- [[Clone]] locations
- [[Demiplane]] contents
- [[Teleportation Circle Network|Teleportation Circle]] sigils
- Glyph triggers
- Passwords
- Guardian commands
- Backup locations

---

# Spellbook Information Security

Your spellbook should contain spell formulas.

It should not unnecessarily contain:

- Clone coordinates
- Passwords
- Teleportation sigils
- Demiplane inventory
- Secret allies
- Security architecture

See [[Spellbook Redundancy & Acquisition]].

If the book is stolen, losing magical knowledge is already bad.

Do not also hand the enemy your infrastructure map.

---

# Secure Communication

Information is vulnerable while being transmitted.

Potential communication tools include:

- Sending
- Telepathy
- Familiar
- Messengers
- Written messages
- Teleportation
- Face-to-face meetings

Choose communication based on:

- Urgency
- Distance
- Sensitivity
- Risk of interception
- Need for confirmation

---

# Sending

Sending is excellent for rapid long-distance communication.

Use it for:

- Warnings
- Status reports
- Short instructions
- Coordination
- Emergency communication

Its word limit forces concise communication.

For sensitive information, avoid including unnecessary details.

---

# Communication Codes

For repeated operations, establish simple predefined phrases.

Example:

> **"Library closed."**

could mean:

> Do not use the normal Teleportation Circle. Destination compromised.

Keep codes simple.

Complicated codebooks become unusable during actual play.

---

# Deception

Information warfare isn't only hiding information.

You can also influence what enemies believe.

Potential tools:

- Illusions
- Disguise
- False documents
- Decoy locations
- Misleading rumors
- Fake spellbooks
- Decoy treasure
- Misleading travel patterns

Use deception when the enemy is actively gathering intelligence.

---

# Decoy Infrastructure

Against sophisticated enemies, you may deliberately expose:

- Fake vault
- Fake Clone chamber
- Fake research
- Fake escape route

while the real infrastructure exists elsewhere.

This is advanced and usually unnecessary unless enemies are specifically hunting your systems.

---

# Illusion Intelligence Warfare

Illusion magic can manipulate:

- Visual information
- Sound
- Apparent numbers
- Apparent positions
- Apparent threats
- Apparent terrain

The goal doesn't always need to be:

> **"Enemy believes the illusion forever."**

Sometimes:

> **"Enemy wastes one round or chooses the wrong route."**

is enough.

---

# Information Denial

Sometimes the best information strategy is simply preventing observation.

Potential methods:

- Darkness
- Fog
- Walls
- Private Sanctum
- Physical barriers
- Distance
- Invisibility
- Silence
- Destroying documents

If enemies cannot observe what you're doing, their decision quality decreases.

---

# Captured Enemy Intelligence

After defeating intelligent enemies, search for:

- Maps
- Orders
- Letters
- Spellbooks
- Journals
- Keys
- Codes
- Sigils
- Plans
- Supply records
- Contact lists

Information may be more valuable than gold.

---

# Enemy Spellbooks

Captured spellbooks provide two types of intelligence:

## Capability

What spells did the enemy know?

## Acquisition

What spells can you copy?

See [[Spellbook Redundancy & Acquisition]].

---

# Prisoners

Prisoners can provide information about:

- Numbers
- Leadership
- Layout
- Patrols
- Defenses
- Objectives
- Morale

But prisoners can:

- Lie
- Be mistaken
- Know only part of the plan

Verify important claims.

---

# Information Warfare vs Divination Spam

Do not burn every spell slot asking questions that mundane investigation could answer.

Good information warfare uses:

**Cheapest reliable method first.**

Example:

**Ask witness**

before:

**Scrying**

before:

**Contact Other Plane**

before:

**Wish**

Escalate only when necessary.

---

# Information Value

Ask:

> **What decision will this information change?**

High-value:

> Does the boss have teleportation?

because it changes containment strategy.

Lower-value:

> What color is the boss's bedroom?

unless that somehow matters.

---

# Unknowns List

Before major operations, distinguish unknown defenses from confirmed ones.

Example:

- Can target teleport?
- Does target have Legendary Resistance?
- Number of guards?
- Second exit?
- Counterspell?

Then decide which unknowns are worth resolving.

This prevents unconscious assumptions.

---

# Pre-Operation Intelligence Checklist

Before a major planned encounter:

- Target identified
- Creature type known
- Location known
- Environment understood
- Entrances identified
- Escape routes identified
- Important resistances checked
- Important immunities checked
- Special senses checked
- Teleportation checked
- Spellcasting checked
- Legendary Resistance checked
- Reinforcements considered
- Enemy awareness considered
- Retreat plan established

You do not need every box for every fight.

Use this for encounters where preparation is actually possible.

---

# Counterintelligence Checklist

For important operations:

- Can enemies Scry us?
- Can they read thoughts?
- Can they locate an important object?
- Do they know our destination?
- Do they know our Teleportation Circle?
- Is communication secure enough?
- Does everyone need to know the full plan?
- Are sensitive documents protected?
- Are backup locations secret?
- Are we revealing predictable patterns?

---

# Information Escalation Ladder

Use:

### Level 1 — Mundane

Observation, research, witnesses, maps.

### Level 2 — Familiar / Scout

Remote reconnaissance.

### Level 3 — Basic Magic

Detection and identification.

### Level 4 — Divination

Scrying, Legend Lore, Contact Other Plane, etc.

### Level 5 — Cross-Class Access

[[Wish]] replication where appropriate.

### Level 6 — Extreme Measures

Only when the information genuinely justifies the resource or risk.

---

# Information Doctrine

> **The strongest spell is often the spell you knew to prepare.**

Information creates that advantage.

A high-level Wizard should attempt to know:

**Who**

they are fighting,

**Where**

they are fighting,

**What**

the enemy can do,

**How**

the enemy can escape,

and

**What the enemy knows about them.**

The objective is not omniscience.

It is:

> **Enter important encounters knowing more about the enemy than the enemy knows about you.**

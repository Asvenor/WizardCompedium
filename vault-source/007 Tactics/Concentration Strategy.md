# Concentration Strategy
<!-- wizard-upgrade:start -->
> [!important] Preserving Concentration is not always preserving the effect
> Fireball can burn away overlapping Web cubes in one round. Web → Fireball preserves the concentration slot but may remove the restraint you wanted to sustain. Use nonoverlapping placement or make that damage-for-control trade deliberately. [Web](https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2619208-web).

A Wizard normally has **one Concentration slot**. Treat it as a major combat resource.

## Core Rule

> Cast the Concentration spell that best solves the encounter, then protect it instead of replacing it for marginal value.

## Choosing the Concentration Spell

| Situation                          | Strong Answers            |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Grounded group                     | Web                       |
| Mixed group / Charm works          | Hypnotic Pattern          |
| Charm unreliable / allies mixed in | Slow                      |
| Enemy casters / ranged formation   | Sleet Storm               |
| Single-target removal              | Banishment, [[Polymorph]] |
| Hard encounter split               | Wall of Force             |
| Sustained manipulation             | Telekinesis               |
| Boss removal without initial save  | Maze                      |

## Protecting Concentration

Priority order:

1. **Avoid the damage entirely** — range, cover, movement, walls.
2. **Prevent the hit** — AC, [[Spells|Shield]], defensive features.
3. **Improve the save** — Constitution proficiency, War Caster, Bladesong, bonuses.
4. **Do not expose yourself unnecessarily** once the spell is already winning.

### Concentration Save

Use the ordinary save math below unless a feature removes that failure route. [[Concentration#Specific feature exceptions|Boon of the Iron Mind and Focused Conjuration]] protect different scopes; neither gives you a second Concentration effect.

**DC = 10 or half the damage taken (round down), whichever is higher, to a maximum DC of 30.**

| CON Save Bonus | DC 10 | DC 10 with Advantage | DC 15 | DC 15 with Advantage |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| +2 | 65% | 87.8% | 40% | 64% |
| +5 | 80% | 96% | 55% | 79.8% |
| +8 | 95% | 99.8% | 70% | 91% |
| +11 | 100% | 100% | 85% | 97.8% |

## After Control Lands

Good follow-up turns:

- Non-Concentration damage
- Cantrips
- Dodge
- Reposition
- Use an item
- Protect allies
- Counterspell
- Forced movement into the existing control zone

### Example

**Turn 1:** Web  
**Turn 2:** Fireball  
**Turn 3:** Synaptic Static  
**Turn 4:** Dodge / cantrip / reposition

Do **not** cast another Concentration spell just because it is higher level.

## When to Drop Concentration

Drop or replace it when:

- The controlled targets are already defeated.
- The battlefield has moved away from the effect.
- The enemy is immune or the spell is no longer producing value.
- A new threat requires a clearly stronger Concentration answer.
- The current spell prevents a necessary rescue or escape.

## Special Cases

- **Conjurer 10:** damage cannot break Concentration on qualifying Conjuration spells; other ways of ending Concentration still matter.
- **Arcane Abeyance:** another creature can become the caster of the released stored spell, including its Concentration.
- **Simulacrum:** a second creature means a second Concentration slot.
- **Glyph of Warding:** a stored spell can operate without a creature maintaining its normal Concentration when the glyph rules apply.

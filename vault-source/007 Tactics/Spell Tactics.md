# Core Combat Doctrine

## Win Before Damage

A Wizard's first priority is usually not maximizing personal DPR.

Prioritize:

1. **Act early**
2. **Identify the encounter's main threat**
3. **Establish the correct control effect**
4. **Protect Concentration**
5. **Exploit the advantage already created**
6. **Spend additional resources only when necessary**

A spell that removes several enemy turns can outperform a spell that simply deals more damage.

---

## Concentration First

Your Concentration slot is one of your most important combat resources.

Once a strong Concentration spell is already controlling the encounter:

> **Do not replace it merely because you have another powerful Concentration spell available.**

### Example

**Turn 1:** Web

**Turn 2:** Fireball

**Turn 3:** Synaptic Static

**Turn 4:** Cantrip / Dodge / reposition / item

This is a menu, not a fixed rotation: Fireball can ignite Web and undo the control you intended to preserve. Reassess friendly fire, terrain, and whether more damage actually removes an enemy action.

See [[Concentration Strategy]].

# Battlefield Control

| Opener | Operating limit that determines the follow-up |
|---|---|
| **Web** | Anchor it between solid masses or layer it on a floor/wall/ceiling; otherwise it collapses at your next turn's start. Surface layers are only 5 feet deep. Dexterity saves occur on first entry on a turn or turn start; escaping normally costs an action and a Strength (Athletics) check. Fire can burn the web away. |
| **Hypnotic Pattern** | Only creatures that see the pattern are affected. Charmed immunity defeats it; any damage or another creature's action to shake the victim ends that victim's effect. Isolate disabled threats while defeating unaffected ones. |
| **Sleet Storm** | The revised Dexterity save can cause both Prone and lost Concentration, on first entry on a turn or turn start. It is not a separate Constitution concentration save; ordinary War Caster does not protect that Dexterity save. Heavy Obscurement also obstructs your side's sight. Specific Concentration-protection features can override the loss. |

Sources: [Web](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/phb-2024/spell-descriptions#Web), [Hypnotic Pattern](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/phb-2024/spell-descriptions#HypnoticPattern), [Sleet Storm](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/phb-2024/spell-descriptions#SleetStorm). For protection exceptions, see [[Concentration#Specific feature exceptions]].

## Web + Forced Movement

**Rating:** S

### Setup

1. Cast Web across an important area.
2. Maintain Concentration.
3. Attack affected enemies from range.
4. Force enemies into disadvantageous positions.
5. If enemies escape, consider forcing them back into the Web rather than replacing it.

### Good Partners

- Thunderwave
- Thorn Whip
- Vortex Warp
- Gravity Sinkhole
- Allied push/pull effects
- Movement reduction

### Why It Works

Web can repeatedly attack enemy:

- Movement
- Actions
- Positioning
- Defenses

Restrained enemies are easier to attack, while escaping the Web can consume valuable enemy resources.

---

## Hypnotic Pattern + Focus Fire

**Rating:** S

### Setup

1. Cast Hypnotic Pattern over the largest useful enemy cluster.
2. Identify which creatures failed.
3. Do **not** damage incapacitated targets unnecessarily.
4. Kill unaffected enemies first.
5. Once active enemies are defeated, select one incapacitated enemy.
6. Focus the party on that target.
7. Repeat.

### Principle

Instead of fighting:

**6 enemies simultaneously**

you potentially create:

**2 enemies → 1 enemy → 1 enemy → 1 enemy**

The spell's value comes from reducing the enemy's effective action economy.

### Watch For

- Charm immunity
- Damage waking targets
- Allies waking targets
- Friendly fire
- Concentration

---

## Sleet Storm + Ranged Party

**Rating:** S

### Setup

1. Place Sleet Storm over the enemy formation.
2. Keep the party outside the area.
3. Force enemies to move through the storm.
4. Attack creatures as they emerge.
5. Use movement reduction and forced movement to keep enemies separated.

### Why It Works

Sleet Storm attacks multiple resources simultaneously:

- Movement
- Sight
- Positioning
- Concentration
- Enemy formation

### Best Against

- Spellcasters
- Ranged formations
- Large groups
- Slow melee enemies

### Watch For

- Blocking allied sight
- Concentration
- Party members entering the area

# Forced Movement

## Forced Movement + Persistent AoE

**Rating:** S

Forced movement becomes significantly stronger when an area has effects triggered by creatures:

- Entering
- Starting their turn
- Ending their turn
- Moving through the area

### Setup

1. Establish the persistent area.
2. Position enemies near its boundary.
3. Force enemies into advantageous positions.
4. Exploit additional valid triggers where the exact spell permits them.
5. Coordinate forced movement across the party.

### Movement Tools

- Thunderwave
- Thorn Whip
- Vortex Warp
- Gravity Sinkhole
- Telekinetic effects
- Allied push/pull abilities

### Possible Zones

- Wall of Fire
- Sickening Radiance
- Web
- Environmental hazards
- Other persistent effects

> [!warning] Trigger Wording
> Always read the exact spell.
>
> **Entering an area**, **starting a turn there**, **ending a turn there**, and **the area moving onto a creature** are not automatically equivalent.

# Layered Control

## Transmute Rock + Concentration Control

**Rating:** A+

Transmute Rock can create persistent battlefield alteration without consuming Concentration afterward.

It requires appropriate **nonmagical rock, mud, or quicksand**, not arbitrary ground. Rock-to-mud costs 4 feet of movement per foot and can Restrain on a Strength save; escaping that mud takes an action without a check. Mud-to-rock instead uses a Dexterity save and its own DC 20 Strength-check/damage escape. Do not treat the two modes as the same save-and-escape engine. [Transmute Rock](https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2403-transmute-rock).

### Setup

**Transmute Rock**

↓

**Alter battlefield**

↓

**Cast Concentration control**

↓

**Enemy deals with both simultaneously**

### Good Follow-Ups

- Web
- Sleet Storm
- Sickening Radiance
- Slow
- Wall of Force

### Why It Works

The first spell changes the battlefield.

The second spell controls creatures trying to operate inside that battlefield.

---

## Synaptic Static + Ability Checks

**Rating:** A+

Synaptic Static is particularly useful when enemies must subsequently make important ability checks.

### Possible Partners

- Web escape attempts
- Maze escape attempts
- Ability checks to escape grapples (not the initial 2024 Unarmed Strike grapple save)
- Environmental checks

### Why It Works

Synaptic Static is **non-Concentration**, allowing it to layer with an existing Concentration control spell.

This makes it more than a damage spell when the encounter contains important ability checks.

The muddle subtracts 1d6 from **attack rolls, ability checks, and Constitution saves to maintain Concentration**, not every saving throw. The target repeats its Intelligence save at each turn's end to end the muddle. An initial 2024 grapple uses a Strength or Dexterity save; the normal action to escape uses Athletics or Acrobatics and can receive the penalty. [Synaptic Static](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/phb-2024/spell-descriptions#SynapticStatic) · [Grappling](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/phb-2024/rules-glossary#Grappling).

# Wall of Force Tactics

## Wall of Force + Persistent Hazard

**Rating:** S+

The basic concept:

**Hazard**

↓

**Enemy enters hazard**

↓

**Wall of Force prevents escape**

↓

**Hazard continues producing value**

### Classic Example

**Sickening Radiance + Wall of Force**

This normally requires **two Concentration sources**.

Possible sources:

- Second caster
- Simulacrum
- Other valid external Concentration source

For extreme Concentration interactions, see [[Arcane Exploits]].

**Sequence and payoff:** establish the hazard before sealing the enclosure; an invisible wall can still block the clear path required for later targeting. Sickening Radiance checks creatures on their first entry on a turn or at their turn's start, not simply because its area appeared around them. Two casters need legal initiative/action timing; a solo two-round setup gives the enemy a response window. The combo is worthwhile only when containment alone does not already solve the encounter. [Wall of Force](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/phb-2024/spell-descriptions#WallofForce) · [Sickening Radiance](https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/14597-sickening-radiance).

### Watch For

- Teleportation
- Disintegrate
- Concentration
- Wall geometry
- Line of effect
- Enemy abilities capable of bypassing containment

---

## Wall of Force + Environmental Hazard

**Rating:** S

This version can avoid the second-Concentration problem entirely.

Use an existing hazard such as:

- Lava
- Fire
- Flooding
- Dangerous machinery
- Collapsing structures
- Existing magical effects
- Other environmental threats

Then use Wall of Force primarily as containment.

### Principle

> Let the environment provide the damage while your Concentration provides the prison.

---

## Wall of Force + Faithful Hound

**Rating:** A+

Mordenkainen's Faithful Hound can provide a persistent threat without requiring Concentration.

**2024 operating limits:** at the start of **your** turn, one enemy within **5 feet of the hound** makes a Dexterity save or takes **4d8 Force** damage. This is not an attack roll or a continuous area-damage aura. Moving the hound up to 30 feet takes your Magic action on a later turn, and the spell ends if you are ever more than 300 feet from it. Keep the hound and intended target on the same side of the force barrier. [Faithful Hound](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/phb-2024/spell-descriptions#MordenkainensFaithfulHound).

### Setup

1. Establish the Hound in the intended area.
2. Position or lure enemies nearby.
3. Cast Wall of Force.
4. Maintain the Wall.
5. Allow the Hound to continue threatening eligible targets.

### Watch For

- Hound positioning
- Target eligibility
- Wall geometry
- Teleportation
- Line-of-effect interactions

# Mobility & Kiting

## Phantom Steed Kiting

**Rating:** S

Phantom Steed is one of the Wizard's strongest mobility tools and can fundamentally change combat on open battlefields.

The steed has extreme movement while requiring no Concentration and potentially no spell slot when created through Ritual casting.

### Core Strategy

**Start outside enemy threat range**

↓

**Move into effective spell range**

↓

**Cast**

↓

**Retreat using mounted mobility**

↓

**Force enemies to spend movement and actions reaching you**

↓

**Repeat**

The objective is not simply to move quickly.

The objective is to repeatedly enter **your** effective range without remaining inside **the enemy's** effective range.

### Why Kiting Works

Most creatures have substantially less mobility than Phantom Steed.

If an enemy cannot:

- Reach you
- Attack effectively at range
- Teleport
- Disable the mount
- Control your movement

then distance becomes a defensive resource.

Every turn an enemy spends:

- Dashing
- Repositioning
- Chasing
- Navigating terrain

is a turn where its offensive action economy is reduced.

### Best With

#### Control

- Sleet Storm
- Web
- Wall of Force
- Slow
- Hypnotic Pattern

#### Damage

- Fireball
- Synaptic Static
- Long-range cantrips

#### Movement Denial

Effects that reduce enemy movement make kiting considerably stronger.

You do not necessarily need to stop an enemy.

You need:

**Enemy ability to close distance < Your ability to maintain distance**

---

## Phantom Steed + Sleet Storm

**Rating:** S+

A particularly strong mobility-control combination.

### Engine

**Sleet Storm**

↓

**Enemy movement and visibility are disrupted**

↓

**Phantom Steed creates distance**

↓

**Enemies struggle to close**

↓

**Party attacks from range**

The enemy's mobility becomes worse while yours remains extremely high.

---

## Phantom Steed + Difficult Terrain

Any effect that increases the movement required to reach you improves kiting.

Examples:

- Web
- Sleet Storm
- Natural Difficult Terrain
- Chokepoints
- Elevation
- Environmental obstacles

Movement denial does not need to completely immobilize enemies to be valuable.

---

## Party Kiting

With sufficient preparation, multiple Phantom Steeds can potentially provide mounts for several party members.

This can turn a ranged party into a highly mobile formation.

Especially useful with:

- Archers
- Ranged Warlocks
- Ranged spellcasters
- Mobile summons
- Other mounted characters

A mobile party is substantially harder to counter than a single mounted Wizard leaving the rest of the group behind.

---

## Protect the Steed

The mount is the primary vulnerability.

Enemies may realize:

> **They do not need to catch the Wizard if they can damage the steed: any damage ends Phantom Steed.**

Its one-minute fading period allows dismounting; it is not an extra minute of guaranteed combat service. A controlled mount moves on the rider's turn and has Dash, Disengage, or Dodge as its action options. See [Phantom Steed](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/phb-2024/spell-descriptions#PhantomSteed) and [Mounted Combat](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/phb-2024/playing-the-game#MountedCombat).

Protect it through:

- Distance
- Cover
- Line-of-sight control
- Positioning
- Terrain
- Target denial

Avoid exposing the steed unnecessarily.

### Best Environments

**Excellent**

- Plains
- Roads
- Deserts
- Large caverns
- Open battlefields
- Large outdoor encounters

**Poor**

- Small rooms
- Tight dungeons
- Narrow corridors
- Heavy obstacles
- Extreme Difficult Terrain
- Areas unsuitable for mounted movement

### Main Counters

- Enemy ranged attacks
- Enemy spellcasters
- Teleportation
- Very fast creatures
- Flying enemies
- Difficult Terrain
- Movement-control effects
- Readied attacks
- Mount targeting
- Confined environments

### Tactical Principle

> **Defense is not only AC and saving throws. Distance is defense.**

If an enemy spends its turn trying to reach you while you continue producing useful spell effects, Phantom Steed is converting **movement advantage into action-economy advantage**.

See [[Movement & Travel]] for overland Phantom Steed calculations.

# Target Priority

## Enemy Spellcasters

Enemy casters should rarely be treated like ordinary enemies.

Prioritize:

- Breaking line of sight
- Counterspell
- Concentration disruption
- Forced movement
- Sleet Storm
- Isolation
- Range control

See [[Countering Spellcasters]].

---

## Bosses

Do not automatically dump damage into a boss.

First identify:

- Legendary Resistance
- Strong and weak saves
- Teleportation
- Condition immunities
- Concentration
- Legendary actions
- Reactions
- Minions
- Escape abilities

Sometimes the optimal move is controlling the **rest of the encounter** while temporarily isolating the boss.

See [[Legendary Resistance & Bosses]].

# First-Turn Priority

A Wizard's first turn often has disproportionate value.

Before acting, ask:

### Can I remove enemies from the fight?

- Hypnotic Pattern
- Web
- Wall of Force
- Banishment
- Maze

### Can I destroy enemy positioning?

- Sleet Storm
- Forced movement
- Walls
- Terrain alteration

### Is immediate damage actually better?

Potentially when:

- A large group is clustered
- Targets are vulnerable
- Several enemies are already near death
- Control is unlikely to work
- Immediate removal through damage prevents enemy turns

### Am I in danger?

Reposition before committing Concentration if necessary.

See [[Initiative & First-Turn Strategy]].

---

# Resource Discipline

Do not judge a spell only by whether it produces value.

Ask:

> **Did I need to spend this resource to win the encounter?**

If Web is already winning:

**Do not replace it with a 5th-level Concentration spell just because you can.**

If a cantrip finishes the target:

**Do not spend Fireball.**

If positioning solves the problem:

**Do not spend Dimension Door.**

An optimized Wizard preserves resources while maintaining encounter control.

# Tactical Decision Loop

^73c134

During combat:

**1. What is currently threatening the party most?**

↓

**2. Can I remove or neutralize it?**

↓

**3. Is my current Concentration already winning?**

↓

**4. Can positioning or distance solve the problem?**

↓

**5. Can a non-Concentration spell improve the situation?**

↓

**6. What is the cheapest resource that reliably solves the problem?**

↓

**7. What can the enemy do to break my plan?**

This should be the default Wizard combat thought process.

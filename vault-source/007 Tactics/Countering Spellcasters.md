# Countering Spellcasters
<!-- wizard-upgrade:start -->
> [!important] Cover and isolation tradeoffs
> Ordinary spell targeting does not pass through Total Cover. A proposed Ready behind cover → move → release technique needs its targeting/timing ruling and full costs: an Action-casting-time spell, casting resources spent when readied, Concentration while held, and a Reaction to release. It can end current control and leave no Shield/Counterspell Reaction. Simply stepping out to cast and back again does not hide the casting. Also, isolating a caster does not by itself end its existing Concentration; an ongoing hazard, buff, or summon may remain useful. Countering a spellcaster is not just casting Counterspell. The strongest plan attacks **sight, range, Concentration, positioning, and action economy**.

## 1. Win Initiative

Acting first can prevent the enemy caster from establishing:

- Concentration
- Summons
- Defensive buffs
- Escape routes
- Battlefield control

See [[Initiative & First-Turn Strategy]].

## 2. Deny Sight

Many spells require the caster to see a target or location.

Useful tools:

- Sleet Storm
- Fog Cloud
- Walls
- Total Cover
- Darkness / obscurement
- Forced movement behind terrain

Against multiple casters, sight denial can outperform trading Counterspells one-for-one.

## 3. Counterspell

### 2024 Operating Notes

- Reaction
- 60-foot range
- Requires the relevant visible casting trigger
- The enemy caster makes a **Constitution saving throw**
- On a failed save, the spell is interrupted
- Under the current 2024 treatment in this compendium, the enemy's action is wasted but the spell slot is not expended

### Use Counterspell When

The denied spell is worth:

- Your Reaction
- Your slot
- Giving up Shield, Absorb Elements, Chronal Shift, Temporal Shunt, or another Reaction

Do not Counterspell minor effects automatically.

## 4. Break Concentration

Sometimes damaging or disabling the caster is cheaper than using Dispel Magic.

Strong methods:

- Focus fire
- Multiple damage instances
- Incapacitation
- Forced movement into hazards
- Effects that directly attack Concentration
- Arcana Unleashed: Disruptive Tune when appropriate

These are normal failure routes, not universal counters. [[Concentration#Specific feature exceptions|Boon of the Iron Mind and Focused Conjuration]] can protect particular routes; against them, distinguish disrupting the caster's Concentration from dispelling or otherwise ending the spell itself.

## 5. Dispel Ongoing Magic

Use Dispel Magic when:

1. There is a valid ongoing effect.
2. Removing it now changes the encounter.
3. Breaking the caster's Concentration is not easier.
4. You know what target/effect you are actually dispelling.

Dispel Magic can end multiple qualifying spells on its target, making separate checks for higher-level spells. It does not automatically end nonspell magical effects or override explicit exceptions.

## 6. Isolate the Caster

Strong answers:

- Wall of Force
- Maze
- Forcecage
- Banishment
- Terrain separation
- Forced movement

A caster that cannot see or reach the fight loses many options, but isolation alone does not end existing Concentration. Its ongoing hazards, buffs, or summons can remain relevant.

## 7. Range Management

If the enemy has Counterspell:

- Cast from beyond 60 feet when possible.
- Cast from cover only when the chosen spell still has a legal target and clear path. A proposed Ready → move → release sequence needs the targeting/timing ruling and spends casting resources, Concentration, and a Reaction as described above.
- Force the enemy to spend its Reaction first.
- Use a spell that does not need to enter the counter-war.

## Anti-Caster Priority

| Problem | Answer |
|---|---|
| One decisive spell now | Counterspell |
| Several enemy casters | Sleet Storm / sight denial |
| Existing magical effect | Dispel Magic |
| Concentrating caster | Break Concentration |
| Boss caster | Isolation / no-save pressure |
| Counterspell duel | Range, cover, Reaction baiting |

## Copying Spells into the Spellbook

When you find a Wizard spell in another spellbook or another valid written source, you can copy it into your own spellbook.

### Copying Cost

| Spell Level | Time | Cost |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 2 hours | 50 gp |
| 2 | 4 hours | 100 gp |
| 3 | 6 hours | 150 gp |
| 4 | 8 hours | 200 gp |
| 5 | 10 hours | 250 gp |
| 6 | 12 hours | 300 gp |
| 7 | 14 hours | 350 gp |
| 8 | 16 hours | 400 gp |
| 9 | 18 hours | 450 gp |

The time represents deciphering the other Wizard's notation and reproducing the spell using your own magical notation.

The gold represents inks and other materials consumed while experimenting with and recording the spell.

## Copying from a Spell Scroll

A Wizard can attempt to copy a Wizard spell from a Spell Scroll into their spellbook.

| Rule | Information |
|---|---|
| Required Spell | Must be on the Wizard spell list |
| Spell Level | Must be a level you can prepare |
| Check | Intelligence (Arcana) |
| DC | 10 + spell level |
| Success | Spell is copied into the spellbook |
| Failure | Spell is not copied |
| Scroll | Destroyed after the copying attempt |

Copying uses the normal copying time and cost above **and consumes the scroll whether the Arcana check succeeds or fails**. You cannot copy a scroll and then cast that same scroll. A spell copied from another spellbook does not have this scroll-specific check or destruction rule.

Sources: [2024 PHB — Wizard](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/phb-2024/character-classes-continued/#Wizard), [2024 DMG — Spell Scroll](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/dmg-2024/magic-items-a-z#SpellScroll).

## Replacing a Spellbook

Losing the book does not itself erase the prepared list. You can still cast prepared spells if their other requirements are met, but cannot freely replace them with lost formulas or use Ritual Adept without a readable book. **Memorize Spell at Wizard 5** swaps one level-1+ prepared Wizard spell on a Short Rest only by studying the spellbook; it is not an emergency way to recover every missing spell. [Wizard](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/phb-2024/character-classes-continued/#Wizard).

If your spellbook is lost or destroyed, you can reproduce spells you currently have prepared in a new spellbook.

Copying your own spells is easier because you already understand your own notation.

### Copying Your Own Spell

| Rule | Cost |
|---|---:|
| Time | 1 hour per spell level |
| Gold | 10 gp per spell level |

This also makes it possible to create backup spellbooks.

# Creating Spell Scrolls

A Wizard can create Spell Scrolls during downtime.

The scribe must:

- Have proficiency in **Arcana or Calligrapher's Supplies**
- Have the spell **prepared on every day of inscription**
- Have any required Material components at hand; consumed components are expended **when the scroll is completed**

Creating a scroll requires both **time and gold**. Each listed day means **8 hours of work**; for a multi-day scroll, the days need not be consecutive. A cantrip scroll created this way uses the scribe's level for its scaling, not automatically the eventual reader's level.

Source: [2024 PHB — Scribing Spell Scrolls](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/phb-2024/equipment).

## Material Components When Creating Scrolls

If the spell normally consumes a material component, that component must also be supplied during scroll creation.

### Example

If a spell requires:

**300 gp of diamond dust, consumed**

the scroll costs:

**Scroll creation cost + 300 gp diamond dust**

The consumed component is expended during the creation process.

Expensive components that are **not consumed** still need to be available during the inscription.

# Casting From a Spell Scroll

A Spell Scroll contains a single spell.

## If the Spell Is on Your Spell List

You can use the scroll according to the Spell Scroll rules.

The scroll provides:

- The spell
- Its stored magical energy
- Its normal casting effect

Use the spell's normal casting time, without spending a spell slot or supplying Material components. After the spell is successfully cast, the scroll is destroyed. If casting is interrupted, the scroll is not lost; this differs from failing the higher-level casting check below.

---

## Scroll Above Your Normal Spell Level

If the spell is on your class spell list but is a higher level than you can normally cast, using the scroll requires an ability check.

| Rule | Value |
|---|---|
| Check | Spellcasting ability |
| DC | 10 + spell level |
| Success | Spell is cast |
| Failure | Scroll fails and is destroyed |

# Spell Scroll Statistics

The generic Spell Scroll magic item uses fixed spell attack bonuses and saving throw DCs based on the spell's level.

However, a Spell Scroll created with the 2024 player scribing rules uses **the scribe's spell save DC and spell attack bonus** instead.

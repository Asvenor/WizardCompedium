# Demiplane
<!-- wizard-upgrade:start -->
[[Home]] · [[Wizard Operations Roadmap]] · [[Problem Navigator]]

> [!warning] Rules clarification
> Revised [Demiplane](https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2619088-demiplane) lets each creature choose to be shunted out when its door vanishes, including a hostile prisoner. A Clone awakening after the doorway has already expired still needs an exit plan; see [[Wizard Operations Roadmap#Recovery chain]].

<!-- wizard-upgrade:end -->


> **Demiplane is permanent extradimensional infrastructure.**
>
> Its real value is not simply "a room."
>
> It gives a high-level Wizard access to persistent, isolated spaces that can become storage vaults, preparation chambers, prisons, laboratories, emergency caches, and magical infrastructure.

# Core Rules

| Property | Demiplane |
|---|---|
| **Level** | 8th |
| **School** | Conjuration |
| **Casting Time** | Action |
| **Range** | 60 feet |
| **Duration** | 1 hour |
| **Concentration** | No |

You create a **shadowy door** on a flat solid surface you can see within range.

The door is large enough for a **Medium creature to pass through unhindered**.

Opening it leads to a demiplane.

---

# Demiplane Size

The demiplane is a:

**30-foot cube**

The room is:

- Empty when first created
- Made of wood or stone
- Enclosed
- Extradimensional

The room itself persists after the doorway disappears.

This is the critical distinction:

> **The doorway is temporary. The demiplane is persistent.**

Objects left inside remain there. Creatures can choose to be shunted outside when the door vanishes, landing Prone; creatures that choose to remain stay inside.

---

# Creating Demiplanes

Each casting gives you two broad choices.

## Create a New Demiplane

Create a new empty demiplane.

This allows a Wizard to build **multiple independent rooms** over time.

## Connect to an Existing Demiplane

You can instead have the door connect to a demiplane you previously created with this spell.

This allows repeated access to established infrastructure.

---

# Known Demiplanes

The spell can also potentially connect to a demiplane created by someone else if you know its **nature and contents**.

This creates interesting possibilities involving:

- Allied Wizard infrastructure
- Captured information
- Known extradimensional vaults
- Shared magical facilities

Do not assume you can access an unknown demiplane merely because you know one exists.

---

# Returning to a Demiplane

The most important logistical rule:

> **Do not enter without considering how you are leaving.**

The doorway created by Demiplane lasts for **1 hour**.

When it disappears, a creature inside can choose the spell's exit shunt. If it instead remains inside—or awakens there after the doorway has already gone—it needs a separate exit method. Casting Demiplane inside does not inherently reopen a route to the Material Plane; it connects only to an eligible demiplane. [2024 Demiplane](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/phb-2024/spell-descriptions#Demiplane).

Always plan:

**Entry**

↓

**Task**

↓

**Exit**

before using the room.

---

# Access & Security

Demiplanes have a major natural security advantage:

**There is no permanent physical entrance.**

When Demiplane isn't actively creating a doorway, the room is separated from normal physical access.

This makes it excellent for storing things that should not simply be reachable by:

- Breaking a door
- Picking a lock
- Digging through a wall
- Sneaking into a building

However:

> **Demiplane is hidden, not absolutely inaccessible.**

Powerful planar magic and knowledge of the demiplane can potentially threaten it.

Do not treat it as mathematically invulnerable.

# Glyph Chamber

This is one of the strongest synergies in your compendium.

[[Glyph of Warding]] normally suffers from its critical limitation:

> Moving the warded object or surface more than 10 feet from where Glyph was cast breaks the glyph.

Demiplane solves the **infrastructure problem**, not by moving the glyphs, but by letting **you travel to them**.

---

# Preparation Chamber

Create fixed Glyphs inside the Demiplane. Each lasts until dispelled or triggered; a used glyph is a consumed defense, not a permanently reusable buff dispenser.

Example:

**Enter Demiplane**

↓

**Trigger selected Glyphs**

↓

**Receive stored effects**

↓

**Leave prepared**

Potential benefits include stored spells that normally require Concentration.

See [[Glyph of Warding#Concentration Bypass]].

---

# Why This Works

The glyphs remain fixed inside the room.

You are not transporting them.

Instead:

**The Wizard moves to the prepared infrastructure.**

This respects Glyph of Warding's movement restriction.

---

# Glyph Network

A dedicated Demiplane could contain multiple prepared stations.

Example:

## Defensive Station

Protective stored effects.

## Mobility Station

Movement-related effects.

## Emergency Station

Recovery / escape preparation.

## Combat Station

Pre-combat effects.

Each Glyph costs resources, so build deliberately.

See [[Glyph of Warding]].

---

# Action Economy

Demiplane infrastructure allows some magical preparation to happen **before Initiative**.

Instead of:

**Round 1 → buff**

you may be able to:

**Before encounter → preparation chamber**

then:

**Round 1 → immediately cast encounter-defining spell**

This is one of the reasons high-level Wizard infrastructure becomes so powerful.

---

# Containment & Prison

Demiplane can function as an extradimensional holding location.

Potential targets:

- Prisoners
- Dangerous creatures
- Cursed objects
- Hazardous materials
- Objects that should not remain near civilization

However, imprisonment requires careful analysis.

Ask:

- Can the prisoner cast planar travel?
- Can it teleport?
- Does it need food/water/air?
- Can it destroy stored infrastructure?
- Can allies rescue it?
- Can it access another plane?
- What happens when you reopen the room?

---

# Prison Rule

> **Never place something in a Demiplane merely because you don't know what else to do with it.**

Know how you will:

- Observe it
- Control it
- Retrieve it
- Kill it
- Release it

before storing it.

---

# Anti-Escape Planning

For dangerous prisoners consider interactions with:

- [[Long-Term Magical Defenses#Private Sanctum|Private Sanctum]]
- [[Planar Binding#Inverted Magic Circle|Magic Circle]]
- [[Glyph of Warding]]
- Bound guardians
- Physical restraints
- Magical restraints

Always verify whether a spell's area and duration actually support permanent installation.

# Important Clone Warning

Security and accessibility must be balanced.

A backup body hidden somewhere unreachable is not useful if the resurrected Wizard cannot leave.

Always build the **post-death exit plan** before trusting the system.

# Emergency Recovery Room

A mature Wizard might dedicate one Demiplane entirely to catastrophic recovery.

# Dangerous Object Vault

Some objects are too dangerous to keep in a Bastion.

Potential examples:

- Cursed artifacts

- Evidence
- Hazardous components

A dedicated Demiplane prevents these from sharing space with your normal operations.

# Segmentation

Do not necessarily put everything in **one** Demiplane.

A major advantage of the spell is the ability to create separate rooms.

Potential network:

| Demiplane | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Vault** | Treasure / components |
| **Laboratory** | Research / crafting |
| **Preparation Chamber** | Glyph infrastructure |
| **Recovery Room** | Clone / backup equipment |
| **Containment Room** | Dangerous objects |
| **Creature Room** | Bound / created assets |

This prevents one security breach from exposing everything.

---

# Demiplane Network

At high levels, think in terms of a **network**, not one room.

Each room has one job.

This improves:

- Organization
- Security
- Risk isolation
- Retrieval
- Containment
- Preparation

---

# Naming System

These illustrative names distinguish room roles; this reference does not require a personal room index or inventory.

Example:

**DP-01 — Component Vault**

**DP-02 — Glyph Chamber**

**DP-03 — Recovery Room**

**DP-04 — Laboratory**

**DP-05 — Containment**

**DP-06 — Creature Storage**

This is especially useful once you have several rooms.

---

# Storage Roles

Example division of resources:

| Room | Contents |
|---|---|
| DP-01 | Diamonds, Planar Binding jewels, rare components |
| DP-02 | Prepared Glyph network |
| DP-03 | Backup spellbook, equipment, recovery supplies |
| DP-04 | Crafting tools and materials |

Separating these roles limits the consequences of one room being compromised.

---

# Deployment Problem

Demiplane is excellent storage but not necessarily instant battlefield deployment.

The spell costs:

**An 8th-level slot**

and requires creating/opening the doorway.

Therefore:

> **Stored resources should justify the cost and action required to access them.**

Demiplane is infrastructure, not a normal backpack.

---

# Carrying Capacity

The room is only:

**30 × 30 × 30 feet**

That is large, but finite.

Large creatures, structures, and enormous stockpiles consume meaningful space.

Plan accordingly.

---

# Doorway Size

The doorway only allows a **Medium creature to pass unhindered**.

This matters for:

- Large creatures
- Huge creatures
- Vehicles
- Oversized equipment
- Construct deployment

Do not assume every creature or object stored inside can conveniently pass through the door.

This is especially important for your [[True Polymorph]] and [[Planar Binding]] assets.

---

For a valuable Demiplane, think in layers:

**Hidden access**

↓

**Controlled doorway**

↓

**Glyphs**

↓

**Guardians**

↓

**Backup systems**

# Demiplane + Bastion

Your [[Bastion]] and Demiplane should perform different jobs.

## Bastion

Best for:

- Daily operations
- Staff
- Crafting
- Social activity
- Accessible storage
- Normal living

## Demiplane

Best for:

- High-security storage
- Dangerous objects
- Magical infrastructure
- Emergency systems
- Isolation
- Backup assets

Think:

> **Bastion = headquarters**
>
> **Demiplane = secure backend infrastructure**

---

# Demiplane + Wish

[[Wish]] can dramatically expand high-level infrastructure by duplicating eligible spells without normal casting requirements.

Demiplane provides persistent space in which long-term infrastructure can accumulate.

Together they support a Wizard who prepares **systems**, not merely spells.

---

# What Demiplane Does Not Do

Demiplane does **not automatically**:

- Make stored creatures loyal
- Prevent planar travel
- Prevent teleportation
- Feed creatures
- Supply air unless the rules/DM establish it
- Create furniture or equipment
- Protect against Dispel Magic on active effects
- Make Glyph of Warding portable
- Solve Planar Binding containment
- Allow arbitrarily large creatures through the doorway
- Guarantee that nobody else can ever access the plane

Do not give the spell capabilities it doesn't state.

---

# Risk Management

The largest Demiplane mistake is creating a **single point of failure**.

Do not store:

**Every spellbook**

+

**Every component**

+

**Every magic item**

+

**Your Clone**

+

**Every emergency resource**

in one room.

Segmentation is safer.

---

# Priority Build Order

When Demiplane becomes available:

### 1. Emergency / Backup Vault

Backup spellbook + critical equipment.

### 2. Component Vault

Protect expensive and rare materials.

### 3. Glyph Preparation Chamber

Once sufficient gold and downtime exist.

### 4. Recovery Infrastructure

Integrate with [[Clone]] / death planning.

### 5. Specialized Rooms

Containment, creatures, laboratory, etc.

---

# Demiplane Doctrine

> **Demiplane turns a high-level Wizard from an adventurer carrying resources into an adventurer backed by infrastructure.**

Its value scales with:

**Time**

+

**Preparation**

+

**Gold**

+

**Spell knowledge**

+

**Cross-spell interactions**

The spell itself creates only a room.

The Wizard determines whether that room becomes:

**an empty cube**

or

**a permanent magical facility.**

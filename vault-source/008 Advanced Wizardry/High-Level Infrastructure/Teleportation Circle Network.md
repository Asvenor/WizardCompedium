> **Teleportation Circle turns geographic distance into infrastructure.**
> 
> One circle is a destination.
> 
> Several circles become a **transportation network**.

# Core Rules

|Property|Teleportation Circle|
|---|---|
|**Level**|5th|
|**School**|Conjuration|
|**Casting Time**|1 minute|
|**Range**|10 feet|
|**Duration**|1 round|
|**Components**|V, M|
|**Material Cost**|Rare inks worth 50+ GP, consumed|
|**Permanent Construction**|365 daily castings|

The spell creates a temporary portal from your location to:

> **A permanent Teleportation Circle whose sigil sequence you know on the same plane of existence.**

The portal remains until the end of your next turn.

Any creature entering it appears:

**Within 5 feet of the destination circle**

or

**In the nearest unoccupied space if necessary.**

---

# Circle Size

When casting the spell, you draw a:

**5-foot-radius circle**

on the ground.

That gives the active departure circle a:

**10-foot diameter**

---

# Same-Plane Restriction

Teleportation Circle cannot connect different planes.

Therefore:

**Material Plane → Material Plane**

✓

**Material Plane → Feywild**

✗

**Material Plane → Demiplane**

✗

For planar movement use other tools.

See [[Teleportation & Planar Travel]].

---

# Permanent Circle Construction

**Travel is not instant setup:** the ordinary spell takes 1 minute of casting with Concentration, then its portal remains only until the end of your next turn. Position travelers before completion; the brief open window and actual movement matter for large groups or cargo. A permanent destination is not a continuously open portal. [Teleportation Circle](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/phb-2024/spell-descriptions#TeleportationCircle).

You can create a permanent Teleportation Circle by casting the spell:

> **In the same location every day for 365 days.**

This is a serious infrastructure project.

You need:

- Access to the location
    
- Daily availability
    
- A level-5-or-higher spell slot each day (normally use the cheapest eligible slot)
    
- Material components
    
- Security
    
- 365 days
    

---

# Construction Cost

Each casting consumes:

**50+ GP**

of rare inks.

Minimum cost:

**365 × 50 GP**

=

# **18,250 GP**

This is before:

- Construction
    
- Security
    
- Guards
    
- Facility costs
    
- Magical defenses
    
- Opportunity costs
    

---

# Construction Schedule

|Progress|Castings|Minimum Cost|
|---|---|---|
|**1 month**|30|1,500 GP|
|**3 months**|90|4,500 GP|
|**6 months**|180|9,000 GP|
|**1 year**|365|**18,250 GP**|

Permanent construction requires the full sequence of 365 daily castings.

Missing a required daily casting can threaten the project depending on how strictly the DM interprets "every day."

Do not casually begin construction somewhere you cannot reliably revisit.

---

# Spell Slot Cost

Construction also consumes:

**One 5th-level spell slot every day**

for an entire year.

During downtime this may be irrelevant.

During active adventuring it isn't.

If you expect combat that day, the construction project effectively reduces your available high-level resources.

---

# Location Selection

A permanent circle is expensive enough that its location should be strategically valuable.

Good locations include:

- [[Bastion]]
    
- Major allied city
    
- Important guildhall
    
- Secure fortress
    
- Major trade center
    
- Allied kingdom
    
- Remote strategic base
    
- Research facility
    
- Important military location
    

Avoid constructing circles merely because a location is convenient today.

Ask:

> **Will I still care about this destination a year from now?**

---

# The Network Principle

One circle alone gives other Teleportation Circle users a destination.

The real strategic value appears when you know **multiple sigil sequences**.

Example:

```
              [Capital]
                 │
                 │
[Guild] ─── [Bastion] ─── [Port]
                 │
                 │
            [Frontier Base]
```

Your spell becomes the departure mechanism.

Each permanent circle becomes a known destination.

---

# Important: Permanent Circles Are Destinations

Do not think of a permanent circle as a permanent two-way Stargate.

A permanent Teleportation Circle primarily gives you:

> **A stable destination with a unique sigil sequence.**

To travel there, someone casts **Teleportation Circle** and specifies that destination.

The permanent circle itself does not automatically remain an open portal.

---

# Sigil Sequences

Every permanent circle has a unique:

**Sigil Sequence**

Think of this as the circle's magical address.

When you first gain the ability to cast Teleportation Circle, you learn:

**Two destinations on the Material Plane**

chosen by the DM.

You can discover more during play.

---

# Learning New Sigils

To memorize a new sigil sequence:

> **Study it for 1 minute.**

That makes discovering a permanent circle strategically valuable even if you don't control it.

Every known sigil potentially becomes:

**A future destination.**

---

# Sigil Registry

These are illustrative destination roles, not a character-specific registry.

|Code|Location|Plane|Owner|Security|Status|
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|TC-01|Bastion|Material|Party|High|Active|
|TC-02|Capital|Material|Crown|High|Active|
|TC-03|Guildhall|Material|Wizard Guild|High|Active|
|TC-04|Frontier|Material|Party|Medium|Active|

Evaluate each destination by:

- Sigil sequence
    
- Exact location
    
- Owner
    
- Political access
    
- Security
    
- Known hazards
    
- Whether the destination remains trustworthy
    

---

# Information Security

A sigil sequence is effectively:

> **An address to your teleportation destination.**

Treat important sequences as sensitive information.

Do not casually:

- Publish them
    
- Leave them in unsecured notes
    
- Give them to strangers
    
- Display them publicly
    

Especially for:

- Bastions
    
- Military bases
    
- Secret facilities
    
- High-security vaults
    

---

# Compromised Sigils

Once an enemy learns your circle's sigil sequence, assume they may retain it.

You cannot rely on secrecy forever.

Therefore:

> **The destination room itself must be defensible.**

A secret circle with no defenses becomes dangerous once the secret leaks.

---

# Circle Security

A permanent circle should ideally exist in a dedicated chamber.

Think:

**Teleportation Chamber**

rather than:

**Circle in the middle of my bedroom.**

Recommended characteristics:

- Controlled doors
    
- Guards
    
- Observation points
    
- Defensive positions
    
- Restricted access
    
- Alarm systems
    
- Magical defenses
    
- Clear kill/control zone around arrival point
    

---

# Arrival Zone

Teleportation Circle has predictable arrival geometry.

Creatures arrive:

**Within 5 feet of the destination circle**

or the nearest available space.

This makes the arrival area defensible.

Potential design:

```
████████████████████████
█                      █
█   Guard Position     █
█                      █
█       ◯ CIRCLE       █
█                      █
█   Guard Position     █
█                      █
██████████ DOOR ████████
```

Anyone arriving appears in a controlled room rather than directly inside sensitive infrastructure.

---

# Layered Circle Security

Potential defensive layers:

**Arrival Chamber**

↓

**Guards**

↓

**Alarm / detection**

↓

**[[Glyph of Warding]]**

↓

**Locked internal access**

↓

**Bastion proper**

This allows legitimate travelers to arrive without granting immediate access to everything you own.

---

# Glyph Integration

[[Glyph of Warding]] pairs naturally with a fixed Teleportation Circle.

The circle doesn't move.

Therefore permanent defensive glyphs can protect:

- Arrival zone
    
- Doors
    
- Restricted areas
    
- Emergency systems
    

Trigger design matters.

Do not create a system that detonates on your allies every time they arrive.

---

# Private Sanctum Interaction

[[Long-Term Magical Defenses#Private Sanctum|Private Sanctum]] and similar anti-teleportation effects require careful placement.

Blocking teleportation directly over your own circle can make your infrastructure unusable.

Instead, design zones intentionally:

**Teleportation Chamber**

→ teleportation allowed

**Secure Interior**

→ teleportation restricted

This creates a controlled magical border.

---

# Access Control

Teleportation Circle itself doesn't inherently authenticate travelers.

Knowing the destination sigil is the important magical requirement.

Therefore actual access control comes from:

- Keeping sigils secret
    
- Physical guards
    
- Controlled rooms
    
- Passwords
    
- Political agreements
    
- Magical defenses
    
- Monitoring
    

The circle is transportation infrastructure.

It isn't automatically a security system.

---

# Bastion Integration

A [[Bastion]] is an ideal network hub.

Your Bastion circle can become:

**Home destination**

for:

- Adventuring return
    
- Allied travel
    
- Trade
    
- Diplomacy
    
- Emergency extraction
    
- Logistics
    

If the campaign uses the 2024 Bastion Teleportation Circle facility, keep its facility rules separate from the normal 365-day spell construction process.

---

# Hub-and-Spoke Network

The simplest architecture:

```
              Capital
                 │
                 │
Guild ─────── BASTION ─────── Port
                 │
                 │
             Frontier
```

Your Bastion becomes the central operational hub.

Advantages:

- Central security
    
- Central logistics
    
- Easy administration
    
- Predictable routes
    

---

# Distributed Network

A more resilient network:

```
Capital ───── Guild
   │            │
   │            │
Bastion ───── Port
   │            │
   │            │
Frontier ─── Fortress
```

This represents political/logistical relationships rather than literal permanent open links.

Because a caster can depart toward any known valid destination on the same plane, the practical network depends on:

- Which circles exist
    
- Which sigils are known
    
- Which destinations are politically accessible
    

---

# Redundancy

Do not rely on one destination.

If your only known circle becomes:

- Occupied
    
- Destroyed
    
- Hostile
    
- Politically inaccessible
    

your network loses much of its value.

Maintain several trusted destinations.

---

# Emergency Destinations

Know at least one circle that functions as:

**Emergency fallback.**

Ideal properties:

- Friendly
    
- Secure
    
- Politically stable
    
- Far from normal enemies
    
- Stocked with basic supplies
    

This gives you somewhere predictable to retreat when another destination becomes unsafe.

---

# Logistics

Teleportation Circle can radically alter logistics.

Potential uses:

- Personnel transport
    
- Component delivery
    
- Reinforcements
    
- Diplomatic travel
    
- Emergency evacuation
    
- Specialist deployment
    
- High-value goods
    
- Messages carried physically
    
- Medical evacuation
    

The spell transports **creatures**, not arbitrary unattended cargo.

Cargo generally needs to be carried by creatures moving through the portal.

---

# Trade Infrastructure

A reliable network can make high-value trade dramatically easier.

Especially for:

- Gems
    
- Magic items
    
- Rare components
    
- Documents
    
- Lightweight luxury goods
    
- Specialist services
    

The limiting factor becomes:

**Spell availability + 50 GP casting cost**

rather than geographic distance.

---

# Economic Threshold

Every trip costs at least:

**50 GP**

in consumed material components.

Therefore Teleportation Circle is poor infrastructure for transporting cheap bulk commodities.

Transporting:

**20 GP of grain**

for:

**50 GP**

is stupid.

Transporting:

**10,000 GP of diamonds**

for:

**50 GP**

is excellent.

---

# High-Value Transport Doctrine

Prioritize:

- People
    
- Intelligence
    
- Magic items
    
- Gems
    
- Spell components
    
- Emergency resources
    
- Rare goods
    

Avoid using the network for low-value bulk transport unless strategic necessity justifies it.

---

# Military Uses

A secure circle network can support:

- Reinforcements
    
- Officer movement
    
- Specialists
    
- Emergency evacuation
    
- Rapid redeployment
    
- Supply of high-value equipment
    

But compromised destination circles are also dangerous.

A military circle should be treated like:

> **A fortified strategic transport terminal.**

---

# Diplomatic Uses

Circle access itself can become politically valuable.

Potential agreements:

- Allied sigil exchange
    
- Restricted diplomatic access
    
- Guild network membership
    
- Emergency mutual aid
    
- Trade agreements
    

A sigil sequence can effectively become a high-level diplomatic credential.

---

# Circle Discovery

When encountering a permanent circle during adventures:

> **Study the sigil.**

It takes only:

**1 minute**

to commit the sequence to memory.

Even if the circle seems irrelevant now, it may become extremely valuable later.

---

# Sigil Acquisition Doctrine

Whenever you visit:

- Major temple
    
- Wizard guild
    
- Royal court
    
- Arcane university
    
- Allied fortress
    
- Important city
    

ask:

> **Is there a permanent Teleportation Circle here?**

If yes:

> **Can I legally gain access to its sigil sequence?**

This gradually expands your network without requiring you to personally spend 18,250 GP on every destination.

---

# Political Access

Knowing a sigil does not mean the owner wants you arriving whenever you like.

Possible restrictions:

- Permission required
    
- Scheduled arrivals
    
- Guild membership
    
- Toll
    
- Diplomatic authorization
    
- Emergency use only
    

These conditions determine whether a known destination is actually usable.

---

# Network Ownership

There is an important distinction:

## Known Circle

You know its sigil.

## Allied Circle

You have permission to use it.

## Controlled Circle

You or your organization controls the location.

## Secret Circle

Very few creatures know it exists.

These are separate access categories: knowing a sigil grants neither ownership nor a safe welcome.

---

# Construction Priorities

If personally constructing circles:

### 1. Bastion / Headquarters

Your primary secure destination.

### 2. Strategic Secondary Base

Another location you control.

### 3. Important Allied Location

If political permission exists.

### 4. Frontier / Campaign Hub

Only if the location will remain strategically important.

Do not spend **18,250 GP + one year** building a destination that will become irrelevant.

---

# Construction Team

One Wizard personally casting the spell for a year is the simplest interpretation.

If your campaign has:

- Multiple capable casters
    
- Organizations
    
- Wizard guilds
    
- Hireable spellcasters
    

ask the DM how the "you can create" wording interacts with shared construction.

Do not assume multiple casters can freely divide the 365 castings.

---

# Network Failure Modes

Plan for:

### Destination Compromised

Enemies control the arrival chamber.

### Sigil Leaked

Enemies know your magical address.

### Political Access Revoked

The circle still exists but arrival may be hostile.

### Circle Destroyed

Destination no longer functions.

### Caster Unavailable

Nobody currently has Teleportation Circle prepared.

### Wrong Plane

Teleportation Circle cannot solve the route.

---

# Pre-Travel Check

Before opening a portal to a potentially dangerous destination:

- Is the circle still allied?
    
- When was it last verified?
    
- Could enemies control it?
    
- Do we have intelligence?
    
- Is there another destination nearby?
    
- Do we have an escape plan?
    

Teleportation is fast.

It is not automatically safe.

# Teleport Comparison

|Need|Best Tool|
|---|---|
|**Known permanent destination**|Teleportation Circle|
|**Fast same-plane strategic travel**|[[Teleportation & Planar Travel#Teleport]]|
|**Cross-plane travel**|[[Teleportation & Planar Travel#Plane Shift]]|
|**Direct planar portal**|[[Teleportation & Planar Travel#Gate]]|
|**Private extradimensional infrastructure**|[[Demiplane]]|
|**Short tactical repositioning**|Dimension Door / Misty Step|

Teleportation Circle is strongest when:

> **Reliability matters more than flexibility.**

---

# Circle vs Teleport

At high levels, [[Teleportation & Planar Travel#Teleport|Teleport]] becomes more flexible.

But Teleportation Circle retains major infrastructure value because:

- Destination is deterministic
    
- Known circle avoids Teleport's location-familiarity uncertainty
    
- Lower-level slot
    
- Supports organizations with appropriate casters
    
- Permanent circles create standardized destinations
    

Think:

> **Teleport = flexible private transport**
> 
> **Teleportation Circle = established transport infrastructure**

---

# Demiplane Difference

[[Demiplane]] is not part of a normal Teleportation Circle network because Teleportation Circle requires the destination to be:

> **On the same plane of existence.**

Use Demiplane for:

- Secure infrastructure
    
- Storage
    
- Isolation
    

Use Teleportation Circle for:

- Geographic infrastructure
    
- Cities
    
- Bastions
    
- Organizations
    
- Strategic destinations
    

---

# Network Record

Illustrative network roles:

| Destination role | Tactical value | Access dependency |
|---|---|---|
| Bastion | Controlled home destination and recovery resources | Known sigil, same plane, secure arrival, legal access |
| Capital guildhall | Services, allies, and transport connections | Guild permission and an uncompromised destination |
| Frontier fortress | Emergency extraction near a dangerous region | Allied permission, suitable defenses, and onward travel |

This becomes more valuable as the campaign grows.

---

# Network Doctrine

> **Teleportation Circle doesn't remove travel.**
> 
> It changes travel from a distance problem into an **infrastructure, information, and access problem**.

Its strategic value scales with:

**Known Sigils**

-   
    

**Controlled Destinations**

-   
    

**Alliances**

-   
    

**Security**

-   
    

**Redundancy**

-   
    

**High-value logistics**

A Wizard with Teleportation Circle knows a spell.

A Wizard with ten trusted sigil sequences and several controlled circles owns a **transportation network**.

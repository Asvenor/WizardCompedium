# Wish
<!-- wizard-upgrade:start -->
[[Home]] · [[Wizard Operations Roadmap]] · [[Problem Navigator]]

<!-- wizard-upgrade:end -->


> **Wish is not primarily a "make a wish" spell.**
> 
> For an optimized Wizard, its baseline power is:
> 
> **Any spell of level 8 or lower → 1 Action → no casting requirements → no costly components → no Wish stress.**
> 
> Reality-changing Wishes are the emergency option, not the default use.

# Core Rules

|Property|Wish|
|---|---|
|**Level**|9th|
|**School**|Conjuration|
|**Casting Time**|Action|
|**Range**|Self|
|**Components**|V|
|**Duration**|Instantaneous|

Wish has three broad modes:

1. **Duplicate a spell of level 8 or lower**
    
2. Use one of Wish's listed alternative effects
    
3. **Reshape Reality** with a custom Wish
    

These should not be treated as equally desirable.

---

# Spell Duplication

The basic and generally strongest use of Wish:

> Duplicate **any other spell of level 8 or lower**.

This is not restricted to Wizard spells.

You effectively gain access to:

**Wizard**

**Cleric**

**Druid**

**Bard**

**Paladin**

**Ranger**

**Sorcerer**

and other eligible spell lists through one prepared spell.

## Critical Advantage

When duplicating a spell:

> You don't need to meet **any requirements to cast that spell**, including costly components.

The spell simply takes effect.

This mode does **not** cause Wish stress.

---

# Why Spell Duplication Is Broken

Normally spellcasting is restricted by:

- Class spell list
    
- Prepared spells
    
- Spellbook
    
- Components
    
- Component cost
    
- Casting time
    
- Available spell slots
    
- Situational preparation
    

Wish ignores several of these constraints simultaneously.

Instead of asking:

> **"What should I Wish for?"**

first ask:

> **"Is there a level 1–8 spell that already does what I want?"**

If yes:

**Duplicate the spell.**

This is usually safer and more predictable than Reshape Reality.

---

# Cross-Class Access

Wish effectively turns every eligible spell list into an extension of your Wizard toolbox.

This is particularly valuable for effects Wizards normally cannot access.

When evaluating Wish targets, search:

- Cleric spells
    
- Druid spells
    
- Bard spells
    
- Paladin spells
    
- Ranger spells
    
- Other eligible spells
    

Do not restrict your thinking to the Wizard list.

---

# Casting-Time Bypass

One of Wish's strongest interactions is often overlooked.

Wish itself takes:

**1 Action**

The duplicated spell simply takes effect.

This means spells that normally require long casting times can potentially become **Action-speed effects** through Wish.

This is enormously valuable.

Look specifically for powerful spells with normal casting times of:

- 1 minute
    
- 10 minutes
    
- 1 hour
    
- Longer ritual/setup periods
    

Wish can turn preparation magic into immediate magic.

---

# Component Bypass

Wish duplication ignores casting requirements, including costly components.

This makes expensive spells especially attractive duplication targets.

Ask:

> **What spell would I normally avoid because its component is expensive or difficult to obtain?**

Wish can potentially remove that obstacle.

This matters enormously for high-level Wizard infrastructure.

---

# Best Replication Target Criteria

A spell becomes an especially attractive Wish target when it has one or more of these properties:

|Property|Value|
|---|---|
|**Not normally available to Wizard**|★★★★★|
|**Expensive component**|★★★★★|
|**Long casting time**|★★★★★|
|**Situational spell you wouldn't prepare**|★★★★☆|
|**Permanent / long-duration effect**|★★★★☆|
|**Powerful emergency effect**|★★★★☆|
|**Normally difficult logistical requirement**|★★★★★|

The strongest Wish targets often bypass **multiple limitations simultaneously**.

# Best Replication Targets

## Clone

Excellent infrastructure use.

Normally requires:

- Expensive material components
    
- Significant setup
    
- Long maturation period
    

Wish can remove the casting requirements.

See [[Clone]] if present.

---

## Simulacrum

One of the classic Wish targets.

Normally expensive and slow.

Wish potentially turns it into:

**1 Action**

with the casting requirements bypassed.

See [[Simulacrum]].

> [!warning]  
> Infinite Simulacrum/Wish chains are notorious table-breaking territory.
> 
> Treat recursive duplication exploits separately from normal optimization.

---

## Resurrection Magic

Wish gives the Wizard access to powerful restorative magic outside the normal Wizard toolkit.

Potential emergency applications include duplicating appropriate resurrection or restoration spells of level 8 or lower.

This makes Wish an extraordinary party insurance policy.

---

## Restoration / Condition Removal

Wish can access spells that solve:

- Conditions
    
- Curses
    
- Ability problems
    
- Other magical afflictions
    

without requiring you to normally prepare or even possess the spell.

Before using a custom Wish to remove an affliction:

> **Check whether an existing level ≤8 spell already solves it.**

---

## Transportation

Wish can duplicate situational transportation spells without requiring them to occupy your normal preparation.

This can provide:

- Emergency escape
    
- Strategic travel
    
- Planar solutions
    
- Extraction
    
- Rescue
    

---

## Divination

Wish can access situational information spells from across spell lists.

This is particularly valuable because divination spells are often powerful but too specialized to prepare every day.

# Wizard Infrastructure

Wish becomes exceptionally powerful during downtime.

Instead of thinking:

> **"How much damage can my 9th-level slot do today?"**

think:

> **"What permanent or long-duration advantage can today's Wish create?"**

Potential infrastructure categories:

- Backup bodies
    
- Defensive preparation
    
- Magical transportation
    
- Information gathering
    
- Restoration
    
- Bastion improvements
    
- Demiplane systems
    
- Long-duration magic
    

See:

- [[Demiplane]]
    
- [[Simulacrum]]
    
- [[Glyph of Warding]]
    
- [[Planar Binding]]

# Downtime Wish

A 9th-level slot is renewable.

That means on days where you expect no major combat, an unused Wish represents an opportunity.

Potential routine:

**Wake**

↓

**Use safe Wish replication for infrastructure**

↓

**Long Rest eventually restores 9th-level slot**

↓

**Repeat on another downtime day**

Over long periods, Wish's power compounds.

---

# Listed Alternative Effects

2024 Wish provides several explicit alternatives to spell duplication.

These include:

## Object Creation

Create one nonmagical object worth up to:

**25,000 GP**

Maximum dimension:

**300 feet**

The object appears on the ground in an unoccupied space you can see.

---

## Instant Health

You and up to **20 visible creatures**:

- Regain all Hit Points
    
- End effects listed in Greater Restoration
    

This is an enormous emergency recovery button.

---

## Resistance

Grant up to **10 visible creatures** Resistance to one chosen damage type.

The Resistance is **permanent**.

This is potentially extremely powerful long-term character improvement.

---

## Spell Immunity

Grant up to **10 visible creatures** immunity to one spell or other magical effect for:

**8 hours**

Extremely situational but potentially encounter-breaking when the threat is known beforehand.

---

## Sudden Learning

Replace one feat with another feat for which you qualify.

Restrictions still apply to prerequisite relationships.

This allows high-level build reconfiguration.

The feat being replaced must be **one the caster has**. It cannot be replaced if it is a prerequisite for another feat or feature the caster has, and **all of its benefits are lost**. The replacement still requires its normal prerequisites; this does not let a level-17 Wizard take a level-19 Epic Boon early or gain a second copy of a nonrepeatable feat.

For Intelligence optimization, count the **net change**, not just the new feat's increase. At level 19+, exchanging a feat with no INT increase for an eligible +1 INT Epic Boon can gain one point above 20. Exchanging a +1 INT feat for it gains no INT, and exchanging a +2 INT ASI loses one point instead, assuming the new increase is not capped. See [[Intelligence above 20#Worked routes to 24 or 26]].

This option triggers **Wish stress**, including the **33% chance of never being able to cast Wish again**. It trades away the old feat and is not a safe, repeatable way to acquire free Epic Boons.

Source: [2024 PHB — Wish](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/phb-2024/spell-descriptions#Wish).

---

## Roll Redo

Undo a die roll from within the previous round.

You can force the reroll with:

- Advantage
    
- Disadvantage
    

and choose whether the original or new result applies.

This can reverse:

- Failed critical saves
    
- Enemy Critical Hits
    
- Important failed checks
    
- Catastrophic attack outcomes
    

---

# Important: Listed Effects Still Cause Stress

The safe exception is:

> **Duplicating another spell of level 8 or lower.**

Using Wish for another effect exposes you to Wish stress.

That includes Wish's predefined alternative effects.

Therefore:

**Permanent Resistance**

may be extremely powerful...

but you are gambling your future access to Wish to obtain it.

---

# Wish Stress

If Wish produces an effect **other than duplicating another spell**, you suffer stress.

Until completing a Long Rest:

Whenever you cast another spell:

**1d10 Necrotic damage × spell level**

This damage cannot be reduced or prevented.

---

# Strength Collapse

After Wish stress:

**Strength becomes 3**

for:

**2d4 days**

Resting and performing only light activity accelerates recovery.

This makes risky Wish particularly unsuitable immediately before continued adventuring unless absolutely necessary.

---

# 33% Wish Loss

The most important cost:

> After suffering Wish stress, there is a **33% chance you can never cast Wish again.**

For a Wizard who has built significant strategy around Wish:

**this is catastrophic.**

Therefore the default doctrine should be:

> **Do not accept Wish stress for something a level ≤8 spell can accomplish.**

---

# Reshape Reality

You can state a Wish outside the listed effects.

This is where Wish becomes genuinely open-ended.

However, the DM has broad authority over the result.

Possible outcomes include:

- Full success
    
- Partial success
    
- Unintended consequences
    
- Failure
    
- Literal interpretation
    
- Major narrative consequences
    

The more ambitious the Wish:

**the greater the risk.**

---

# Reshape Reality Doctrine

Use custom Wishes when:

- No existing level ≤8 spell solves the problem
    
- The listed Wish effects don't solve it
    
- The situation is genuinely campaign-defining
    
- Failure is otherwise catastrophic
    
- The potential reward justifies possibly losing Wish forever
    

Do **not** use custom Wish casually.

---

# Wording

When using Reshape Reality:

**Be precise.**

But do not assume legalistic paragraphs guarantee safety.

Wish explicitly gives the DM broad latitude.

A 500-word contract does not override the spell.

The best protection is not clever wording.

It is:

> **Use spell duplication whenever possible.**

---

# Emergency Wish

Risky Wish becomes rational when the alternative is worse than losing Wish.

Examples:

- Preventing campaign-ending catastrophe
    
- Saving the entire party
    
- Reversing an irreversible disaster
    
- Stopping a world-scale threat
    
- Solving a problem no existing spell can address
    

At that point:

**33% chance of losing Wish**

may be preferable to:

**100% chance of disaster.**

---

# Wish + Simulacrum

Wish and [[Simulacrum]] have historically produced some of the most extreme Wizard optimization interactions.

Wish can bypass Simulacrum's:

- Long casting time
    
- Costly components
    

making it dramatically easier to deploy.

However:

> [!warning]  
> Recursive Simulacrum/Wish chains can destroy encounter and campaign balance.
> 
> Keep theoretical exploits separate from normal operating doctrine.

See [[Simulacrum]] and [[Arcane Exploits]].

---

# Wish + Glyph of Warding

Wish can potentially provide access to situational spells that can then support your broader prepared infrastructure.

However, Wish itself is not a method for making Glyph of Warding portable.

See [[Glyph of Warding]].

---

# Wish + Demiplane

Demiplane gives you persistent infrastructure.

Wish gives you universal spell access.

Together they support:

- Backup systems
    
- Stored resources
    
- Long-term magical infrastructure
    
- Secure preparation
    
- Emergency contingencies
    

See [[Demiplane]].

---

# Wish + Planar Binding

Before attempting some elaborate custom Wish involving extraplanar creatures:

Check whether Wish can simply duplicate an appropriate:

- Summoning spell
    
- Calling spell
    
- Containment spell
    
- Utility spell
    

and combine it with [[Planar Binding]].

Existing spells are safer than custom reality alteration.

---

# What Wish Does Not Mean

Wish is enormously powerful, but don't automatically interpret:

> **"You don't need to meet any requirements to cast that spell"**

as:

> **"Every rule written inside the duplicated spell disappears."**

Wish bypasses the requirements **to cast** the duplicated spell.

The duplicated spell still produces its actual effect according to its rules.

This distinction matters for optimization.

---

# Wish Priority

When considering Wish, use this order:

### 1. Existing Spell?

Can a level ≤8 spell solve the problem?

**Yes → duplicate it.**

### 2. Listed Wish Effect?

If no spell solves it, does one of Wish's explicit alternatives solve it?

**Yes → evaluate whether Wish stress is worth it.**

### 3. Reshape Reality?

Only then consider a custom Wish.

---

# Daily Wish Checklist

Before ending a safe adventuring day with your 9th-level slot unused:

> **Could today's Wish create a lasting advantage?**

Check:

- Infrastructure
    
- Expensive spell components
    
- Long casting-time spells
    
- Cross-class spells
    
- Restoration
    
- Information
    
- Backup systems
    
- Long-duration effects
    

A high-level Wizard should not automatically waste unused Wish opportunities during extended downtime.

---

# Wish Doctrine

> **Wish is strongest when used boringly.**

The flashy part is:

**"I alter reality."**

The optimized part is:

**"I have every level 1–8 spell in the game available as an Action without its casting requirements."**

That is already extraordinary.

Treat reality rewriting as the emergency override.

# Magic Jar Optimization
<!-- wizard-upgrade:start -->
> [!warning] Revised host benefits and survival limits
> Under the 2024 Magic Jar, the host replaces only your HP, Hit Dice, STR, DEX, CON, Speed, and senses; you otherwise retain your statistics. Do not budget the host's natural armor, resistances, immunities, special actions, or innate spellcasting as extra RAW gains. Legacy-style host-trait suggestions below require a different source version or an explicit table exception.
>
> Remote body storage is a security-versus-survival tradeoff. When the spell ends, a dead original body or one more than 100 feet away means death. A killed host requires a Charisma save against your own spell DC and a container within 100 feet for the stated survival route. A distant headquarters or Demiplane is not an uncomplicated safety upgrade. [2024 Magic Jar](https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2619021-magic-jar).

> **Magic Jar is not merely a possession spell.**
>
> It allows a Wizard to temporarily operate through another Humanoid body while retaining key parts of their own character.
>
> Its optimization value comes from finding a body that improves your physical capabilities without sacrificing the Wizard abilities that actually matter.

> [!warning] Rules-Dense Spell
> Magic Jar has unusually complicated interactions with creature statistics, class features, special traits, death, range, and possession.
>
> Verify unusual host abilities individually before assuming they transfer.

---

# Core Concept

Magic Jar separates three things:

**Your soul**

**Your original body**

**The possessed Humanoid body**

The goal is to gain useful physical characteristics from the host while continuing to function as a Wizard.

This means host selection should not simply ask:

> **"Is this creature powerful?"**

Instead ask:

> **"What does this body improve compared with my normal body?"**

---

# Host Evaluation

When encountering a potential host, evaluate it in this order.

## Physical Ability Scores

The most important scores to inspect are:

### Constitution

Potentially valuable for:

- Durability
- Constitution saving throws
- Concentration
- Surviving high-damage effects

### Dexterity

Potentially valuable for:

- AC
- Initiative
- Dexterity saving throws
- Stealth
- Other Dexterity-based checks

### Strength

Usually lower priority for a normal Wizard, but useful for:

- Carrying capacity
- Grappling
- Escaping restraints
- Physical obstacles
- Melee-oriented builds

# Host Evaluation Checklist

| Feature | Priority | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **High CON** | Very High | Durability and potentially Concentration |
| **High DEX** | Very High | AC, Initiative, saves |
| **High HP / Hit Dice interaction** | Verify | Potential durability increase |
| **High STR** | Medium | Physical utility |
| **Natural Armor** | Does not transfer | Calculate AC using your retained rules and the host's transferred DEX; do not copy host AC |
| **Flight** | Exceptional | Concentration-free mobility if retained |
| **Climb / Swim Speed** | High | Exploration and positioning |
| **Blindsight** | Exceptional | Powerful sensory capability |
| **Darkvision** | Useful | General exploration |
| **Tremorsense** | High | Specialized detection |
| **Resistances** | Does not transfer | Retain your own, not the host's |
| **Immunities** | Does not transfer | Retain your own, not the host's |
| **Condition Immunities** | Does not transfer | Retain your own, not the host's |
| **Special Actions** | Does not transfer | Not in the 2024 transfer list |
| **Innate Spellcasting** | Does not transfer | Retain your own spellcasting |
| **Multiattack** | Does not transfer | Not in the 2024 transfer list |
| **Size** | Not a listed statistic transfer | Physical appearance may matter narratively; do not automatically copy mechanical size benefits |

Anything marked **Verify** should be checked against the exact Magic Jar wording and the specific creature's statblock.

# Ideal Host Profile

For a standard Wizard, an exceptional host would potentially provide:

**High CON**

+

**High DEX**

+

**High HP and Hit Dice**

+

**Useful movement**

+

**Useful senses**

without interfering with normal Wizard spellcasting.

The perfect host does not need extraordinary offensive actions.

Your Wizard spells already provide offense.

The host should primarily improve the weaknesses of the Wizard chassis.

---

# Host Roles

Different bodies can solve different problems.

## Defensive Host

Prioritize:

- CON
- DEX
- AC
- High HP and Hit Dice
- Your retained defenses, recalculated with the transferred physical scores

Purpose:

> Keep the Wizard alive and maintain Concentration.

---

## Mobility Host

Prioritize:

- Flight
- Climbing
- Swimming
- High Speed
- Special movement

Purpose:

> Gain mobility without spending Concentration or spell slots.

---

## Sensory Host

Prioritize:

- Blindsight
- Tremorsense
- Superior Darkvision
- Other unusual senses

Purpose:

> Gain information and detection capabilities difficult to reproduce permanently.

---

## Infiltration Host

Prioritize:

- Social access
- Appearance
- Position within an organization
- Physical capabilities
- Mobility

Magic Jar can potentially provide something ordinary transformation spells cannot:

> **Access to an existing identity.**

This can make the spell extremely powerful in political, social, espionage, and infiltration situations.

---

## Physical Utility Host

Prioritize:

- STR
- Carrying capacity
- Size (not a transferred statistic; do not assume the host's carrying-capacity multiplier or other size-based mechanical benefits)
- Movement
- Physical skills

Useful when the Wizard needs capabilities their normal body lacks.

# Host Selection Principle

Do not maintain a massive list of theoretically optimal Humanoids.

Instead:

> **Evaluate useful Humanoids when the campaign actually gives you access to them.**

A theoretically perfect statblock is irrelevant if the creature never appears.

Magic Jar rewards **opportunistic optimization**.

When an interesting Humanoid appears:

1. Inspect its physical statistics.
2. Identify unusual traits.
3. Determine what Magic Jar actually grants.
4. Compare it with your current body.
5. Evaluate the possession risk.
6. Decide whether the upgrade is worth pursuing.

---

# Possession Preparation

Do not cast Magic Jar casually.

**2024 operating limits:** the normal casting takes 1 minute and requires Concentration during setup, not throughout possession. A visible Humanoid must be within 100 feet for an attempt; Protection from Evil and Good or Magic Circle can prevent possession. A successful target save blocks another attempt against that target for **24 hours**, so containment does not grant unlimited immediate retries. When Magic Jar ends, its **500+ GP container is destroyed**. Budget that as an eventual loss even though the component is not consumed at the initial cast. [Magic Jar](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/phb-2024/spell-descriptions#MagicJar).

Before attempting possession, prepare:

- Your original body
- The container
- The possession location
- Allies
- Escape options
- Defensive magic
- Recovery options

The spell creates several failure states that can be catastrophic without preparation.

---

# Protect the Original Body

Your original body becomes one of your most valuable possessions.

Treat it accordingly.

Possible protection includes:

- [[Bastion]]
- [[Demiplane]]
- Locked and guarded rooms
- [[Long-Term Magical Defenses#Private Sanctum|Private Sanctum]]
- [[Glyph of Warding]]
- Trusted allies
- Bound guardians
- Physical barriers
- Magical alarms

The exact setup depends on what the spell allows while your soul is operating elsewhere.

## Principle

> **Never leave your original body somewhere an enemy can casually discover it.**

If the enemy understands what you are doing, your helpless original body becomes an obvious target.

---

# Protect the Container

The Magic Jar container is another critical vulnerability.

Treat it as mission-critical equipment.

Consider:

- Concealment
- Physical protection
- Trusted carriers
- Secure containers
- Defensive wards
- Redundant security
- Keeping enemies unaware of what the object actually is

Do not unnecessarily advertise which object contains the Magic Jar effect.

---

# Possession Location

A controlled environment dramatically improves safety.

Ideal conditions:

- Target contained
- Original body protected
- Container protected
- Allies present
- Escape routes available
- No unexpected hostile creatures
- Countermeasures prepared

Magic Jar becomes much safer when treated as a **procedure** rather than an improvised combat spell.

# Failure Planning

Before attempting possession, answer:

### What happens if the target succeeds?

Have a plan for:

- Containment
- Escape
- Repeated attempts where legal
- Hostile retaliation

### What happens if the host dies?

Know exactly what Magic Jar requires when the possessed body dies.

### What happens if the container is destroyed?

This is potentially catastrophic.

Know the exact consequences before using the spell.

### What happens if my original body is killed?

This should be treated as one of the highest-risk failure states.

### What happens if I am too far away?

Track the spell's relevant range restrictions carefully.

# Concentration Optimization

One of the most important questions for any host is:

> **Does this body improve my ability to maintain Concentration?**

Potential improvements may come from:

- Better Constitution
- Better AC
- Your own retained defensive features
- Mobility
- Improved positioning

A body that substantially improves Concentration reliability can be more valuable than one with impressive offensive abilities.

See [[Concentration Strategy]].

---

# Initiative Optimization

A host with improved Dexterity may improve Initiative depending on the exact statistics Magic Jar uses.

For a control Wizard, acting earlier can be worth more than gaining additional melee damage.

Earlier turns mean earlier:

- Web
- Hypnotic Pattern
- Sleet Storm
- Wall of Force
- Banishment
- Maze

See [[Initiative & First-Turn Strategy]].

---

# Spellcasting Priority

Do not choose a host primarily because it has strong weapon attacks.

You are still optimizing around being a **Wizard**.

The question is:

> **Does this body make my Wizard better?**

not:

> **Does this body make me a mediocre martial character?**

Your actions are usually better spent casting Wizard spells. The host's special actions and innate spellcasting are not added to your options by the revised spell.

---

# Identity & Infiltration

Magic Jar has significant non-combat potential.

Possessing an existing Humanoid can potentially provide access to:

- Restricted locations
- Organizations
- Political structures
- Enemy bases
- Social circles
- Secure facilities

However, possessing the body does not automatically provide all information required to convincingly impersonate the individual.

Potential problems include:

- Memories
- Mannerisms
- Relationships
- Passwords
- Knowledge
- Language
- Expected behavior

Combine possession with information gathering.

See [[Information Warfare]].

# Long-Term Operation

For extended possession, these dependencies determine whether the tactic is safe:

| Resource | Tactical requirement |
|---|---|
| **Original Body** | Must be alive and within 100 feet when the spell ends |
| **Magic Jar Container** | Within 100 feet for the host-death survival route; protect it from destruction |
| **Host** | Eligible visible Humanoid within possession range; only listed statistics transfer |
| **Host Identity** | Does not grant memories, passwords, or automatic social credibility |
| **Recovery** | Nearby protected body and allied response are more important than distant storage |

Long-term possession should be treated as infrastructure, not merely a spell effect.

# Quick Host Evaluation

When you encounter an interesting Humanoid:

### Step 1 — Physical Upgrade

**STR:** Better?  
**DEX:** Better?  
**CON:** Better?

### Step 2 — Defense

**AC:** Better?  
**Resistances:** Keep your own.  
**Immunities:** Keep your own.  
**Defensive traits:** Keep your own; host traits do not transfer.

### Step 3 — Mobility

**Speed:** Better?  
**Flight:** Available?  
**Swim:** Available?  
**Climb:** Available?

### Step 4 — Senses

**Darkvision:** Better?  
**Blindsight:** Available?  
**Tremorsense:** Available?  
**Other senses:** Useful?

### Step 5 — Special Features

What unusual abilities does the statblock contain?

**Do they actually transfer under Magic Jar?**

### Step 6 — Wizard Compatibility

Can you still:

- Cast normally?
- Use your equipment?
- Use your spellbook?
- Use material components?
- Perform Somatic components?
- Speak Verbal components?

### Step 7 — Risk

What happens if:

- Possession fails?
- Host dies?
- Container is destroyed?
- Original body is attacked?
- Dispel/antimagic becomes involved?

# Magic Jar Principle

> **The best Magic Jar host is not necessarily the strongest Humanoid.**
>
> It is the body that provides the largest useful physical and defensive upgrade while preserving everything that makes you a powerful Wizard.

Magic Jar converts:

**Access to a suitable Humanoid**

+

**Preparation**

+

**Security**

into:

**A potentially upgraded operating body for the Wizard.**

That makes it primarily an **Advanced Wizardry spell**, not merely a combat spell.

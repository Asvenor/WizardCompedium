## Saving Throw

A saving throw resists an effect imposed on a creature.

Common Wizard save targets:

| Save | Typical Targets |
|---|---|
| **STR** | Forced movement, physical restraint |
| **DEX** | Explosions, area damage, some restraints |
| **CON** | Endurance, poison, many necromancy effects |
| **INT** | Mental disruption and some illusions |
| **WIS** | Control, charm, fear, many shutdown spells |
| **CHA** | Banishment, planar or identity-based effects |

## Ability Check

An ability check represents an attempt to accomplish something.

Important Wizard examples:

- Arcana
- Investigation
- Athletics escape checks
- Maze escape checks
- Study actions against illusions

## Key Difference

**Legendary Resistance normally interacts with failed saving throws, not ordinary ability checks.**

This makes no-save effects and ability-check escape mechanics especially valuable against bosses.

## Advantage & Disadvantage

Advantage: roll two d20s and use the higher.

Disadvantage: roll two d20s and use the lower.

They normally do not stack into multiple extra dice.

If at least one source of each applies, **all Advantage and Disadvantage cancel**, regardless of how many sources you have.

The total must **meet or exceed** the DC to succeed. A natural 20 is not an automatic success on an ordinary ability check or save, and a natural 1 is not an automatic failure; attacks and Death Saving Throws have their own special rules. Initiative is a DEX ability check, not a saving throw.

**Expected success:** with a fixed bonus, count the d20 faces for which `roll + bonus ≥ DC`, then divide by 20. Advantage changes success probability `p` to `1 − (1 − p)²`; Disadvantage changes it to `p²`. This assumes no rerolls, replacements, automatic-result feature, or Legendary Resistance.

Source: [2024 PHB — D20 Tests](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/phb-2024/playing-the-game#D20Tests).

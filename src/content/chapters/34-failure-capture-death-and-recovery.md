Optimization is incomplete if it only describes winning positions. This chapter handles the moment the first plan fails: an ally drops, concentration ends, the Wizard is restrained or silenced, the spellbook disappears, the party is captured, or death becomes possible.

<!-- source:B00555 -->
<aside class="callout" data-source-locator="B00555.T001"><strong>FAILURE HIERARCHY</strong><p>Stop the irreversible loss first. Life, escape, the spellbook, and irreplaceable components outrank damage, pride, optional treasure, and completing the original objective.</p></aside>

<!-- source:B00556 -->
## Immediate crisis matrix

<!-- source:B00557 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00557.T001" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Failure state</th><th scope="col">First priority</th><th scope="col">Wizard response</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Melee ally at 0 HP</td><td>Prevent another hit or restore real Hit Points.</td><td>Break enemy access with a wall, control, teleport, forced movement, or healing item/spell access; temporary Hit Points do not wake a creature at 0 HP.</td></tr><tr><td>Wizard near 0 HP</td><td>Break line of sight and movement access.</td><td>Misty Step/Dimension Door, cover, Dodge, Resilient Sphere, control, or retreat before spending the action on damage.</td></tr><tr><td>Concentration lost</td><td>Reassess the board.</td><td>Do not automatically recast. Decide whether control, extraction, defense, or damage now wins the changed position.</td></tr><tr><td>Counterspell or reaction unavailable</td><td>Avoid the predictable trigger.</td><td>Use range, Total Cover, line-of-sight denial, spacing, or force the enemy to spend the spell on a lesser target.</td></tr><tr><td>Spellbook missing</td><td>Survive and preserve prepared capability.</td><td>Do not chase blindly. Secure current spells, identify the theft window, protect backups, and plan recovery with divination, contacts, or investigation.</td></tr><tr><td>Focus/components lost</td><td>Identify which spells remain castable.</td><td>Use spells whose components you can still provide, retrieve a backup focus/pouch, employ items or mundane actions, and withdraw if the loadout collapses.</td></tr><tr><td>Captured</td><td>Hide capability and learn the prison.</td><td>Do not reveal every prepared spell, item, contact, or escape trigger. Map guards, routines, doors, materials, anti-magic, and the safest release window.</td></tr><tr><td>Antimagic or spell suppression</td><td>Move out or solve the physical problem.</td><td>Use cover, doors, elevation, mundane gear, allies, movement, pre-existing non-suppressed resources, and the objective rather than waiting to cast.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

## Zero Hit Points and death-save rules

<!-- source:B00560 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00560.T001" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Rule</th><th scope="col">2024 baseline</th><th scope="col">Operational meaning</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Start turn at 0 HP</td><td>Make a Death Saving Throw.</td><td>A downed ally may be one enemy action away from death; do not assume three full rounds.</td></tr><tr><td>Success/failure</td><td>10+ succeeds; three successes make the creature Stable; three failures kill it.</td><td>Track both openly and reset them when Hit Points return or the creature becomes Stable.</td></tr><tr><td>Natural 1 / natural 20</td><td>A 1 causes two failures; a 20 restores 1 HP.</td><td>Any delay can swing sharply. Real healing is safer than gambling on the roll.</td></tr><tr><td>Damage at 0 HP</td><td>Damage causes one failure; a Critical Hit causes two; damage at least equal to maximum HP kills.</td><td>Block adjacent attackers and area damage before assuming stabilization is enough.</td></tr><tr><td>Stabilizing</td><td>Help action plus DC 10 Wisdom (Medicine); a Stable creature remains Unconscious at 0 HP.</td><td>Stability stops death saves but does not restore actions, movement, or concentration.</td></tr><tr><td>Temporary Hit Points</td><td>Do not restore consciousness at 0 HP.</td><td>Emergency plans need actual healing or successful stabilization/extraction.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

## Two-player rescue protocol

<!-- source:B00562 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00562.T001" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Situation</th><th scope="col">Wizard + one melee decision</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Ally drops beside one enemy</td><td>Remove the enemy’s access first if healing would only allow another immediate knockout; then restore HP or extract.</td></tr><tr><td>Ally drops inside a group</td><td>Use area control, Wall of Force geometry, Sleet Storm, forced movement, or Dimension Door. Walking into the group to administer aid may lose both characters.</td></tr><tr><td>Wizard drops</td><td>The melee partner should prioritize real healing, a potion, extraction, or stopping adjacent attacks. A Stable Wizard still cannot carry the encounter.</td></tr><tr><td>Both are nearly down</td><td>Abandon optional objectives and treasure. Break pursuit, close doors, create obscurity, teleport, or surrender when survival is the only remaining win condition.</td></tr><tr><td>No healing access</td><td>Carry accessible potions, healer’s kits if used by the table, and a clear extraction plan. Prevention and separation are the Wizard’s healing substitute.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

## Casting impairment and condition triage

<!-- source:B00564 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00564.T001" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Problem</th><th scope="col">What it actually threatens</th><th scope="col">Response priority</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Incapacitated / Unconscious</td><td>Actions, reactions, and concentration; Unconscious also drops held objects.</td><td>Prevent follow-up damage, restore HP/end the condition, recover dropped focus or item, then rebuild position.</td></tr><tr><td>Silence or inability to speak</td><td>Spells with Verbal components.</td><td>Leave the area, use legal non-Verbal options/items, break the source, or rely on allies and mundane actions.</td></tr><tr><td>Hands restrained or occupied</td><td>Somatic and Material component handling depends on the exact spell, focus, hands, and restraints.</td><td>Check the real component requirement; do not assume every spell is disabled or usable. Escape, switch tools, or cast a legal option.</td></tr><tr><td>Grappled</td><td>Speed becomes 0 and attacks against targets other than the grappler are disadvantaged; it does not by itself erase spellcasting.</td><td>Teleport, force movement, control the grappler, or use a spell that remains legal and useful.</td></tr><tr><td>Restrained</td><td>Speed 0, attacks against you gain Advantage, your attacks have Disadvantage, and Dexterity saves suffer.</td><td>Favor save-based or no-attack spells, teleportation, defense, and ending the source.</td></tr><tr><td>Exhaustion</td><td>Each level subtracts 2 from D20 Tests and 5 feet from Speed; level 6 kills.</td><td>Stop optional risks, secure rest and supplies, reduce repeated tests, and avoid pretending one Long Rest removes several levels.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

## Spellbook-loss protocol

<!-- source:B00567 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00567.T001" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Phase</th><th scope="col">Procedure</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Before loss</td><td>Keep the primary book dry and concealed; maintain a practical backup of essential spells; separate the book from obvious valuables; know which spells are currently prepared.</td></tr><tr><td>Discovery</td><td>Record the last confirmed location, who had access, missing components/items, witnesses, magical traces, and whether the theft was targeted.</td></tr><tr><td>Immediate play</td><td>Prepared spells remain the active operating package; conserve irreplaceable answers and avoid a reckless pursuit that also loses the Wizard.</td></tr><tr><td>Recovery</td><td>Use contacts, Arcana/Investigation, divination, familiar scouting, legal authorities, and enemy motives. Decide whether retrieval, negotiation, theft-back, or replacement is safest.</td></tr><tr><td>Reconstruction</td><td>Acquire a new book and rebuild core defense, control, rituals, mobility, rescue, and campaign answers first. Luxury and duplicate damage wait.</td></tr><tr><td>After action</td><td>Change storage, access, travel, disguise, backup, and trusted-contact procedures so the same attack cannot repeat.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

## Death and long-term recovery infrastructure

<!-- source:B00569 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00569.T001" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Tier</th><th scope="col">Recovery plan</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Low level</td><td>Know who can stabilize, who carries potions, where the nearest temple or healer is, and what payment or favor revival may require.</td></tr><tr><td>Mid level</td><td>Reserve transport for a body, protect required remains/components, maintain faction contacts, and avoid selling every diamond or restoration resource.</td></tr><tr><td>High level</td><td>Use Contingency, secure sanctums, Teleport/Demiplane logistics, Clone when available and permitted, protected archives, and instructions allies can execute without guessing.</td></tr><tr><td>After revival</td><td>Recover equipment and spellbook access, address exhaustion or lasting effects, repay obligations, update enemies and security, and do not immediately repeat the failed mission unchanged.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

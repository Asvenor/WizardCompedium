## Time-window priority guide

<!-- source:B00478 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00478.T001" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Time available</th><th scope="col">Highest-value Wizard actions</th><th scope="col">Avoid</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>A few minutes</td><td>Check immediate threats, inspect urgent loot, verify components, reposition the familiar, and ritual-cast only if time permits.</td><td>Beginning a long task or spending slots merely because a rest may happen soon.</td></tr><tr><td>Short Rest</td><td>Use eligible recovery, Identify or examine urgent loot, ritual-cast, reassess remaining slots, and decide whether continuing is safe.</td><td>Automatically spending Arcane Recovery or assuming the area is secure.</td></tr><tr><td>Long-Rest Camp</td><td>Secure the location, protect the spellbook, assign simple watch/scouting jobs, review the next objective, and adjust the next-day package.</td><td>Treating the rest as guaranteed or assuming crafting is automatically compatible.</td></tr><tr><td>Several hours in town</td><td>Buy components, locate scrolls/formulas, identify specialists, gather information, and choose realistic projects.</td><td>Shopping without knowing the next objective or starting work that cannot finish.</td></tr><tr><td>One full day</td><td>Copy priority spells, scribe affordable scrolls, research a known threat, or complete part of a common/uncommon project.</td><td>Copying narrow spells while core defense, control, ritual, or escape gaps remain.</td></tr><tr><td>Several days</td><td>Craft, copy, build contacts, search libraries, commission work, and acquire rare materials.</td><td>Solo downtime that consumes the whole table without advancing party goals.</td></tr><tr><td>One week or more</td><td>Major crafting, spell trading, secure facilities, travel networks, long-term defense, and high-level preparation.</td><td>Campaign-warping infrastructure without confirming the intended limits.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

## Rest priority order

<!-- source:B00480 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00480.T001" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Priority</th><th scope="col">Use the available time to...</th><th scope="col">Reason</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>1. Prevent the next failure</td><td>Recover critical resources, restore essential consumables, protect the spellbook, and confirm an escape option.</td><td>Survival and continuity beat luxury projects.</td></tr><tr><td>2. Convert information</td><td>Identify loot, study notes, question witnesses, scout, and research the next objective.</td><td>The correct prepared spell is stronger than a blind “best spell.”</td></tr><tr><td>3. Convert gold into future actions</td><td>Copy spells, scribe scrolls, buy components, craft useful consumables, or commission specialists.</td><td>Gold becomes additional spell access and party action economy.</td></tr><tr><td>4. Improve strategic position</td><td>Establish contacts, secure transport/lodging/workspace, obtain maps, and locate libraries or magical suppliers.</td><td>Infrastructure makes later preparation faster and more reliable.</td></tr><tr><td>5. Pursue luxury projects</td><td>Only after the next mission’s real gaps are covered.</td><td>Redundant or thematic work should not consume the resources needed for survival.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

## Camp optimization

<!-- source:B00482 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00482.T001" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Phase</th><th scope="col">Optimized procedure</th><th scope="col">Why</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Choose the site</td><td>Prefer concealment, cover, defensible approaches, and at least one escape route. Avoid obvious traps and enclosed dead ends.</td><td>The Wizard cannot control an ambush that begins with the party surrounded and asleep.</td></tr><tr><td>Secure the perimeter</td><td>Use mundane watch discipline first, then rituals or magic when the risk justifies them.</td><td>Low-cost security preserves slots and prevents overconfidence in one spell.</td></tr><tr><td>Assign the familiar</td><td>Give one simple job: perimeter look, route check, concealed watch point, or messenger duty.</td><td>Simple instructions preserve table speed and reduce misinterpretation.</td></tr><tr><td>Protect the spellbook</td><td>Keep it dry, concealed, difficult to steal, and separate from obvious valuables when practical.</td><td>Losing the only spellbook is a campaign-scale failure.</td></tr><tr><td>Review the next objective</td><td>Identify expected terrain, enemies, travel, social needs, components, and party weaknesses.</td><td>Tomorrow’s prepared package should solve tomorrow’s problem.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

<aside class="callout" data-source-locator="B00482.T002"><strong>CAMP RULE</strong><p>A familiar is an information tool, not a complete watch system. Do not assume it replaces party watches, understands complex contingencies, or can safely scout beyond the information chain.</p></aside>

## Short-Rest optimization

<!-- source:B00485 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00485.T001" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Step</th><th scope="col">Static procedure</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>1. Verify safety</td><td>Decide whether the location can realistically remain secure for the full rest. Retreat first if the party cannot protect the time.</td></tr><tr><td>2. Use recovery intelligently</td><td>Use Arcane Recovery or another eligible feature only after comparing remaining slots, likely encounters, and the value of saving it.</td></tr><tr><td>3. Process urgent information</td><td>Identify items, inspect captured spellbooks/notes, question allies, and determine whether the plan has changed.</td></tr><tr><td>4. Ritual-cast when valuable</td><td>Use rituals that improve the next segment without consuming the spell slots needed for combat.</td></tr><tr><td>5. Rebuild the combat plan</td><td>Confirm the main concentration spell, reaction priority, escape option, and low-resource fallback.</td></tr><tr><td>6. Decide continue or withdraw</td><td>Do not let sunk time force the party deeper when the remaining resources no longer support the objective.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

## Long-Rest optimization

<!-- source:B00487 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00487.T001" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Moment</th><th scope="col">Optimized action</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Before settling</td><td>Choose a secure site, protect the spellbook/components, inventory consumed resources, and decide what must be restored or replaced.</td></tr><tr><td>During the camp period</td><td>Use permitted rituals, watches, scouting, conversation, and planning. Do not assume crafting is compatible with the rest or environment.</td></tr><tr><td>After reviewing the day</td><td>Separate facts from guesses, note which spells overperformed or failed, and identify the next objective’s real requirements.</td></tr><tr><td>When preparation becomes available</td><td>Adjust the prepared package, keep one escape/rescue answer, choose the likely concentration plan, and confirm reaction competition.</td></tr><tr><td>Before departure</td><td>Check focus/hands, costly components, scrolls, familiar status, travel magic, and the party’s first-round plan.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00489.T001" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Tier</th><th scope="col">Do this first</th><th scope="col">Why</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>S</td><td>Find scrolls and spellbooks; buy missing costly components; identify magic; research the next objective; locate formulas and qualified specialists.</td><td>These expand spell access or prevent known campaign failures.</td></tr><tr><td>A</td><td>Copy high-priority spells; scribe broadly useful scrolls; craft resource-saving items; secure a workspace; establish magical contacts.</td><td>Turns downtime into permanent breadth and repeatable advantages.</td></tr><tr><td>B</td><td>Sell low-value loot; commission narrow items; replenish mundane supplies; research side objectives; create situational consumables.</td><td>Useful after the primary mission gaps are already covered.</td></tr><tr><td>LOW</td><td>Marginal damage upgrades, projects that finish too late, redundant effects, or expensive personal items that do not beat current attunement.</td><td>Consumes campaign time and gold without changing a meaningful decision.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

## Downtime conversion engine

<!-- source:B00491 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00491.T001" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Resource</th><th scope="col">Convert it into...</th><th scope="col">Payoff</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Gold</td><td>Scrolls, components, formulas, materials, specialist labor, transport, and secure facilities.</td><td>More spell access and fewer future bottlenecks.</td></tr><tr><td>Time</td><td>Spell copying, crafting, research, contacts, training, and infrastructure.</td><td>Permanent breadth instead of unused calendar days.</td></tr><tr><td>Information</td><td>Prepared spell changes, save targeting, route selection, component shopping, and encounter plans.</td><td>Wins before initiative without using hidden stat blocks.</td></tr><tr><td>Contacts</td><td>Libraries, scroll traders, artificers, alchemists, temples, hirelings, and rare-material suppliers.</td><td>Makes future projects possible and reduces search time.</td></tr><tr><td>Facilities</td><td>Safe laboratories, libraries, workshops, storage, wards, and transport hubs.</td><td>Enables larger projects and protects long-term investments.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

## Spellbook and scroll priorities during downtime

<!-- source:B00494 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00494.T001" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Action</th><th scope="col">Priority order</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Copy first</td><td>Missing core defense/control → important rituals → mobility/rescue → anti-caster answers → campaign-specific information → redundancy and niche utility.</td></tr><tr><td>Scribe first</td><td>Emergency defense/escape → frequently used utility → narrow spells worth carrying but not preparing → party-support spells a compatible user can exploit.</td></tr><tr><td>Buy first</td><td>Costly components that unlock already-known spells → high-value scrolls/formulas → rare materials for a confirmed project → ordinary supplies.</td></tr><tr><td>Delay</td><td>Theme-only spells, duplicate damage, and projects with no known use before the next major objective.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

## Research a known threat without metagaming

<!-- source:B00496 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00496.T001" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Step</th><th scope="col">Fair-information procedure</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>1. Gather observations</td><td>Record what the party has actually seen: movement, senses, attacks, magic, resistances, behavior, terrain, and objectives.</td></tr><tr><td>2. Separate fact from assumption</td><td>Do not turn genre expectations or player memory into character knowledge.</td></tr><tr><td>3. Use legitimate sources</td><td>Libraries, witnesses, captured notes, Arcana/History/Nature/Religion, divinations, experts, and previous encounters.</td></tr><tr><td>4. Prepare flexible answers</td><td>When information remains uncertain, favor terrain, no-save pressure, scouting, protection, and lower-cost tests.</td></tr><tr><td>5. Update after contact</td><td>Observe results and change the plan instead of repeating a failed assumption.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

## Rest and downtime traps

<!-- source:B00500 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00500.T001" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Trap</th><th scope="col">Correct rule</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>“Use every slot before resting.”</td><td>Do not manufacture risk to avoid theoretical waste. Unused resources are acceptable when the objective is already secure.</td></tr><tr><td>“Copy every spell I find.”</td><td>Copy real gaps first. Gold and time are strategic resources.</td></tr><tr><td>“Crafting always works during travel or rests.”</td><td>Only when the active rules, workspace, safety, and DM allow the activity.</td></tr><tr><td>“Start the strongest item.”</td><td>Start the strongest item that will actually finish and beat its opportunity cost.</td></tr><tr><td>“The familiar can handle watch alone.”</td><td>Use it as one layer of information, not the entire security plan.</td></tr><tr><td>“Yesterday’s prepared list is still fine.”</td><td>Review the objective, terrain, party plan, and known threats whenever preparation can change.</td></tr><tr><td>“Downtime is my solo minigame.”</td><td>Choose projects that advance shared goals and keep table time moving.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

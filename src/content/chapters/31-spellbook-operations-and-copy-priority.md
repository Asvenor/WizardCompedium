The spellbook is a strategic resource, not a worksheet. Use this procedure whenever a level-up, scroll, captured spellbook, shop, reward, or downtime opportunity adds possible spells.

<!-- source:B00504 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00504.T001" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Step</th><th scope="col">Static procedure</th><th scope="col">Optimization reason</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>1. Verify legality</td><td>Confirm the spell is a selectable Wizard spell and a level the character can access.</td><td>Prevents illegal copying or version mixing.</td></tr><tr><td>2. Use the active copying rule</td><td>Check current D&amp;D Beyond time, cost, source handling, and subclass benefits.</td><td>Copying details can differ by version or feature.</td></tr><tr><td>3. Fill missing functions first</td><td>Prioritize defense, control, ritual, mobility, information, rescue, and campaign-specific answers.</td><td>Gold and downtime should close real gaps.</td></tr><tr><td>4. Prefer unique rituals and utility</td><td>A spell that rarely needs preparation can still be an excellent spellbook addition.</td><td>The spellbook is broader than the prepared list.</td></tr><tr><td>5. Protect the primary book</td><td>Use concealment, secure storage, and available magical protection.</td><td>Losing the only book should hurt, not erase the character.</td></tr><tr><td>6. Maintain a backup when practical</td><td>Use a backup spellbook or equivalent campaign-supported method for essential spells.</td><td>Reduces catastrophic campaign risk.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

## Copy priority

<!-- source:B00506 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00506.T001" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Priority</th><th scope="col">Copy first</th><th scope="col">Examples of the need</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>S</td><td>Missing core defense/control, important rituals, and campaign-critical answers.</td><td>Shield/escape gaps, anti-caster need, information bottleneck, recurring environmental problem.</td></tr><tr><td>A</td><td>Mobility, scouting, non-concentration alternatives, rescue, and broad utility.</td><td>Dimension changes, infiltration, travel, party weakness, repeatable dungeon value.</td></tr><tr><td>B</td><td>Situational control, damage coverage, crafting, and redundant answers.</td><td>Useful when the main spell is resisted or the campaign rewards the niche.</td></tr><tr><td>C</td><td>Very narrow effects or spells already covered by a stronger option.</td><td>Copy only when cheap, thematic, or clearly relevant to the next objective.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

## Copying a Spell into Your Spellbook

<!-- source:B00508 -->
The 2024 and 2014 Wizard entries use the same normal transcription baseline, while Spell Scrolls add their own risk. Use the exact active D&D Beyond Wizard, spell, source object, and Spell Scroll entries whenever versions differ.

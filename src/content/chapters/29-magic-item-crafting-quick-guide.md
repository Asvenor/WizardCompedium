## Crafting requirements

<!-- source:B00457 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00457.T001" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Gate</th><th scope="col">Required answer</th><th scope="col">Optimization consequence</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>1. Arcana</td><td>For magic items other than Potions of Healing and Spell Scrolls, the crafter and every assistant have Arcana proficiency.</td><td>Arcana is mandatory for the general magic-item lane and independently qualifies a spellcaster for Spell Scroll scribing.</td></tr><tr><td>2. Tool</td><td>For general magic items, every crafter has the exact tool proficiency assigned to the item category.</td><td>The Spell Scroll and Potion of Healing rules are separate lanes with their own proficiency gates.</td></tr><tr><td>3. Item/campaign gate</td><td>Any formula, recipe, permission, instruction, special facility, or ingredient required by the exact item or campaign is available.</td><td>These are item- or campaign-specific gates, not universal requirements in the public 2024 crafting baseline.</td></tr><tr><td>4. Spell</td><td>Every spell cast by the finished general magic item is prepared on every crafting day; a scribed spell is prepared on every inscription day.</td><td>Spell-linked projects must be planned before each full workday begins.</td></tr><tr><td>5. Resources</td><td>Base item, gold, special materials, monster parts, and workspace are secured.</td><td>Material access can be the real bottleneck even when the party has enough gold.</td></tr><tr><td>6. Time</td><td>The party has enough complete 8-hour workdays before leaving the location or ending the campaign arc.</td><td>Partial hours do not count under the public baseline unless the DM explicitly creates a partial-progress rule.</td></tr><tr><td>7. User</td><td>The final user can activate, wield, or attune to the item.</td><td>Craft for the party’s action economy, not only for the Wizard’s inventory.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

## 2024 crafting baseline

<!-- source:B00459 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00459.T001" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Rarity</th><th scope="col">Working baseline</th><th scope="col">Cost baseline</th><th scope="col">Wizard verdict</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Common</td><td>5 days / 40 hours</td><td>50 gp</td><td>Excellent early projects; realistic when they solve a repeated problem.</td></tr><tr><td>Uncommon</td><td>10 days / 80 hours</td><td>200 gp</td><td>The main optimization tier: strong enough to matter and still attainable.</td></tr><tr><td>Rare</td><td>50 days / 400 hours</td><td>2,000 gp</td><td>A major project; use assistants and confirm the campaign can support the schedule.</td></tr><tr><td>Very Rare</td><td>125 days / 1,000 hours</td><td>20,000 gp</td><td>Long-term infrastructure only; compare against campaign length and access.</td></tr><tr><td>Legendary</td><td>250 days / 2,000 hours</td><td>100,000 gp</td><td>World-scale downtime and wealth; rarely a normal adventuring project.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

<aside class="callout" data-source-locator="B00459.T002"><strong>RULES BOUNDARY</strong><p>For other magic items, every crafting day is 8 hours; qualified assistants divide the required days, and normally only one assistant is allowed. Consumables other than Spell Scrolls halve the rarity-table time and cost. Potion of Healing is a separate Herbalism Kit rule: 1 day / 8 hours and 25 gp. Spell Scrolls use the separate table in <a href="/chapters/32-scroll-use-backup-books-and-security/">Chapter 32</a>. Material availability is DM-controlled; the public baseline gives 75% in a city, 25% in another settlement, and a 7-day wait before checking again after failure.</p></aside>

## Tool lanes at a glance

<!-- source:B00461 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00461.T001" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Project family</th><th scope="col">Typical tool lane</th><th scope="col">Wizard use</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Metal melee weapons and eligible medium/heavy armor</td><td>Smith’s Tools</td><td>Warning/initiative equipment, armored-Wizard defense, and party martial support.</td></tr><tr><td>Wands, staves, and wooden weapons</td><td>Woodcarver’s Tools</td><td>Rechargeable control, utility, damage, and spell-distribution projects.</td></tr><tr><td>Potions and oils</td><td>Assigned general-item tool; Potion of Healing separately uses Herbalism Kit</td><td>Healing, resistance, mobility, and pre-fight buffs that do not consume Wizard concentration.</td></tr><tr><td>Wondrous items</td><td>Usually Tinker’s Tools</td><td>Flight, storage, information, logistics, and campaign-shaping utility.</td></tr><tr><td>Spell scrolls</td><td>Arcana OR Calligrapher’s Supplies</td><td>Direct conversion of gold, prepared spell access, and full inscription days into extra Wizard spell uses.</td></tr><tr><td>Rings and jewelry</td><td>Jeweler’s Tools</td><td>High ceiling but usually expensive, slow, and pressured by attunement.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

<aside class="callout" data-source-locator="B00461.T002"><strong>ITEM-SPECIFIC RULE</strong><p>The exact item entry wins over the broad category. Never assume a wand is non-attunement, a familiar can activate it, or a wondrous item automatically belongs to Tinker’s Tools.</p></aside>

## Time economy and assistance

<!-- source:B00463 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00463.T001" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Question</th><th scope="col">Optimized handling</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Can work be split?</td><td>Count only complete 8-hour crafting or inscription days under the public baseline. Days need not be consecutive; partial hours count only if the DM explicitly adds that rule.</td></tr><tr><td>Can travel time be used?</td><td>Only when the character can complete a full 8-hour workday with the required workspace, tools, materials, spells, and safety; normal travel duties usually conflict.</td></tr><tr><td>Can work happen during a rest?</td><td>Not under the baseline. An 8-hour crafting day is work, while a Long Rest requires at least 6 hours of sleep and allows no more than 2 hours of light activity.</td></tr><tr><td>Can allies help?</td><td>Yes when every helper meets the lane’s proficiency and spell requirements. Divide the required days by the number of qualified crafters; normally only one assistant is allowed.</td></tr><tr><td>Can hirelings help?</td><td>Only qualified professionals count as assistants. Confirm Arcana, the assigned tool, any prepared-spell requirement, availability, loyalty, and supervision.</td></tr><tr><td>What usually delays the project?</td><td>Materials, prepared-spell access, qualified helpers, secure facilities, item-specific gates, and the party leaving town before enough full workdays are completed.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

## Static crafting procedure

<!-- source:B00465 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00465.T001" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Step</th><th scope="col">Procedure</th><th scope="col">Why it matters</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>1. Choose the problem</td><td>Define the need: spell-slot depth, initiative, flight, defense, healing, scouting, storage, or party support.</td><td>Prevents crafting a famous item that does not solve the campaign’s actual weakness.</td></tr><tr><td>2. Pick the project lane</td><td>Choose scroll, potion, wand, staff, armor, weapon, ring, or wondrous item.</td><td>Directs the correct proficiency lane, category tool, cost, time, spell, and user requirements.</td></tr><tr><td>3. Verify the exact entry</td><td>Check the exact item, rarity, Arcana/tool gate, prepared spells, gold, materials, complete workdays, assistants, activation, and attunement. Add a formula or special gate only when the item/campaign requires it.</td><td>Stops rules assumptions from consuming weeks of downtime.</td></tr><tr><td>4. Compare alternatives</td><td>Compare crafting against buying, copying a spell, using a scroll, commissioning an NPC, or solving the problem with an existing spell.</td><td>Crafting is good only when it beats the opportunity cost.</td></tr><tr><td>5. Check campaign timing</td><td>Confirm the item will finish before the next departure, level jump, or likely campaign ending.</td><td>A perfect item completed too late has zero practical value.</td></tr><tr><td>6. Commit and protect the project</td><td>Secure the workspace, materials, item-specific requirements, and unfinished item until completion.</td><td>Crafting creates valuable stationary targets for theft, sabotage, and interruption.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

## Craft or skip decision matrix

<!-- source:B00467 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00467.T001" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Situation</th><th scope="col">Verdict</th><th scope="col">Reason</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Legal Common item, useful every adventuring day</td><td>CRAFT</td><td>Reliable value and low risk of becoming obsolete.</td></tr><tr><td>Covers a major party weakness</td><td>CRAFT</td><td>Healing, mobility, initiative, information, or defense can outperform another personal damage upgrade.</td></tr><tr><td>Converts downtime into extra spell uses</td><td>USUALLY CRAFT</td><td>Scrolls and rechargeable utility preserve the Wizard’s prepared spells and slots.</td></tr><tr><td>Can be handed to an ally for extra actions</td><td>STRONG CANDIDATE</td><td>Party-wide action economy is often worth more than another item on the Wizard.</td></tr><tr><td>Requires attunement but cannot beat the current top three</td><td>SKIP</td><td>The real cost is the stronger attunement slot it displaces.</td></tr><tr><td>Duplicates an easy spell with no action-economy gain</td><td>USUALLY SKIP</td><td>The item spends gold and time without expanding the party’s options.</td></tr><tr><td>Finishes after the party leaves or the campaign likely ends</td><td>SKIP</td><td>Theoretical value never becomes table value.</td></tr><tr><td>Depends on a disputed exploit or permissive activation reading</td><td>CONFIRM FIRST</td><td>Do not spend campaign resources on an interaction the DM will reject.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

## Wizard project priority

<!-- source:B00469 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00469.T001" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Priority</th><th scope="col">Project lane</th><th scope="col">Examples and logic</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>S</td><td>Resource multiplication</td><td>Spell scrolls and reusable utility/control that preserve spell slots or move a situational spell out of the prepared list.</td></tr><tr><td>S</td><td>Initiative and mobility</td><td>Warning/initiative effects, flight, escape, and superior positioning. Acting first and staying unreachable win encounters.</td></tr><tr><td>A / S</td><td>Reusable spell platforms</td><td>Enspelled staves, focused wands, and similar rechargeable items when their exact activation and charge rules are favorable.</td></tr><tr><td>A</td><td>Party weakness coverage</td><td>Healing, resistance, freedom of movement, detection, storage, and logistics.</td></tr><tr><td>B</td><td>Martial support</td><td>Weapons, armor, and ally buffs when they improve the party more than another Wizard project.</td></tr><tr><td>C or lower</td><td>Marginal personal upgrades</td><td>Small damage increases, redundant effects, and expensive attunement projects that do not change a meaningful decision.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

## Enspelled Staff and rechargeable spell platforms

<!-- source:B00471 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00471.T001" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Platform</th><th scope="col">Best optimized use</th><th scope="col">Verify before crafting</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Enspelled Staff</td><td>Broad spell support and party resource distribution; usually the most Wizard-aligned enspelled platform.</td><td>Allowed spell schools/levels, prepared-spell requirement, charges, recharge, DC, attunement, and user restrictions.</td></tr><tr><td>Enspelled Weapon</td><td>Martial ally support or Bladesinger niche after core casting needs are already covered.</td><td>Weapon type, allowed spell schools, attunement, activation, and whether the chosen user can exploit the effect.</td></tr><tr><td>Enspelled Armor</td><td>Protective or support effects for armored allies and armored-Wizard shells.</td><td>Armor eligibility, spell restrictions, activation timing, charges, and attunement.</td></tr><tr><td>Wand</td><td>A focused rechargeable spell effect that can preserve slots or distribute repeatable control/damage.</td><td>Every wand’s specific attunement, user, charge, recharge, DC, and maximum-charge rules.</td></tr><tr><td>Spell Scroll</td><td>Flexible one-use access, emergency insurance, and storage for narrow spells.</td><td>Normal casting time/range, class-list restrictions, ability checks, and consumption rules.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

<aside class="callout" data-source-locator="B00471.T002"><strong>CHARGE WARNING</strong><p>Some enspelled items use charges and daily recharge, but the exact item entry controls. Never generalize one item family’s charge or school rules to every wand, staff, weapon, or armor.</p></aside>

## Crafting severity

<!-- source:B00475 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00475.T001" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Level</th><th scope="col">Examples</th><th scope="col">Table handling</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Standard optimized play</td><td>Scrolls, healing potions, ordinary utility items, one-off resistance preparation.</td><td>Use normally after confirming the rules and resources.</td></tr><tr><td>DM-sensitive optimization</td><td>Powerful rechargeable spell items, familiar/summon item use, unusual enspelled combinations, aggressive helper use.</td><td>Confirm the exact interpretation before spending time or gold.</td></tr><tr><td>Campaign-warping optimization</td><td>Mass-produced flight/storage, item-production economies, repeated loophole loops, or infrastructure that bypasses major campaign constraints.</td><td>Discuss the intended campaign limits first; do not present it as a surprise technicality.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

Gold is not only purchasing power. For a Wizard, it becomes spellbook breadth, costly components, scrolls, secure facilities, specialist labor, transport, and extra actions stored for later. This chapter explains how to earn wealth without assuming every campaign provides unlimited dungeon treasure or allowing Fabricate to become the only economic plan.

<!-- source:B00533 -->
<aside class="callout" data-source-locator="B00533.T001"><strong>ECONOMY RULE</strong><p>Use the campaign economy the DM actually runs. Official service prices are market anchors, not a guarantee that every settlement has buyers, that a player character can charge the same amount, or that a client accepts unrestricted magical risk.</p></aside>

<!-- source:B00534 -->
## Income lanes

<!-- source:B00535 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00535.T001" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Lane</th><th scope="col">What the Wizard sells</th><th scope="col">Best payoff</th><th scope="col">Main risk</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Adventuring</td><td>Bounties, recovered research, monster removal, escort work, ruin clearance, rare-component retrieval.</td><td>Gold plus spells, components, contacts, and information from the same mission.</td><td>The party accepts danger before confirming reward, ownership, or salvage rights.</td></tr><tr><td>Information</td><td>Arcane investigation, translation, identification, research, divination, locating people or objects, magical forensics.</td><td>Low material cost and little market saturation.</td><td>Bad information, privacy disputes, or promises beyond what the spell can establish.</td></tr><tr><td>Professional</td><td>Security audits, counter-magic, ward design, teaching, magical consultation, ritual support.</td><td>Repeat clients and reputation rather than one sale.</td><td>Liability when a ward fails or a client uses advice illegally.</td></tr><tr><td>Production</td><td>Scrolls, crafted items, commissioned objects, Fabricate-assisted manufacture, specialist tool work.</td><td>Converts time, proficiency, formulas, and materials into saleable output.</td><td>Finite buyers, guild hostility, raw-material shortages, and crafting downtime.</td></tr><tr><td>Infrastructure</td><td>Communication, transport, construction support, emergency extraction, secure storage, teleportation networks.</td><td>High-value contracts with institutions and factions.</td><td>Political control, regulation, sabotage, and the cost of maintaining access.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

## Official service-price anchors

<!-- source:B00537 -->
The 2024 equipment rules provide purchasing prices for ordinary hirelings and spellcasting services. Treat them as a baseline for what magic costs in the setting, then adjust for availability, components, danger, urgency, legality, and negotiation.

<!-- source:B00538 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00538.T001" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Market anchor</th><th scope="col">Listed cost</th><th scope="col">Wizard use</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Skilled hireling</td><td>2 gp per day</td><td>Compare the value of your own downtime against paying a qualified assistant, guard, researcher, or craftsperson.</td></tr><tr><td>Untrained hireling</td><td>2 sp per day</td><td>Use for hauling, cleaning, basic camp labor, messages, and supervised mundane work.</td></tr><tr><td>Messenger</td><td>2 cp per mile</td><td>A cheap alternative when Sending, teleportation, secrecy, or speed is unnecessary.</td></tr><tr><td>Cantrip service</td><td>30 gp</td><td>A market reference for simple magical work; availability begins in villages.</td></tr><tr><td>Level 1 spell service</td><td>50 gp</td><td>A low-level magical service should still account for risk, travel, and components.</td></tr><tr><td>Level 2 spell service</td><td>200 gp</td><td>Useful benchmark for specialized utility or investigation.</td></tr><tr><td>Level 3 spell service</td><td>300 gp</td><td>Normally requires at least a town; mission urgency can raise the price.</td></tr><tr><td>Level 4-5 spell service</td><td>2,000 gp</td><td>Premium magic with limited providers and major opportunity cost.</td></tr><tr><td>Level 6-8 spell service</td><td>20,000 gp</td><td>City-level strategic magic, usually negotiated through factions or institutions.</td></tr><tr><td>Level 9 spell service</td><td>100,000 gp</td><td>A world-level transaction, not routine shop inventory.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

<!-- source:B00539 -->
<aside class="callout callout--note"><p>Expensive material components are added to listed spellcasting-service costs. When selling your own service, require the client to provide or prepay consumed components before casting.</p></aside>

## Income by character tier

<!-- source:B00541 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00541.T001" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Tier</th><th scope="col">Reliable income opportunities</th><th scope="col">Avoid</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Levels 1-4</td><td>Arcana tutoring, translation, magical investigation assistance, identification, message delivery, local research, tool work, minor bounties.</td><td>Advertising yourself as able to solve curses or threats beyond your actual spell access.</td></tr><tr><td>Levels 5-10</td><td>Sending and communication, security inspection, scouting, counter-magic support, commissioned scrolls/items, construction assistance, serious adventuring contracts.</td><td>Selling every slot and entering the next mission without combat resources.</td></tr><tr><td>Levels 11-16</td><td>Long-range intelligence, secure transport, major wards, planar consultation, noble or military contracts, emergency extraction, rare research.</td><td>Accepting political exclusivity without understanding the enemies it creates.</td></tr><tr><td>Levels 17-20</td><td>Strategic teleportation, planar access, national defense, major divination, fortress design, Demiplane storage, industrial Fabricate contracts.</td><td>Treating world-level magic as an anonymous cash transaction with no campaign consequences.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

## Pricing and contract procedure

<!-- source:B00543 -->
<aside class="callout" data-source-locator="B00543.T001"><strong>MINIMUM FEE</strong><p>Consumed components + project materials + paid assistance + travel and security + downtime value + danger or secrecy premium + lost adventuring opportunity. A one-action casting can still represent a rare daily resource and years of training.</p></aside>

<!-- source:B00544 -->

<!-- source:B00545 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00545.T001" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Step</th><th scope="col">Contract decision</th><th scope="col">Protection</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>1. Define the deliverable</td><td>State the exact result: inspect one vault, transport named passengers, identify specified objects, or defend a location for a fixed period.</td><td>Prevents an unlimited promise to “solve the magical problem.”</td></tr><tr><td>2. Verify authority</td><td>Confirm who owns the object, property, body, information, or destination.</td><td>Avoids becoming the tool in theft, fraud, kidnapping, or political sabotage.</td></tr><tr><td>3. Price the complete cost</td><td>Include components, travel, preparation, assistants, risk, lost slots, and time.</td><td>Prevents apparent profit that actually consumes more resources than it earns.</td></tr><tr><td>4. Take an advance</td><td>Collect all consumed materials and a meaningful deposit before beginning.</td><td>Stops the Wizard from financing the client’s project.</td></tr><tr><td>5. Define failure conditions</td><td>Record what happens if a target resists, information is unavailable, access is blocked, or the client changes the objective.</td><td>Separates a failed spell from a broken contract.</td></tr><tr><td>6. Protect secrets</td><td>Do not expose the primary spellbook, full ritual procedure, safe-house locations, or every defensive contingency.</td><td>Income should not create an easy blueprint for robbing the Wizard.</td></tr><tr><td>7. Record reputation</td><td>Keep evidence of successful work, reliable witnesses, and trusted contacts.</td><td>Future access and referrals become more valuable than one payment.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

## Service selection and legal pressure

<!-- source:B00547 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00547.T001" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Service</th><th scope="col">Why clients pay</th><th scope="col">Boundary to settle</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Arcane investigation</td><td>The Wizard can combine Intelligence skills, rituals, divination, and magical detection.</td><td>What the evidence proves versus what it merely suggests.</td></tr><tr><td>Security and wards</td><td>Merchants, temples, nobles, and laboratories pay to reduce theft, intrusion, and hostile magic.</td><td>Duration, maintenance, authorized users, emergency access, and liability.</td></tr><tr><td>Communication</td><td>Fast reliable information can be worth more than damage magic.</td><td>Identity verification, message wording, confidentiality, and failed delivery.</td></tr><tr><td>Transport and extraction</td><td>Speed bypasses distance, pursuit, borders, and ordinary logistics.</td><td>Passenger consent, destination knowledge, cargo, legal jurisdiction, and return plan.</td></tr><tr><td>Crafting and production</td><td>The Wizard provides scarce formulas, tools, magical labor, or Fabricate productivity.</td><td>Who supplies materials, owns the formula, controls distribution, and bears failure cost.</td></tr><tr><td>Counter-magic</td><td>Dispel, containment, inspection, and expert advice solve threats ordinary guards cannot.</td><td>Unknown spell level, retaliation, collateral damage, and whether the effect should be preserved as evidence.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

## Loot conversion and business discipline

<!-- source:B00550 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00550.T001" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Asset</th><th scope="col">Keep when...</th><th scope="col">Sell or trade when...</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Spellbook or scroll</td><td>It fills defense, control, ritual, mobility, rescue, information, or campaign-specific gaps.</td><td>The spell is illegal for you to copy, already redundant, or another caster offers a better exchange.</td></tr><tr><td>Costly component</td><td>It unlocks an already-known high-value spell or party revival/restoration need.</td><td>The relevant spell is unavailable and the sale funds a more immediate survival gap.</td></tr><tr><td>Magic item</td><td>It beats current attunement, protects concentration, expands actions, or patches the party.</td><td>It is redundant, user-restricted, politically dangerous, or worth more as project capital.</td></tr><tr><td>Formula or rare material</td><td>A confirmed project will finish and materially improve the campaign.</td><td>The project is theoretical, too slow, or inferior to buying/copying a direct solution.</td></tr><tr><td>Mundane valuables</td><td>They have evidence, faction, ritual, or future bargaining value.</td><td>They are only weight and can be converted safely without flooding a tiny market.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

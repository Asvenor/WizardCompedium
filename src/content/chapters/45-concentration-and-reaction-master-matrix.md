## Concentration replacement test

<!-- source:B00743 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00743.T001" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Question</th><th scope="col">Keep the current spell when...</th><th scope="col">Replace it when...</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Is it still removing meaningful enemy actions?</td><td>The affected enemies remain unable to contribute efficiently.</td><td>The enemy has escaped, adapted, or become irrelevant to the objective.</td></tr><tr><td>Is concentration under real pressure?</td><td>Cover, distance, defenses and positioning keep the risk acceptable.</td><td>The spell is likely to end before creating another valuable turn.</td></tr><tr><td>Does the party need another function more?</td><td>Control already supplies the mobility, defense or isolation required.</td><td>Rescue, flight, protection or a different form of control now decides the objective.</td></tr><tr><td>Can non-concentration actions finish the job?</td><td>Cantrips, movement, Dodge, items and allies can exploit the advantage.</td><td>Only a different concentration spell can answer the new threat.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

## Match the concentration spell to the encounter

Keep the established matrix above. These research-informed choices add the missing alternatives without turning an optimizer's ranking into a universal order. Ask what the spell prevents, how long you can sustain it, and what the party loses by choosing it.

<tr><td>One early dangerous target; allies can leave it alone</td><td><a href="/spells/tashas-hideous-laughter/">Tasha's Hideous Laughter</a></td><td>Repeated saves and damage-triggered saves make focus-firing the same target a poor plan.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Compact grounded group in a chokepoint</td><td>Web</td><td>Fire, easy exits or party melee geometry can erase the advantage. Allied forced movement helps; Repelling Blast is an ally option, not an assumed Wizard cantrip.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Several charm-susceptible enemies</td><td>Hypnotic Pattern</td><td>Charm immunity, spread formations and allies waking victims reduce its value.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Enemy casters or ranged attackers depend on sight</td><td>Sleet Storm</td><td>Do not assume allies can see and attack through the obscurement. Preserve a useful position outside the storm.</td></tr>
<tr><td>One weak Intelligence save; believable misdirection matters</td><td><a href="/spells/phantasmal-force/">Phantasmal Force</a></td><td>It does not grant arbitrary conditions or turn an invented illusion into a physical restraint.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Low-level burst control is a poor fit, but another combatant helps</td><td><a href="/spells/summon-fey/">Summon Fey</a></td><td>Component availability, summon survival and action contribution must justify giving up area control.</td></tr>

<tr><td>A suitable hazard can be enclosed by another caster</td><td><a href="/spells/sickening-radiance/">Sickening Radiance</a> [X]</td><td>Requires a legal second concentration holder, initiative coordination and favorable escape geometry; see Chapter 22.</td></tr>

<tr><td>Split a fight without an initial saving throw</td><td>Wall of Force</td><td>Teleportation, geometry and the party's need to reach the enclosed targets can change the answer.</td></tr>

**Protection order:** land an appropriate control spell, then protect the caster with distance and cover. Constitution-save proficiency and War Caster help but do not make a save automatic. A long ritual, a readied spell, or a new concentration spell ends the current concentration; fleeing into Rope Trick also blocks spell effects across its portal. An ally holding Slow or another concentration spell is a separate party contribution, not a free rider on your Web.

The supplied videos did not establish a single best sixth-level concentration pick. Do not manufacture that ranking: existing Wall of Force may remain the right concentration while Mass Suggestion or Contingency performs a different, non-concentration job. At ninth level, Shapechange and True Polymorph are encounter-dependent alternatives, not effects one Wizard sustains together.

## Familiar role selection

<!-- source:B00389 -->
Choose the permitted form from the current Find Familiar entry. Compare movement, senses, size, environment, and how suspicious the creature looks in the scene; do not choose a form only because an online guide calls it the universal best.

<!-- source:B00390 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00390.T001" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Job</th><th scope="col">Useful traits</th><th scope="col">Caution</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Open-terrain scout</td><td>Safe movement, good perception, useful elevation, and a clear return route.</td><td>An obvious aerial scout can be noticed, attacked, or followed.</td></tr><tr><td>Dungeon / indoor scout</td><td>Small profile, climbing or tight-space movement, suitable senses, and quiet behavior.</td><td>Closed doors, magical darkness, traps, and distance can break the information chain.</td></tr><tr><td>Social surveillance</td><td>A form that naturally belongs in the location and can remain still without drawing attention.</td><td>Strange behavior can expose the familiar even when the animal itself looks ordinary.</td></tr><tr><td>Touch-spell delivery</td><td>Speed, cover, approach route, and an escape path after delivery.</td><td>Risk it only when the spell is worth the familiar and action cost.</td></tr><tr><td>Watch and perimeter</td><td>Useful senses, a concealed position, and a simple alarm instruction.</td><td>Do not assume the familiar replaces party watch rules or understands complex plans.</td></tr><tr><td>Sacrificial information</td><td>A form suited to one high-value look behind a door, around a corner, or into a dangerous area.</td><td>Dying for decisive information can be correct; dying for minor advantage is not.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

## Illusion ruling principles

<!-- source:B00402 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00402.T001" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Question the table must settle</th><th scope="col">Why it matters</th><th scope="col">Conservative operating principle</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>What action or check examines an illusion?</td><td>Determines how much enemy action economy the spell can consume.</td><td>Use the exact spell text; do not assume free disbelief or automatic Investigation.</td></tr><tr><td>What counts as physical interaction?</td><td>Defines when touch, projectiles, movement, or environmental contact reveal the effect.</td><td>Treat direct contradictory interaction as stronger evidence than suspicion alone.</td></tr><tr><td>Who learns the truth?</td><td>Prevents automatic hive-mind knowledge.</td><td>A creature can communicate what it learned, but others still act on the information available to them.</td></tr><tr><td>Does the image block sight before discovery?</td><td>Separates visual concealment from physical cover.</td><td>Visual obstruction can affect sight without creating physical protection unless the effect says otherwise.</td></tr><tr><td>How are sound, movement, smell, and heat handled?</td><td>Sets the sensory boundaries.</td><td>Only grant sensory elements the spell or feature actually creates.</td></tr><tr><td>How do repeated tricks affect enemies?</td><td>Allows intelligent adaptation without omniscience.</td><td>Enemies may become cautious after evidence, not before they have a reason.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

## Counterspell decision flow

<!-- source:B00404 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00404.T001" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Decision</th><th scope="col">Ask</th><th scope="col">Default response</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>1. Perception and legality</td><td>Can you perceive the casting, use a Reaction, and meet the exact range/target requirements?</td><td>If not, use positioning, cover, or the next available response.</td></tr><tr><td>2. Consequence</td><td>Would the spell kill someone, remove an ally, break the party plan, enable escape, or swing the encounter?</td><td>Counter only meaningful spells; preserve the Reaction against low-value casting.</td></tr><tr><td>3. Reaction competition</td><td>Could Shield, Absorb Elements, Song of Defense, Chronal Shift, or another Reaction be more important before your next turn?</td><td>Spend the Reaction on the larger expected loss.</td></tr><tr><td>4. Alternative answer</td><td>Can line-of-sight denial, movement, concentration damage, control, or Dispel Magic solve the problem more efficiently?</td><td>Use the cheaper or more reliable answer when the threat is not immediate.</td></tr><tr><td>5. Resolution</td><td>Can the caster make the Constitution save against your spell save DC, and was the spell cast with a slot?</td><td>On a failed save, the spell and its casting action are lost, but the target's spell slot is not expended; your Counterspell slot is expended.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

### Dispel Magic decision flow

<!-- source:B00406 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00406.T001" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Decision</th><th scope="col">Ask</th><th scope="col">Default response</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>1. Valid effect</td><td>Is there an ongoing magical effect, creature, or object that the active spell can legally target?</td><td>Confirm the exact target before spending the action.</td></tr><tr><td>2. Urgency</td><td>Does removing it now prevent more lost actions, damage, control, escape, or failure?</td><td>If not urgent, keep the slot and solve the encounter first.</td></tr><tr><td>3. Concentration shortcut</td><td>Is the effect maintained by a caster whose concentration can be broken?</td><td>Breaking concentration may be cheaper than spending Dispel Magic.</td></tr><tr><td>4. Multiple effects</td><td>Does the target carry more than one magical effect, and how does the active wording resolve them?</td><td>Do not assume one casting removes everything.</td></tr><tr><td>5. Resolution</td><td>What slot/check does the current rule require?</td><td>Resolve once, record what ended, and leave unaffected effects in play.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

Use this scorecard only when two legal spells are close. The six value factors ask how often a spell matters, how reliably it works, how many actions it influences, what it returns for the slot and setup, whether it adds unique coverage, and whether it completes the prepared portfolio. The amber rows subtract conflicts and operating burden. A spell that uniquely solves the real mission still wins, even when its generic score is lower.

<!-- source:B00151 -->
<aside class="callout" data-source-locator="B00151"><strong>30-SECOND METHOD — DECISION AID, NOT A GAME RULE</strong><p>1) Confirm D&amp;D Beyond selectability and legal Wizard access. 2) Score each blue value row from 0 to 2 and add them for 0–12. 3) Score each amber friction row as 0, −1, or −2. 4) Apply the mission modifier only after the base score. 5) Read the final band separately for acquisition and today’s preparation. No writing is required—when comparing spells mentally, focus first on the rows where their scores differ.</p></aside>

## Scoring rubric — what the numbers mean

<!-- source:B00153 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00153" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Value factor · add</th><th scope="col">0 · low</th><th scope="col">1 · useful</th><th scope="col">2 · strong</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Expected frequency</td><td>Rare or speculative</td><td>Several plausible uses</td><td>Recurring in combat and/or noncombat</td></tr><tr><td>Reliability</td><td>Commonly negated with no floor</td><td>Save/attack/condition with acceptable odds</td><td>No-save, strong partial, repeated attempt, or dependable floor</td></tr><tr><td>Action leverage</td><td>Minor damage/flavor</td><td>Meaningful pressure, defense, or rescue</td><td>Removes, redirects, protects, or creates several important actions</td></tr><tr><td>Slot efficiency</td><td>Premium slot/setup for a modest result</td><td>Fair exchange</td><td>Ritual, long duration, scalable targets, or encounter-defining return</td></tr><tr><td>Unique coverage</td><td>A better owned spell already fills the job</td><td>Useful alternate defense, geometry, or role</td><td>Only practical answer to a recurring failure condition</td></tr><tr><td>Portfolio fit</td><td>Creates another gap or duplicates the prepared package</td><td>Fits without weakening a core role</td><td>Closes a missing defense, control, rescue, information, or mission role</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

<aside class="callout" data-source-locator="B00157"><strong>COMPLETE FORMULA</strong><p>Base score = six value factors (0–12) minus two friction penalties (0–4), for a final range of −4 to 12. Then apply a mission modifier: +2 known direct need; +1 plausible need; 0 neutral; −1 redundant today; −2 blocked or unusable today. D&amp;D Beyond selectability and legal access are gates, not points. The mission-adjusted total ranges from −6 to 14.</p></aside>

## Prepared-spell portfolio coverage matrix

<!-- source:B00163 -->
<div class="table-wrap" data-source-locator="B00163" tabindex="0"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Function</th><th scope="col">Recommended coverage target</th><th scope="col">Typical answer families</th><th scope="col">Failure if omitted</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Defense</td><td>One immediate defense</td><td>Shield, Absorb Elements, Mirror Image, cover/mobility</td><td>A normal attack or area effect removes the Wizard or concentration.</td></tr><tr><td>Mobility / rescue</td><td>One self-extraction; party rescue when possible</td><td>Misty Step, Dimension Door, Fly, Polymorph, Resilient Sphere</td><td>Control succeeds but the party cannot reach, leave, or save the objective.</td></tr><tr><td>Primary concentration</td><td>One default plus one different-save/role alternative</td><td>Web, Sleet Storm, Slow, Banishment, Wall of Force, Telekinesis</td><td>The first immunity, geometry problem, or lost concentration collapses the plan.</td></tr><tr><td>Non-concentration follow-up</td><td>One efficient action after control lands</td><td>Cantrip, Dodge, Fireball/Synaptic Static, forced movement, item use</td><td>The Wizard breaks winning concentration merely to do something visible.</td></tr><tr><td>Antimagic</td><td>Prepare when enemy casting is plausible</td><td>Counterspell, Dispel Magic, sight denial, separation, Globe</td><td>One hostile spell reverses a winning encounter or blocks the objective.</td></tr><tr><td>Information / ritual</td><td>Spellbook access; prepare only when required</td><td>Find Familiar, Detect Magic, Identify, divinations, languages</td><td>Slots and encounters are spent solving questions that planning could answer cheaply.</td></tr><tr><td>Special campaign answer</td><td>At least one answer to the known pressure</td><td>Curse removal access, environment, social/infiltration, planar travel, crafting</td><td>The spell list is powerful in generic combat but helpless against the actual adventure.</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

<aside class="callout" data-source-locator="B00165"><strong>PREPARE, OWN, OR SCROLL?</strong><p>Prepare effects that must be available immediately. Keep rituals, setup spells, downtime tools, and narrow environment answers in the spellbook when preparation is unnecessary. Carry scrolls for low-frequency emergencies only when access, cost, and the active scroll rules justify them. A high score does not mean every spell belongs in today’s prepared list.</p></aside>

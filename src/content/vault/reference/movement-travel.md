## Tactical Movement

## Movement Types

Important movement concepts:

- Difficult Terrain
- Climbing
- Swimming
- Flying
- Crawling
- Jumping
- Falling
- Forced Movement
- Teleportation
- Squeezing
- Mounts

## Difficult Terrain

Difficult Terrain costs **1 extra foot of movement for every foot moved** through it.

Multiple sources of Difficult Terrain do not normally multiply this cost unless a specific rule says otherwise.

## Movement costs at the table

| Movement | Cost or restriction |
|---|---|
| Climb or swim without the matching special Speed | 2 feet of movement per foot traveled; 3 feet per foot in Difficult Terrain. The appropriate Climb/Swim Speed removes the extra cost for that mode. |
| Crawl | 2 feet per foot; 3 in Difficult Terrain. |
| Stand from Prone | Spend half your Speed, rounded down; impossible at Speed 0. |
| Jump | Each foot costs movement; a jump is not free distance. See [Common DCs & Rules](/library/reference/common-dcs-rules/). |
| Fly | Falling occurs when Incapacitated, Prone, or Fly Speed becomes 0, unless the creature can hover or a specific effect says otherwise. |
| Move through creatures | Depends on allegiance, Incapacitated, and relative size; see [Creature Sizes](/library/reference/creature-sizes/). |

You can split movement around your Action and Bonus Action. Changing movement modes does not grant a fresh full movement budget for each mode. A visible enemy leaving your reach can provoke; teleporting or movement that spends none of the target's movement, Action, Bonus Action, or Reaction does not. See [Timing reminders](/library/reference/action-economy/#timing-reminders).

Source: [2024 PHB movement glossary](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/phb-2024/rules-glossary#Climbing).

## Forced Movement

Forced movement is especially valuable with:

- Web
- Wall of Fire
- Sickening Radiance
- Cliffs
- Environmental hazards
- Chokepoints
- Other persistent area effects

Always read the exact trigger of an effect.

Moving a creature **into** an area, moving an area **onto** a creature, entering an area voluntarily, and starting a turn there are not automatically the same trigger.

## Teleportation

Teleportation is not normal movement.

It can bypass:

- Difficult Terrain
- Creatures blocking movement
- Many physical obstacles
- Opportunity-attack positioning
- Dangerous terrain between the start and destination

The specific teleportation effect determines destination requirements and restrictions.

## Overland Travel

## Travel Pace

A group traveling outside combat chooses a **Fast, Normal, or Slow** pace.

| Pace | Per Minute | Per Hour | Per Day | Effect |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| **Fast** | 400 ft. | 4 miles | 30 miles | Disadvantage on Wisdom (Perception or Survival) and Dexterity (Stealth) checks |
| **Normal** | 300 ft. | 3 miles | 24 miles | Disadvantage on Dexterity (Stealth) checks |
| **Slow** | 200 ft. | 2 miles | 18 miles | Advantage on Wisdom (Perception or Survival) checks |

A normal travel day assumes **8 hours of travel**.

### Choosing a Pace

**Fast**
- Best when time matters more than caution.
- Worse for noticing danger, navigation/survival tasks, and stealth.

**Normal**
- Standard travel speed.
- Still imposes Disadvantage on Dexterity (Stealth) checks.

**Slow**
- Best for careful exploration.
- Improves Perception and Survival checks.
- Costs significant travel distance.

---

## Terrain

Terrain can limit the maximum pace a group can use.

| Terrain | Maximum Pace |
|---|---|
| Arctic | Fast* |
| Coastal | Normal |
| Desert | Normal |
| Forest | Normal |
| Grassland | Fast |
| Hill | Normal |
| Mountain | Slow |
| Swamp | Slow |
| Underdark | Normal |
| Urban | Normal |
| Waterborne | Special |

*Appropriate equipment such as skis is required to maintain Fast pace in Arctic terrain.

## Good Roads

A good road increases the group's maximum pace by **one step**:

**Slow → Normal → Fast**

## Slower Travelers

If any member of the group has their Speed reduced to **half or less of normal**, the group must travel at a **Slow pace**.

## Ignoring Terrain

Flying or special movement that allows the group to ignore Difficult Terrain can allow **Fast pace regardless of terrain**.

---

## Extended Travel

Characters can travel for more than **8 hours per day**, but doing so risks Exhaustion.

At the end of each additional hour:

**Constitution Save = DC 10 + 1 for each hour past 8**

| Hour of Travel | CON Save |
|---:|---:|
| 9th | DC 11 |
| 10th | DC 12 |
| 11th | DC 13 |
| 12th | DC 14 |
| 13th | DC 15 |

On a failure:

**Gain 1 Exhaustion level.**

Each traveler makes their own saving throw.

## Mounts

Mounts can temporarily increase overland travel speed.

When riding horses or other mounts, the group can travel **twice the normal distance for 1 hour**.

After doing so, the mounts require a **Short or Long Rest** before they can travel at that increased rate again.

## Mounted Burst Travel

| Pace | Normal Distance / Hour | Mounted Burst / Hour |
|---|---:|---:|
| Fast | 4 miles | 8 miles |
| Normal | 3 miles | 6 miles |
| Slow | 2 miles | 4 miles |

This increased rate lasts for **1 hour**, not the entire travel day.

A mount's combat Speed does not automatically determine its normal overland travel distance.

## Common Mount Carrying Capacity

## Wagons & Land Vehicles

Travelers using:

- Wagons
- Carriages
- Carts
- Chariots
- Other ordinary land vehicles

use the normal travel rules and choose a travel pace as appropriate.

A wagon does **not** automatically make the party travel faster.

Its main advantage is transportation capacity.

An animal pulling a cart or similar vehicle can move **five times its base carrying capacity**, including the vehicle's own weight. Add the animals' capacities when several pull together. For example, two Riding Horses provide a 4,800-lb total pulling allowance; a 400-lb Wagon leaves 4,400 lb for cargo and occupants, before terrain, welfare, or other specific limits. [2024 equipment](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/phb-2024/equipment#MountsandCargo).

**Which speed rule?** The PHB explicitly gives ordinary land vehicles a travel pace; the DMG directs vehicles with a listed miles-per-hour speed to use that speed without choosing a pace. Use a listed vehicle speed where one exists and establish the DM's handling before combining magical mounts, vehicles, and special-movement rates. A cart itself does not supply an extra speed multiplier.

For a Wizard, this can be extremely useful for carrying:

- Spellbooks
- Backup spellbooks
- Expensive material components
- Crafting materials
- Equipment
- Loot
- Supplies
- Containers
- Corpses or other unusual magical-project materials

## Waterborne Travel

Waterborne vessels are limited by the vessel's own speed rather than choosing the normal Fast, Normal, or Slow travel pace.

Depending on the vessel and crew, a ship may be able to travel for **up to 24 hours per day**.

Weather and water conditions can modify travel speed.

Examples include:

- Strong winds
- Dead calm
- River currents
- Crew availability

## Special Movement

When a party can maintain an unusually high Speed for extended travel, such as through certain spells or magic items, use:

**Miles per hour = Speed ÷ 10**

Then:

**Normal miles per day = Miles per hour × hours traveled**

Typically:

**Miles per day = Miles per hour × 8**

For other paces:

**Fast = Normal distance × 1⅓**

**Slow = Normal distance × ⅔**

Round down where required.

### Example

A sustained **60 ft. Speed** gives:

**60 ÷ 10 = 6 miles per hour**

Over 8 hours:

**6 × 8 = 48 miles at Normal pace**

This system is for movement that can actually be sustained over extended travel, not simply any temporary combat Speed increase.

## Wizard Travel

Wizard mobility should be divided into **Tactical**, **Overland**, and **Strategic** travel.

## Tactical Mobility

### Misty Step

Short-range emergency repositioning.

Best for:

- Escaping melee
- Crossing small obstacles
- Reaching advantageous positions
- Breaking dangerous positioning

### Fly

Tactical and short-duration exploration mobility.

Best for:

- Vertical terrain
- Bypassing ground hazards
- Reaching inaccessible locations
- Crossing gaps
- Combat positioning

### Dimension Door

Powerful medium-range emergency transportation.

Best for:

- Escaping dangerous encounters
- Extracting an ally
- Crossing major obstacles
- Bypassing walls and terrain

## Overland Wizard Travel

## Phantom Steed

One of the Wizard's strongest overland travel spells.

**Casting Time:** 1 minute or Ritual  
**Duration:** 1 hour  
**Speed:** 100 ft.  
**Travel Rate:** **13 miles per hour**

The steed uses the Riding Horse stat block except for its increased Speed and special travel rate.

### Advantages

- Ritual
- No spell slot required when Ritual cast
- 13 miles of travel during its 1-hour duration
- Can be repeatedly recast
- Extremely efficient Wizard transportation

### Party Use

A Wizard can create multiple Phantom Steeds with enough preparation time.

Because each casting creates one steed and the spell lasts 1 hour, maintaining mounts for an entire party requires planning and repeated casting.

Phantom Steed is dramatically faster than ordinary overland travel.

## Strategic Wizard Travel

## Teleportation Circle

Long-distance transportation to known permanent circles.

Known permanent sigils expand destination options.

Relevant constraints:

- Location
- Sigil sequence
- Plane
- Owner or faction
- Access restrictions
- Security
- Nearby resources
- Strategic importance

A growing list of known circles becomes a Wizard's **transportation network**.

## Teleport

Makes geographic distance dramatically less important.

Reliability depends on familiarity with the destination.

Whenever possible:

- Visit important locations personally.
- Obtain reliable destination information.
- Maintain safe locations to teleport to.
- Establish fallback destinations.

## Plane Shift

Strategic planar transportation.

Relevant constraints:

- Tuning forks
- Associated planes
- Safe destinations
- Known settlements
- Environmental dangers
- Return routes

Losing access to the correct tuning fork can remove access to an entire planar route.

---

## Wizard Travel Priority

For increasing distances, think roughly:

**Misty Step → Fly → Dimension Door → Phantom Steed → Teleportation Circle → Teleport / Plane Shift**

But these spells solve different problems.

| Problem | Preferred Tool |
|---|---|
| Immediate repositioning | Misty Step |
| Vertical / hazardous terrain | Fly |
| Emergency extraction | Dimension Door |
| Overland travel | Phantom Steed |
| Known magical network | Teleportation Circle |
| Extreme geographic distance | Teleport |
| Planar distance | Plane Shift |

> Do not spend high-level resources solving a travel problem that a ritual, mount, road, vehicle, or established transportation network can solve efficiently.

Keep **combat movement**, **overland travel**, and **strategic magical transportation** separate.

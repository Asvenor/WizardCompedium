## Damage Types

- Acid
- Bludgeoning
- Cold
- Fire
- Force
- Lightning
- Necrotic
- Piercing
- Poison
- Psychic
- Radiant
- Slashing
- Thunder

## Wizard Use

Do not choose damage only by average numbers.

Consider:

- Resistance
- Immunity
- Vulnerability
- Saving throw targeted
- Area and number of targets
- Whether the spell uses Concentration
- Whether damage actually removes an enemy action

## Practical Rule

**Apply damage in order:** bonuses, reductions and other adjustments first; Resistance second (**halve, round down**); Vulnerability third (**double**). Multiple sources of the same Resistance do not quarter damage. For example, 28 Fire damage reduced by 5, then resisted and made vulnerable, becomes **22**, not 23: (28 − 5) → 23 → 11 → 22.

Immunity to a damage type prevents that damage. Immunity to a condition prevents that condition; **Poisoned immunity is not automatically Poison damage immunity**, or vice versa. Damage types do not automatically add conditions: Cold does not universally slow, and Lightning does not universally stun.

Source: [2024 PHB — Resistance and Vulnerability](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/phb-2024/playing-the-game#ResistanceandVulnerability).

If an enemy appears resistant or immune to a damage type, switch jobs rather than repeatedly forcing the same damage plan.

Control, terrain, buffs, summons, and no-save effects can bypass damage resistance entirely.

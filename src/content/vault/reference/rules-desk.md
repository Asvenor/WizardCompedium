## Resolve a ruling

1. Establish which edition, book, printing, and errata the table uses.
2. Open the exact spell, feature, item, or creature entry. A tier list is an opinion about usefulness.
3. Check targeting, sight, clear path, action/reaction, concentration, components, duration, and immunity.
4. Check specific exceptions and relevant errata before applying a general rule.
5. If an interaction remains ambiguous, ask the DM which interpretation applies before relying on it. Compare the precise source text and any official clarification.

## Probability and cost reference

[Wizard Optimization Math](/vault-assets/PDF%20for%20the%20Compendium/wizard_optimization_math_2024.pdf) supplies baseline save, attack, initiative, travel, spellbook, and binding calculations. Its assumptions matter: repeated Concentration rolls use a constant bonus, initiative ties are modeled as 50/50, and binding costs assume successful attempts. Special movement rates require a sustainable special speed; they do not replace the ordinary walking-pace table. Three attuned items is the general limit, subject to explicit features such as higher-level Artificer benefits. The 100-point item score is a custom comparison heuristic, not an official ranking or a required character tracker.

**Damage precision:** the PDF's save-for-half table uses the usual average-damage approximation. If each successful save halves the rolled damage and rounds down, 8d6 averages **28 on failure and 13.75 on success**, not 14. At failure probabilities of 40% / 60% / 80%, exact expected damage is **19.45 / 22.30 / 25.15 per target**, before resistance, immunity, overkill, and other adjustments. The difference does not change the table's broad tactical comparison.

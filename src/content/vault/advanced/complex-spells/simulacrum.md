**Rules baseline:** 2024 Player's Handbook.

> **Simulacrum converts gold, preparation, and a 7th-level spell slot into a persistent second creature using the copied target's game statistics.**
>
> For a Wizard, its greatest value is usually not raw damage.
>
> It is **action economy, additional spellcasting, a second Concentration slot, additional reactions, independent positioning, and strategic utility.**

---

## Core Rules

| Property | Simulacrum |
|---|---|
| **Level** | 7th |
| **School** | Illusion |
| **Casting Time** | 12 hours |
| **Range** | Touch |
| **Duration** | Until Dispelled |
| **Target** | Beast or Humanoid |
| **Consumed Component** | Powdered ruby worth 1,500+ GP |
| **Concentration** | Required throughout the 12-hour casting; not after creation |

The target must remain within **10 feet** of you for the entire casting.

At completion, you touch:

- The target
- A same-sized pile of ice or snow

The pile becomes the Simulacrum.

---

## What Gets Copied?

The Simulacrum uses the target's **game statistics at the time of casting**, with three explicit changes:

1. Its creature type becomes **Construct**
2. Its Hit Point maximum becomes **half** the original's
3. It **cannot cast Simulacrum**

This makes the exact state of the target when the spell is completed important.

---

## Control

The Simulacrum is:

- Friendly to you
- Friendly to creatures you designate
- Obedient to your commands

In combat:

> **It acts on your turn.**

This is extremely important for coordinated spellcasting.

You do not need to wait for a separate Initiative result to execute a planned combination.

---

## The Core Wizard Engine

A Wizard Simulacrum potentially gives you:

**Wizard**

+

**Second Wizard-like creature**

which means access to:

- Two actions
- Two positions
- Two Concentration slots
- Additional reactions
- Additional spellcasting resources
- Additional utility
- Additional magic-item interactions
- Additional information-gathering capability

The Simulacrum should therefore be treated as a **resource multiplier**, not merely a minion.

---

## Dual Concentration

One of the strongest benefits is having another creature capable of maintaining its own Concentration.

### Example

**Wizard**

→ [Wall of Force](/library/spells/spells/)

**Simulacrum**

→ Sickening Radiance

Or:

**Wizard**

→ [Maze](/library/spells/spells/) on the primary threat

**Simulacrum**

→ Controls the remaining encounter

Or:

**Wizard**

→ Battlefield control

**Simulacrum**

→ Summoning spell

The normal one-Concentration limitation applies separately to each creature.

---

## Coordinated Turns

Because the Simulacrum acts on your turn, you can coordinate effects very precisely.

This is particularly valuable for combinations requiring:

**Setup**

↓

**Follow-up**

For example:

**Creature A creates hazard**

↓

**Creature B creates containment**

Or:

**Creature A applies control**

↓

**Creature B immediately exploits that control**

This minimizes the opportunity for enemies to respond between the two effects.

Do not assume this duplicates physical equipment or supplies components; equip the duplicate for both spells. In particular, establish a persistent hazard before a force barrier that would prevent placing it afterward. Acting on your turn improves coordination, but does not erase targeting, components, or either creature's own action/resource limits.

---

## Action Economy

The largest mistake is evaluating Simulacrum as:

> "Another spellcaster that has some spell slots."

The more important advantage is:

> **Another creature taking meaningful actions every round.**

In a four-round encounter:

Without Simulacrum:

**~4 Wizard Actions**

With Simulacrum:

**~8 combined Actions**

before considering:

- Reactions
- Concentration
- Movement
- Object interaction
- Magic items
- Other features

This can fundamentally change encounter balance.

---

## Resource Conservation

The 2024 Simulacrum:

- Cannot gain levels
- Cannot take Short Rests
- Cannot take Long Rests

Therefore, treat its expendable resources as **finite unless a specific feature provides another valid method of recovery**.

## Do Not Waste Its Resources

Avoid spending powerful limited resources on:

- Easy encounters
- Routine travel
- Problems the normal party can solve
- Low-value utility

Instead preserve them for:

- Boss encounters
- Major spellcasters
- Campaign-critical fights
- Emergencies
- Strategic objectives

---

## Resource Priority

Think of Simulacrum resources in tiers.

## Renewable / Repeatable

Use relatively freely where the copied statistics actually provide repeatable options.

Examples may include:

- Cantrips
- Movement
- Ordinary actions
- Non-resource abilities
- Skills
- Information gathering

## Limited

Use deliberately.

Examples may include:

- Spell slots
- Limited-use features
- Charges from resources tied to the copied statistics

## Irreplaceable

Preserve aggressively.

If a resource requires a Short or Long Rest to recover, the Simulacrum's inability to take those rests may make it effectively nonrenewable.

---

## Spell Slot Doctrine

Do not use the Simulacrum as:

> **Fireball machine #2**

unless that damage is actually necessary.

Its limited high-level slots are more valuable when they produce effects that fundamentally change encounters.

Prioritize:

- Control
- Emergency defense
- Countermagic
- Encounter splitting
- Mobility when essential
- High-value utility

The Simulacrum's highest-level resources should solve problems that ordinary resources cannot.

---

## Counterspell Coverage

A second caster can dramatically improve reaction coverage.

Potentially:

**Wizard Reaction**

+

**Simulacrum Reaction**

This can matter enormously against enemy spellcasters.

Possible benefits include:

- More Counterspell opportunities
- Independent positioning for Counterspell range
- Less pressure on one creature's Reaction
- Better coverage against multiple enemy casters

See [Countering Spellcasters](/library/tactics/countering-spellcasters/).

---

## Positioning

Do not stand beside your Simulacrum unless necessary.

Two Wizard-like creatures occupying different positions can:

- Cover different Counterspell ranges
- Attack from different angles
- Avoid the same AoE
- Maintain separate lines of sight
- Control larger portions of the battlefield
- Reduce the chance of losing both to one effect

### Principle

> **Two casters should usually create two threat zones.**

---

## Protect the Simulacrum

The Simulacrum has only:

**Half the original creature's Hit Point maximum.**

It is therefore substantially more fragile than the original.

Protect it using:

- Distance
- Cover
- Line-of-sight control
- Defensive spells
- Terrain
- Positioning
- Summons
- Walls
- Other creatures

The Simulacrum should rarely be the easiest valuable target available to the enemy.

---

## Repairing Damage

Normal healing is not the Simulacrum's recovery system.

The spell provides a specific repair method.

To restore Hit Points:

1. You take a Long Rest.
2. The Simulacrum remains within **5 feet** of you during the repair.
3. You expend components worth **100 GP per Hit Point restored**.

### Repair Cost

| HP Restored | Cost |
|---:|---:|
| **1 HP** | 100 GP |
| **5 HP** | 500 GP |
| **10 HP** | 1,000 GP |
| **25 HP** | 2,500 GP |
| **50 HP** | 5,000 GP |

Repairs become extremely expensive.

---

## Repair Economics

Because repair costs are so high:

> **Preventing damage is much cheaper than repairing damage.**

Spending:

- Defensive positioning
- Cover
- Movement
- Low-level defensive spells

can be economically superior to allowing the Simulacrum to absorb damage.

### Example

Preventing **10 damage** potentially saves:

**1,000 GP**

This changes how aggressively the Simulacrum should be used.

---

## Disposable vs. Preserved Simulacrum

There are two broad strategies.

## Preserved Asset

Treat it as a long-term strategic resource.

Prioritize:

- Safety
- Resource conservation
- Limited exposure
- High-value missions

Best when:

- Gold is limited
- Spell resources remain valuable
- The Simulacrum has important capabilities

---

## Disposable Asset

Use it aggressively when the immediate objective is worth more than preserving the duplicate.

Potentially appropriate for:

- Campaign climax
- Extreme emergency
- Preventing party death
- Defeating a major villain

Do not preserve a Simulacrum at the cost of losing the campaign objective.

---

## Who Should You Copy?

The spell can copy:

- A Beast
- A Humanoid

For a Wizard optimizer, the obvious candidate is often:

> **Yourself**

because this potentially duplicates the statistics that make you valuable:

- Wizard spellcasting
- Class features
- Feats
- Saving throws
- Skills
- Other statistics present at the time of casting

However, another powerful Humanoid can potentially be worth considering.

---

## Copying Yourself

### Advantages

You know exactly what capabilities you're getting.

You potentially duplicate:

- Your spellcasting package
- Your Wizard features
- Your optimized statistics
- Your utility
- Your tactical options

This is generally the cleanest use of Simulacrum for a high-level Wizard.

---

## Copy Timing

The Simulacrum uses the original's statistics:

> **at the time of casting**

Therefore, consider the target's current state before beginning the 12-hour casting.

This matters particularly for resources represented in the copied game statistics.

### Optimization Question

Before completing Simulacrum, ask:

> **Is the target currently in the state I actually want copied?**

---

## Equipment

The 2024 spell no longer contains the old explicit sentence stating that the Simulacrum is formed without equipment.

However, the new wording does not straightforwardly state that physical equipment is duplicated either.

<aside class="callout callout-warning" data-callout="warning">

**Equipment Ruling**

Do not assume that Simulacrum duplicates valuable physical equipment or magic items merely because the creature's game statistics reflect their use.

Treat equipment duplication as a separate rules question requiring DM adjudication.

</aside>

The safest operational assumption is:

> **Equip the Simulacrum separately.**

---

## Magic Items

A Simulacrum is a creature.

That makes non-attunement magic-item use potentially very important.

However, forming a normal attunement bond requires completing a Short Rest, and a Simulacrum explicitly cannot take Short or Long Rests.

Therefore:

- A Simulacrum can use appropriate **non-attunement items**.
- It cannot normally attune to a new item.
- Whether a newly created Simulacrum somehow inherits an existing attunement state is not established by the spell and requires DM adjudication.

<aside class="callout callout-warning" data-callout="warning">

**Do Not Plan a Secondary Attunement Package**

Under the normal 2024 procedure, the Simulacrum cannot complete the Short Rest required to attune. Build its equipment package around non-attunement items unless the DM explicitly rules otherwise.

</aside>

See [Magic Item Attunement](/library/items/magic-item-attunement/).

---

## Non-Attunement Items

Non-attunement magic items are especially attractive because they can expand the Simulacrum's capabilities without competing for scarce attunement slots.

When evaluating magic items, consider:

> **Would this item be more valuable on my Simulacrum than sitting unused in storage?**

---

## Downtime Utility

The Simulacrum does not need to exist only for combat.

Potential uses include:

- Research
- Guard duty
- Information gathering
- Maintaining a Wizard facility
- Communication
- Helping operate a Bastion
- Performing repeatable magical utility
- Accompanying allies

However, remember:

**It cannot take Short or Long Rests.**

Do not casually spend finite resources on mundane downtime work.

---

## Bastion Use

A Simulacrum can potentially become an extremely valuable Bastion asset.

Possible roles:

- Defensive caster
- Guardian
- Research assistant
- Emergency responder
- Magical specialist

Its presence means your Bastion can potentially retain a powerful Wizard-like defender even when you are elsewhere.

See [Bastion](/library/bastions/bastion/).

---

## Simulacrum Limit

The 2024 spell contains two major anti-abuse protections.

## Cannot Cast Simulacrum

The duplicate explicitly:

> **cannot cast Simulacrum.**

This shuts down the classic direct Simulacrum-chain engine.

---

## Recasting Destroys Your Previous Simulacrum

If **you cast Simulacrum again**, any Simulacrum you previously created with the spell is instantly destroyed.

Therefore:

**Wizard casts Simulacrum A**

↓

**Wizard casts Simulacrum B**

↓

**Simulacrum A is destroyed**

The standard spell does not let one Wizard simply accumulate an unlimited army through repeated normal castings.

---

## 2014 Exploit Warning

Older optimization guides frequently discuss:

- Simulacrum chains
- Simulacrum armies
- Simulacra casting Simulacrum
- Infinite duplication loops

<aside class="callout callout-warning" data-callout="warning">

**Legacy Information**

Do not automatically import these strategies into a 2024 rules compendium.

The 2024 version explicitly prevents the Simulacrum from casting Simulacrum.

</aside>

This is an important example of why older Wizard optimization guides must be checked against current wording.

## Wish + Simulacrum

[Wish](/library/advanced/complex-spells/wish/) deserves separate analysis.

Wish can reproduce the effect of another spell according to Wish's own rules, which creates questions involving:

- Casting time
- Components
- Creation timing
- Simulacrum restrictions
- Whether the result counts as you casting Simulacrum for the final destruction clause

These interactions should **not** be assumed from older 2014 Simulacrum-loop guides.

<aside class="callout callout-warning" data-callout="warning">

**Complex Interaction**

Verify the exact 2024 Wish interaction separately before treating Wish + Simulacrum as an established exploit.

</aside>

See [Wish](/library/advanced/complex-spells/wish/) and [Arcane Exploits](/library/tactics/arcane-exploits/).

## Simulacrum + Complex Spells

A duplicate Wizard can potentially dramatically increase the value of spells that benefit from having another independent caster.

Examples include:

- [Glyph of Warding](/library/advanced/complex-spells/glyph-of-warding/)
- [Planar Binding](/library/advanced/complex-spells/planar-binding/)
- [Contingency](/library/advanced/complex-spells/contingency-optimization/)
- [Magic Jar](/library/advanced/complex-spells/magic-jar-optimization/)
- [Wish](/library/advanced/complex-spells/wish/)
- Battlefield control spells
- Summoning spells

The important question is always:

> **Does the Simulacrum have the required resource, and can that resource ever be recovered?**

---

## Concentration Engine

Simulacrum's second Concentration slot is one of its most consistently powerful benefits.

Useful combinations include:

### Containment + Hazard

**Wizard:** Wall of Force

**Simulacrum:** Persistent Concentration hazard

### Double Control

**Wizard:** Controls one section of battlefield

**Simulacrum:** Controls another

### Control + Summon

**Wizard:** Battlefield control

**Simulacrum:** Concentration summon

### Mobility + Control

**Wizard:** Control

**Simulacrum:** Maintains party mobility effect

See [Concentration Strategy](/library/tactics/concentration-strategy/).

---

## Simulacrum Combat Doctrine

## Before Combat

- Preserve high-level resources
- Position separately
- Determine Reaction responsibilities
- Decide which creature will establish primary Concentration
- Protect the Simulacrum

## Opening Round

Prioritize combinations where two actions immediately change the encounter.

Example:

**Wizard Action**

→ Divide battlefield

**Simulacrum Action**

→ Disable remaining threats

## Later Rounds

Avoid spending slots simply because two casters are available.

Use:

- Cantrips
- Dodge
- Positioning
- Items
- Repeatable abilities
- Non-slot actions

when the encounter is already controlled.

---

## Reaction Doctrine

Decide in advance which creature is responsible for which reactions.

For example:

### Wizard

Primary Counterspell coverage

### Simulacrum

Secondary Counterspell / defensive reaction

Or reverse the roles depending on:

- Position
- Remaining spell slots
- Threat
- Concentration
- Personal safety

Do not waste both reactions responding unnecessarily to the same problem.

---

## Simulacrum Resource Limits

| Resource | Optimization consequence |
|---|---|
| **HP** | Repairs cost 100 GP per HP and require the creator's Long Rest |
| **Spell slots** | Rest-based recovery is unavailable; reserve high-level slots for decisive effects |
| **Limited features** | Recovery works only if the feature has a valid non-rest method |
| **Attunement** | Ordinary new attunement requires a Short Rest, which the duplicate cannot take |
| **Consumables** | Use only when the resulting action is worth both the item and the duplicate's exposure |

---

## When to Recreate It

Eventually a Simulacrum may become sufficiently depleted that replacing it is more efficient than preserving it.

Consider:

- Remaining HP
- Remaining spell slots
- Remaining limited-use features
- Repair cost
- 1,500 GP replacement cost
- 12-hour casting time
- Current target statistics
- Upcoming threats

### Important Economic Comparison

A new Simulacrum costs at least:

**1,500 GP**

Repairing only:

**15 HP**

also costs:

**1,500 GP**

This means heavy repairs can quickly become economically questionable.

However, replacing the Simulacrum also resets the entire asset and requires another 12-hour casting with the desired target available.

---

## Simulacrum Principle

> **Do not treat Simulacrum as another party member with renewable resources.**
>
> Treat it as a persistent stockpile of actions, Concentration, spellcasting, and limited resources that you decide when to spend.

Its power comes from converting:

**1,500+ GP**

+

**12 hours**

+

**A 7th-level spell slot**

into:

**A persistent second creature using the target's game statistics.**

For a high-level Wizard, that can be one of the largest action-economy multipliers available.

The optimization challenge is keeping that asset alive and spending its finite resources only when they materially change the outcome.

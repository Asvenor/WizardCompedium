<aside class="callout callout-important" data-callout="important">

**Does the spell still work in the triggering condition?**

A low-HP trigger and an Unconscious/0-HP trigger are not interchangeable. Resilient Sphere, Greater Invisibility, Fly, and Polymorph require Concentration, so they are not reliable concentration-based rescue while you are Incapacitated. Ordinary temporary HP do not wake a creature unconscious at 0 HP. Blink also is not immediate off-turn extraction: the first disappearance roll is at the end of your own turn, so triggering it during an enemy's turn does not remove you before follow-up attacks. [SRD 5.2.1: Contingency, Blink, Concentration, and Incapacitated](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/srd/5.2/SRD_CC_v5.2.1.pdf).

</aside>

> **Contingency converts preparation into future action economy.**
>
> You cast a spell in advance, define the condition that releases it, and carry that protection for up to 10 days.
>
> The real optimization problem is:
>
> **Which spell should I store, and what trigger should release it?**

---

## Core Rules

| Property | Contingency |
|---|---|
| **Level** | 6th |
| **School** | Abjuration |
| **Casting Time** | 10 minutes |
| **Range** | Self |
| **Duration** | 10 days |
| **Concentration** | Required during the 10-minute setup; not for the waiting Contingency |
| **Material** | Gem-encrusted statuette of yourself worth 1,500+ GP |
| **Consumed?** | No |
| **Maximum Contingent Spell** | Level 5 |
| **Contingent Spell Casting Time** | Action |
| **Target Requirement** | Must be able to target you |

You cast both:

**Contingency**

and

**the contingent spell**

during the 10-minute setup.

Both spell slots are expended immediately.

The contingent spell does **not** take effect yet.

Instead, it waits for the trigger.

---

## Trigger Resolution

When creating the Contingency, describe a trigger.

When that trigger occurs for the first time:

**Trigger occurs**

↓

**Contingent spell takes effect immediately afterward**

↓

**Contingency ends**

This happens whether you want the spell to activate at that moment or not.

Therefore:

> **Trigger quality is as important as spell choice.**

---

## Important Restrictions

The contingent spell must:

- Be level **5 or lower**
- Have a casting time of **an Action**
- Be a spell you can cast
- Be capable of targeting you

The contingent spell ultimately affects **only you**, even if it could normally target additional creatures.

You can have only:

**1 Contingency active at a time**

Casting Contingency again ends the previous Contingency.

---

## The Statuette

Contingency requires:

**Gem-encrusted statuette of yourself worth at least 1,500 GP**

The statuette is **not consumed**.

However:

> **Contingency ends if the statuette is ever not on your person.**

Treat the statuette as important equipment.

Protect it accordingly.

Possible precautions:

- Secure internal pocket
- Protected component pouch
- Redundant physical attachment
- Concealed storage on your person

Losing the statuette means losing the Contingency.

---

## What Makes a Good Contingent Spell?

The best contingent spell usually solves a problem where:

**waiting until your next turn would be dangerous.**

Contingency is therefore especially valuable for:

- Emergency defense
- Escape
- Condition removal
- Survival
- Mobility
- Protection from sudden threats

The stored spell should solve something that normally threatens your ability to respond.

---

## Contingency Roles

## Emergency Defense

Trigger a defensive spell when a dangerous condition occurs.

Potential objectives:

- Prevent follow-up attacks
- Become harder to target
- Escape confinement
- Survive sudden focus fire
- Protect Concentration
- Prevent an enemy from exploiting your vulnerable state

---

## Emergency Escape

A Contingency can potentially create an automatic escape response.

Ideal when:

- Surrounded
- Grappled
- Restrained
- Trapped
- Isolated
- Suddenly exposed to overwhelming danger

The exact spell must still satisfy Contingency's targeting and casting-time requirements.

---

## Condition Response

Contingency can potentially react to dangerous conditions before you get another turn.

Examples of problems worth preparing against:

- Incapacitation
- Restraint
- Dangerous transformations
- Environmental hazards
- Debilitating magical effects

The contingent spell must actually be capable of solving the chosen problem.

---

## Strong Contingency Candidates

The exact best spell depends heavily on your spellbook and campaign.

## Dimension Door

### Extraction role

**Immediate non-Concentration extraction.** Unlike Blink, its effect does not wait for the end of your next turn. The destination must be within 500 feet; agree during setup how the chosen destination will resolve when the trigger occurs. Contingency moves only you, even though ordinary Dimension Door can bring an ally.

### Cost and counter

An occupied arrival space makes the teleport fail and deals **4d6 Force** damage. Teleportation wards can defeat the route. Prefer a concrete, legal destination over wording that assumes the spell automatically discovers the safest location. [Dimension Door](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/phb-2024/spell-descriptions#DimensionDoor) · [Contingency](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/phb-2024/spell-descriptions#Contingency).

---

## Otiluke's Resilient Sphere

### Role

**Emergency protection**

Potentially useful when:

- Heavily focused
- Surrounded
- About to receive overwhelming attacks
- You need time for allies to recover the situation

### Strength

Can radically change your immediate vulnerability.

### Cost

You may temporarily remove yourself from contributing normally to the fight.

Use it when survival is more important than maintaining offense.

---

## Greater Invisibility

### Role

**Defense + positioning**

Potentially useful when:

- Targeted by enemies
- Entering dangerous combat
- Exposed to attackers relying on sight

### Important

Requires Concentration.

If the contingent spell requires Concentration when it takes effect, it functions normally and therefore requires you to Concentrate on it.

Do not accidentally destroy an already more valuable Concentration spell.

---

## Fly

### Role

**Emergency mobility**

Potentially useful against:

- Falling
- Ground hazards
- Inaccessible enemies
- Terrain traps
- Sudden vertical positioning problems

### Important

Fly requires Concentration.

Trigger design should account for what you might already be Concentrating on.

---

## Fire Shield

### Role

**Non-Concentration defense**

Advantages:

- No Concentration
- Long duration
- Defensive resistance choice
- Punishes nearby attackers

Particularly attractive because it does not compete with your normal Concentration spell.

---

## Mirror Image

### Role

**Non-Concentration defense**

Useful because it can increase immediate survivability without occupying Concentration.

This is especially attractive when you expect weapon or spell attacks.

It does not protect against saving-throw effects or damage without an attack roll, and attackers with Blindsight, Truesight, or the Blinded condition ignore it. Unlike Concentration payloads, it can remain useful after you become Unconscious; it still does not heal or wake you. [Mirror Image](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/phb-2024/spell-descriptions#MirrorImage).

---

## Blink

### Role

**Defensive disruption**

Can make repeatedly targeting the Wizard substantially more difficult.

Does not require Concentration.

However, its protection is probabilistic rather than guaranteed.

---

## Protection from Energy

### Role

**Damage-type response**

Potentially useful when a particular elemental damage type suddenly becomes a major threat.

Requires Concentration.

The trigger must be written carefully because the spell requires choosing the damage type.

---

## Polymorph

### Role

**Emergency body / survival**

Potentially useful as a large emergency HP buffer or transformation tool.

However:

- Requires Concentration
- Replaces many of your normal capabilities while transformed
- Can interfere with Wizard spellcasting

This is a specialized Contingency rather than a universal default.

---

## Concentration Warning

Contingency itself does **not** require Concentration.

However:

> **The contingent spell functions normally when triggered.**

Therefore, if the contingent spell requires Concentration:

**You must Concentrate on it.**

This creates an important optimization problem.

Imagine:

**You are Concentrating on Wall of Force**

↓

**Contingency triggers Greater Invisibility**

↓

**Greater Invisibility requires Concentration**

You cannot maintain both normally.

This can destroy the spell that was already winning the encounter.

---

## Prefer Non-Concentration Protection

When two Contingency options provide similar protection, strongly consider the one that does **not** require Concentration.

This allows you to maintain:

- Web
- Hypnotic Pattern
- Sleet Storm
- Wall of Force
- Telekinesis
- Maze
- Summoning spells
- Other control

while the Contingency handles your emergency.

See [Concentration Strategy](/library/tactics/concentration-strategy/).

---

## Trigger Design

A bad trigger can waste the entire spell.

A good trigger should be:

- Specific
- Objective
- Relevant
- Difficult to trigger accidentally
- Broad enough to catch the intended emergency
- Narrow enough to avoid wasting the spell

---

## Bad Trigger

> **"When I am in danger."**

Problems:

- What counts as danger?
- Does seeing an enemy count?
- Does crossing a dangerous bridge count?
- Does taking 1 damage count?

The wording is unnecessarily ambiguous.

---

## Better Trigger

> **"Immediately after I become Restrained against my will."**

This identifies:

- A specific condition
- An involuntary event
- A clear activation point

---

## Trigger Philosophy

Prefer triggers based on **observable game states**.

Examples:

- When I become Restrained against my will
- When I become Grappled against my will
- When I begin falling and have no safe means of stopping the fall
- Immediately after I take a specified type or amount of damage
- When a specific dangerous condition affects me

Avoid overly complicated triggers attempting to predict every possible situation.

---

## HP Threshold Triggers

A tempting trigger is:

> **"When I fall below X HP."**

This can work conceptually but has disadvantages.

The Contingency activates **after** the triggering event.

Therefore, it does not retroactively prevent the damage that caused the threshold to be crossed.

If one attack takes you from:

**60 HP → 0 HP**

a trigger at:

**below 30 HP**

does not activate before that damage occurs.

Design survival contingencies around this limitation.

---

## Falling Contingency

Falling is a particularly useful emergency to automate because it can happen outside your turn.

A suitable contingent mobility spell may prevent or mitigate the situation without requiring:

- Your Action
- Your Reaction
- Your next turn

This demonstrates the core value of Contingency:

> **Solving emergencies before normal action economy would allow you to respond.**

---

## Action Economy

Contingency's greatest combat advantage is not simply obtaining another spell effect.

It is:

**The spell was cast days ago.**

When the trigger occurs, you do not need to spend your current:

- Action
- Bonus Action
- Reaction

to produce the contingent effect.

This allows your normal turn to remain available.

---

## Example

### Without Contingency

**Emergency occurs**

↓

**Spend next Action solving emergency**

↓

**Lose offensive/control tempo**

### With Contingency

**Emergency occurs**

↓

**Contingent spell activates**

↓

**Normal Action remains available**

↓

**Continue controlling encounter**

This is why Contingency can be substantially stronger than the stored spell appears by itself.

---

## Preparation Strategy

Contingency lasts:

**10 days**

This makes it a long-term preparation spell rather than something that needs to be cast every adventuring day.

Once established:

- Carry the statuette
- Track expiration
- Recast before important missions
- Change the stored spell when circumstances change

---

## Contingency Selection Reference

| Choice | Rules consequence |
|---|---|
| **Stored spell** | Must be level 5 or lower, Action-cast, castable by the Wizard, and capable of targeting the Wizard |
| **Trigger** | First occurrence activates automatically, immediately after the event |
| **Duration** | Up to 10 days; losing the carried statuette ends the spell |
| **Concentration** | A Concentration payload competes with existing control and fails as reliable rescue while Incapacitated |
| **Purpose** | Match the payload to the danger; temporary HP do not revive an Unconscious creature at 0 HP |

## Choosing Your Current Contingency

Before an adventure, ask:

### What is most likely to kill or disable me?

**Physical focus fire?**

→ Defensive Contingency

**Enemy spellcasters?**

→ Magical-defense Contingency

**Dangerous terrain?**

→ Mobility Contingency

**Capture / restraint likely?**

→ Escape Contingency

**Known elemental threat?**

→ Damage-resistance Contingency

**Unknown expedition?**

→ General-purpose defensive Contingency

Your Contingency should change when the mission changes.

## Contingency and Information

Contingency becomes stronger when you know what you are preparing for.

Information from:

- Divination
- Scouting
- Familiar reconnaissance
- Scrying
- Arcane Eye
- Intelligence gathering
- Previous encounters

can inform the correct contingent spell.

This creates a powerful Wizard loop:

**Gather Information**

↓

**Identify Threat**

↓

**Prepare Contingency**

↓

**Enter Encounter With Countermeasure Already Loaded**

See [Information Warfare](/library/tactics/information-warfare/).

## Important Rules Interactions

## Trigger Requires No Action

Once established, the contingent spell activates from the defined trigger rather than requiring your normal Action.

---

## Trigger Is Automatic

The first time the defined trigger occurs:

**the spell activates whether you want it to or not.**

You cannot simply decide:

> "Not this time."

Trigger wording must account for this.

---

## Self Only

Even if the contingent spell could normally affect multiple creatures:

**Contingency makes it take effect only on you.**

Do not build a Contingency around protecting the entire party.

---

## One Contingency

You cannot stack several Contingencies on yourself.

A new casting ends the previous one.

This makes your single active Contingency a strategic choice.

---

## Statuette Dependency

If the statuette stops being on your person:

**Contingency ends.**

This creates an unusual vulnerability to:

- Theft
- Disarmament of equipment
- Capture
- Losing possessions
- Separation from gear

Protect the statuette.

---

## Arcane Exploits

Contingency can produce unusual interactions because its trigger system operates outside normal turn timing.

Potential exploit areas include:

- Trigger wording
- Automatic spell activation
- Action-economy manipulation
- Condition-response automation
- Interaction with unusual self-targeting spells

Any extreme interpretation should be individually verified before being added as established RAW.

See [Arcane Exploits](/library/tactics/arcane-exploits/).

---

## Contingency Principle

> **Contingency should solve the emergency that would otherwise prevent you from taking your next useful Wizard turn.**

The strongest Contingency is not necessarily the strongest spell you can store.

It is the spell that automatically solves your most dangerous likely failure state.

Contingency converts:

**Preparation**

+

**Information**

+

**Two spell slots spent in advance**

into:

**Automatic emergency response without spending your future action economy.**

That makes it one of the defining spells of a prepared high-level Wizard.

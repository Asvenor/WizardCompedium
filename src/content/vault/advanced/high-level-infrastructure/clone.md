> **Clone converts gold, preparation, and 120 days of downtime into a persistent safeguard against death.**
> 
> The spell is simple. The optimization comes from **security, redundancy, recovery equipment, location, and contingency planning**.

## Core Rules

|Property|Clone|
|---|---|
|**Level**|8th|
|**School**|Necromancy|
|**Casting Time**|1 hour|
|**Range**|Touch|
|**Duration**|Instantaneous|
|**Concentration**|Required during the 1-hour casting; not afterward|
|**Maturation**|120 days|

Material components:

- **Diamond worth 1,000+ GP — consumed**
    
- **Sealable vessel worth 2,000+ GP**
    
- Vessel must be large enough to hold the creature being cloned
    

Total initial infrastructure requirement:

**3,000+ GP**

The 2,000 GP vessel is not listed as consumed.

---

## Creating the Clone

You touch either:

- The creature
    
- Or at least **1 cubic inch of its flesh**
    

An inert duplicate begins forming inside the vessel.

After:

**120 days**

the clone finishes growing.

Until then:

> **It provides no protection against death.**

---

## Mature Clone

Once mature, the clone:

- Remains inert
    
- Persists indefinitely
    
- Remains viable while its vessel stays undisturbed
    

There is no stated expiration date.

This means Clone rewards **long-term preparation**.

---

## Death & Transfer

Once the clone has matured:

**Original creature dies**

↓

**Soul is free**

↓

**Soul is willing to return**

↓

**Soul transfers to the clone**

↓

**Clone becomes the creature's new body**

The clone has the original's:

- Personality
    
- Memories
    
- Abilities
    

but:

> **None of the original's equipment.**

---

## Original Body

After the soul transfers:

The original remains become **inert**.

They cannot subsequently be revived because the soul is now elsewhere.

This prevents treating the original corpse as another resurrection path after Clone has activated.

---

## Age Selection

When creating the clone, you choose whether it is:

- The same age as the creature
    
- Or **younger**
    

This gives Clone an obvious longevity application.

A sufficiently prepared Wizard can maintain younger replacement bodies over time.

Clone therefore protects against more than violent death.

It can potentially form part of a long-term strategy against aging.

---

## The Real Problem: Equipment

Clone returns:

**You**

but not:

**Your stuff.**

For a Wizard this is a major distinction.

You may wake without:

- Spellbook
    
- Arcane Focus
    
- Component Pouch
    
- Armor
    
- Magic Items
    
- Gold
    
- Clothing
    
- Adventuring Equipment
    

Therefore:

> **Never build a Clone without building the recovery system around it.**

---

## Clone Chamber

A proper Clone chamber should contain more than the vessel.

Recommended:

- Clone vessel
    
- Backup spellbook
    
- Arcane Focus
    
- Component Pouch
    
- Clothing
    
- Spare armor
    
- Basic adventuring equipment
    
- Gold
    
- Expensive components
    
- Food
    
- Water
    
- Communication method
    
- Exit method
    

The goal is:

**Death**

↓

**Clone activates**

↓

**Wake**

↓

**Equip**

↓

**Communicate**

↓

**Escape / recover**

---

## Recovery Cache

Clone transfers the person, **not their equipment**, and does not state that expended spell slots or limited-use features recharge. A recovery route should not require an assumed fresh level-8 slot immediately after death; an available rest, usable transport item, or allied extraction may be needed.

At minimum, store:

## Essential

- Backup Spellbook
    
- Arcane Focus
    
- Component Pouch
    
- Clothing
    

## Strongly Recommended

- Gold
    
- Armor
    
- Basic weapons
    
- Healing resources
    
- Food
    
- Water
    

## High-Level

- Spare magic items
    
- Teleportation resources
    
- Expensive spell components
    
- Scrolls
    
- Emergency communication
    

---

## Backup Spellbook

For a Wizard, this may be the most important recovery item.

Your clone retains your **abilities**, but the spell explicitly does not transfer equipment.

Your spellbook is equipment.

Therefore:

> **A Clone chamber without a backup spellbook is an incomplete Wizard resurrection system.**

Maintain a periodically updated copy.

See [Spellbook](/library/advanced/spellbook-stuff/) if applicable.

---

## Spellbook Synchronization

Whenever you acquire important new spells:

**Primary Spellbook**

↓

**Update Backup Spellbook**

Do not allow the backup to remain years behind your active book.

A perfect Clone with an obsolete spellbook is avoidable infrastructure failure.

---

## Security

The Clone vessel must remain **undisturbed**.

Therefore the location should prioritize:

- Secrecy
    
- Physical security
    
- Magical security
    
- Environmental stability
    
- Limited access
    
- Redundancy
    

Avoid obvious locations.

Your bedroom is convenient.

It is also one of the first places an intelligent enemy might search.

---

## Location Selection

Good candidates:

- [Demiplane](/library/advanced/high-level-infrastructure/demiplane/)
    
- Hidden Bastion chamber
    
- Secret underground vault
    
- Remote protected facility
    
- Allied high-security location
    

The location should satisfy two competing requirements:

**Hard for enemies to reach**

but

**Possible for newly resurrected you to leave.**

---

## Demiplane Integration

[Demiplane](/library/advanced/high-level-infrastructure/demiplane/) is one of the strongest infrastructure pairings.

Concept:

**Dedicated Recovery Demiplane**

containing:

- Clone vessel
    
- Backup spellbook
    
- Equipment
    
- Components
    
- Gold
    
- Communication
    
- Exit resources
    

This isolates the Clone from normal physical intrusion.

---

## Critical Demiplane Problem

Do not create:

> **The world's safest coffin.**

If your Clone activates inside a Demiplane and you cannot leave, the resurrection system has failed strategically even though Clone worked mechanically.

Before using a Demiplane, answer:

> **How does newly resurrected me exit this room?**

Build that solution **before** trusting the Clone.

See [Demiplane](/library/advanced/high-level-infrastructure/demiplane/).

---

## Bastion Integration

A Bastion can provide:

- Guards
    
- Staff
    
- Physical security
    
- Equipment
    
- Crafting
    
- Communication
    
- Easier recovery
    

But it is more accessible than a Demiplane.

A strong system can use both:

**Bastion**

→ normal operations

**Demiplane / hidden vault**

→ catastrophic backup

---

## Multiple Clones

The spell does not specify **which clone receives the soul when several are mature**. Multiple vessels can provide redundancy, but a selectable destination or automatic priority order is a DM ruling, not an additional printed feature. Labeling a room "primary" does not itself make it the transfer destination. [Clone](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/phb-2024/spell-descriptions#Clone).

The spell doesn't state that creating another Clone destroys an existing mature one.

This creates the possibility of **redundancy**.

Instead of:

**One Clone**

consider:

**Clone A**

-   
    

**Clone B**

-   
    

**Clone C**

stored in independent secure locations.

---

## Why Redundancy Matters

One Clone creates a single point of failure.

An enemy who discovers and destroys it removes your protection.

Multiple geographically or dimensionally separated clones make total failure harder.

### Principle

> **Do not put every backup in the same room.**

---

## Suggested Redundancy

## Primary Clone

Highly secure recovery location.

## Secondary Clone

Different secure location.

## Emergency Clone

Completely separate fallback infrastructure.

Do not necessarily reveal every location to the same people.

---

## 120-Day Vulnerability Window

A newly cast Clone does nothing for:

**120 days.**

Therefore replacement planning matters.

After a Clone activates:

> **Start replacing your backup infrastructure as soon as practical.**

You have returned to life, but your safety net has been consumed.

---

## Clone Rotation

A mature high-level Wizard could maintain a rotation.

Concept:

**Clone A — Mature**

**Clone B — Mature**

**Clone C — Growing**

If one activates:

**Begin Clone D**

This maintains redundancy over long campaigns.

---

## Cost of Redundancy

Each normal casting requires:

**1,000+ GP consumed diamond**

plus access to an appropriate:

**2,000+ GP vessel**

Multiple Clones therefore represent substantial infrastructure investment.

But at the level where Clone is available:

> **Preventing permanent character death is one of the highest-value uses of wealth available.**

---

## Wish + Clone

[Wish](/library/advanced/complex-spells/wish/) can duplicate any spell of level 8 or lower.

Clone is level 8.

This makes Clone one of the most interesting safe Wish targets.

Wish duplication:

- Uses an Action
    
- Does not require the duplicated spell's normal casting requirements
    
- Does not trigger Wish stress when used for spell duplication
    

Therefore Wish can potentially bypass Clone's normal:

- 1-hour casting time
    
- Expensive casting requirements
    

See [Wish](/library/advanced/complex-spells/wish/).

---

## Why Wish Changes Clone Economics

Normal Clone:

**1,000+ GP consumed diamond**

-   
    

**2,000+ GP vessel**

-   
    

**1 hour**

Wish → Clone:

**9th-level slot**

and the duplicated spell takes effect without requiring its normal casting requirements.

For downtime days where your 9th-level slot would otherwise go unused, this is potentially an extraordinary infrastructure use.

---

## Important Wish Question

Wish bypasses the **requirements to cast Clone**.

It does not automatically erase the rules of Clone's resulting effect.

The duplicate still:

- Forms
    
- Requires 120 days to mature
    
- Remains inert
    
- Transfers the soul only after maturation
    
- Provides no equipment
    

Do not interpret Wish as:

> **Instant mature Clone.**

It isn't.

---

## Clone for Allies

Clone isn't restricted to yourself.

You can target another creature or sufficient flesh from that creature.

Potential beneficiaries:

- Party members
    
- Important allies
    
- Critical NPCs
    

This allows a Wizard to build resurrection infrastructure for the entire party.

---

## Party Clone Network

At sufficiently high wealth:

|Character|Clone|Backup Equipment|Location|
|---|---|---|---|
|Wizard|✓|Spellbook + gear|Secure|
|Cleric|✓|Focus + armor|Secure|
|Fighter|✓|Weapons + armor|Secure|
|Rogue|✓|Equipment|Secure|

This can radically change the consequences of party death.

---

## Clone + Flesh

2024 Clone allows you to cast the spell by touching:

> **At least 1 cubic inch of the creature's flesh**

instead of touching the creature directly.

This is no longer listed as a consumed material component.

That matters for planning and access.

However, possession of flesh does not override other limitations of the spell.

---

## Soul Requirements

Clone only activates if the soul is:

**Free**

and

**Willing to return.**

Therefore Clone can fail against situations involving:

- Soul imprisonment
    
- Soul destruction
    
- Effects preventing return
    
- A creature choosing not to return
    

Clone protects the **body problem**.

It does not automatically solve every **soul problem**.

---

## Death Threat Analysis

When relying heavily on Clone, distinguish:

## Body Threats

Usually what Clone is designed to defeat.

Examples:

- Massive damage
    
- Disintegration of ordinary body
    
- Physical destruction
    
- Normal death
    

## Soul Threats

Potentially bypass Clone.

Examples:

- Soul trapping
    
- Effects explicitly preventing resurrection
    
- Effects controlling the soul
    

Against enemies capable of attacking the soul directly:

> **Clone should not be treated as absolute immortality.**

---

## Clone + Contingency

[Contingency](/library/advanced/complex-spells/contingency-optimization/) protects you **before death**.

Clone protects you **after death**.

They solve different layers.

Ideal philosophy:

**Layer 1 — Avoid death**

→ positioning, defense, Contingency

**Layer 2 — Escape catastrophe**

→ teleportation / emergency systems

**Layer 3 — Death occurs**

→ Clone

This is stronger than relying on Clone alone.

---

## Clone + Death Planning

Clone should integrate directly with:

[Death & Contingency Planning](/library/advanced/death-contingency-planning/)

That note should answer:

- What happens when I drop?
    
- What happens when I die?
    
- Where do I wake?
    
- What equipment do I have?
    
- Who knows?
    
- How do I return?
    
- What if enemies have my corpse?
    
- What if my soul is trapped?
    

Clone is one layer in the larger system.

---

## Clone + Glyph of Warding

A recovery room can potentially contain prepared [Glyph of Warding](/library/advanced/complex-spells/glyph-of-warding/) infrastructure.

Possible purposes:

- Defensive protection
    
- Emergency buffs
    
- Intruder defenses
    
- Recovery assistance
    

But remember:

A Glyph network costs substantial gold and should not accidentally endanger the newly awakened Clone.

Design triggers carefully.

---

## Information Security

If enemies know:

**You have Clone**

and

**where the Clone is**

they can prepare for your resurrection.

Therefore Clone locations should be treated as sensitive information.

Possible policy:

- Party knows you have a backup
    
- Very few people know where
    
- Even fewer can access it
    

---

## False Backup

For extremely paranoid high-level play, an obvious secure vault could even serve as a decoy while the real Clone exists elsewhere.

This is campaign-dependent and generally unnecessary unless enemies are actively hunting your resurrection infrastructure.

---

## Age & Longevity

Clone lets you choose a younger version of the creature.

This creates a path toward extreme longevity:

**Age**

↓

**Maintain younger mature Clone**

↓

**Eventually die**

↓

**Return in younger body**

The spell does not state a minimum chosen age.

Exactly how aggressively this can be exploited may become a worldbuilding/DM issue.

---

## What Clone Does Not Do

Clone does **not**:

- Give you your equipment
    
- Give you your original corpse back
    
- Protect you during the first 120 days
    
- Force an unwilling soul to return
    
- Free a trapped soul
    
- Automatically provide an escape route
    
- Automatically protect its vessel
    
- Automatically update a backup spellbook
    
- Prevent enemies from discovering your infrastructure
    
- Make you literally impossible to kill
    

It is powerful because it removes one enormous failure state:

> **Ordinary death no longer needs to be permanent.**

---

## Priority Setup

When Clone becomes available:

### 1. Acquire or bypass the components

### 2. Select a secure location

### 3. Cast Clone

### 4. Build recovery equipment around it

### 5. Wait 120 days

### 6. Verify the Clone is mature

### 7. Establish redundancy

### 8. Maintain backup equipment and spellbooks

---

## Clone Doctrine

> **Clone is not immortality by itself.**
> 
> It is a backup body.

Its actual reliability depends on everything surrounding that body:

**Security**

-   
    

**Redundancy**

-   
    

**Equipment**

-   
    

**Information control**

-   
    

**Soul protection**

-   
    

**Exit planning**

-   
    

**120-day replacement cycle**

A careless Wizard owns a Clone.

A prepared Wizard owns a **recovery system**.

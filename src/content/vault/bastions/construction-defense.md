A Bastion can take almost any physical form. For a Wizard, the goal is usually a **secure, compact, expandable base** with good access to civilization and magical transportation.

## Acquiring a Bastion

A Bastion normally becomes available at **character level 5**.

This assumes the DM has enabled the optional Bastion system. A purchased building alone does not grant Special Facility benefits outside that system.

It does not need to begin as a newly constructed house. A suitable structure can become your Bastion through the campaign.

Examples:

- Building a new Wizard tower
- Purchasing a property
- Receiving a stronghold as a reward
- Inheriting a property
- Restoring an abandoned tower
- Rebuilding a ruin
- Claiming a captured stronghold
- Combining headquarters with the rest of the party

The exact location and method of acquisition are established with the DM.

---

## Choosing a Location

There is no single required location for a Bastion.

For a Wizard, useful considerations are:

1. **Security**
2. **Access to civilization**
3. **Access to spell components and trade**
4. **Privacy**
5. **Space for expansion**
6. **Reliable transportation**
7. **Political stability**

## Wizard Tower

A compact tower is an excellent general layout.

### Advantages

- Small ground footprint
- Vertical expansion
- Easy separation of facilities by floor
- Small defensive perimeter
- Natural location for an Observatory
- Easy to organize and secure

## Underground Bastion

An underground complex is another strong option.

### Advantages

- Few exposed entrances
- Naturally concealed
- Large expansion potential
- Small surface footprint
- Easier to control access

### Disadvantages

- An Observatory may require a separate surface structure
- Access to civilization can be less convenient

## City Bastion

A Bastion inside a major settlement prioritizes convenience.

### Advantages

- Shops
- Craftspeople
- Spell components
- Scrolls
- Libraries
- NPC spellcasters
- Information
- Political connections

### Disadvantages

- Less privacy
- Local laws
- Limited expansion
- More people around your magical projects

## Recommended Wizard Location

A strong general choice is:

**A compact Wizard tower near a major settlement, but outside the dense city center.**

This provides access to trade and civilization while maintaining privacy, security, and room for expansion.

At higher levels, Teleportation Circle, Teleport, and other magical transportation make physical distance increasingly less important.

## Facility Space

Facilities are divided into three size categories.

| Facility Size | Maximum Area |
|---|---:|
| **Cramped** | 4 × 5-foot squares |
| **Roomy** | 16 × 5-foot squares |
| **Vast** | 36 × 5-foot squares |

Facilities do not all need to be located on the same floor.

These are counts of individual **5-foot-by-5-foot squares**, not rooms 4, 16, or 36 squares along each edge.

## Basic facility construction

A new Bastion starts with **two free Basic Facilities**, one Cramped and one Roomy. Further Basic Facilities use the following costs; their furnishings are included, but they do not grant Special Facility mechanics.

| New Basic Facility | Cost | Construction time |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cramped | 500 GP | 20 days |
| Roomy | 1,000 GP | 45 days |
| Vast | 3,000 GP | 125 days |

Enlarging Cramped to Roomy costs **500 GP / 25 days**; Roomy to Vast costs **2,000 GP / 80 days**. Multiple Basic Facility projects can run simultaneously and do not require the owner's presence. **Special Facilities cannot be purchased this way**: they come from level progression. A Special Facility's own enlargement rule takes precedence over these Basic Facility costs.

A Wizard tower can therefore stack facilities vertically, while an underground Bastion can spread them across connected chambers.

This makes compact structures particularly attractive because a large amount of usable facility space can exist behind a relatively small defensive perimeter.

## Defensive Walls

Defensive Walls can be constructed around a Bastion.

## Wall Cost

**Defensive Wall: 250 gp per 5-foot section**

Each 5-foot square of wall takes **10 days** to build and is **20 feet high**. If the Bastion is **completely enclosed**, an Attack event uses **two fewer defender-loss dice**. A partial wall, a normal room's walls, or a compact footprint alone does not grant that mechanical reduction. Whether spell-built construction replaces the stated cost/time is a DM adjudication, not an automatic Fabricate or Wall of Stone discount.

| Wall Length | Cost |
|---:|---:|
| 5 ft | 250 gp |
| 10 ft | 500 gp |
| 20 ft | 1,000 gp |
| 50 ft | 2,500 gp |
| 100 ft | 5,000 gp |
| 200 ft | 10,000 gp |

The important optimization factor is therefore **perimeter**, not total interior floor space.

### Compact Tower

A tower can place facilities on several floors while maintaining a relatively small ground perimeter.

### Sprawling Compound

A large horizontal compound requires considerably more wall to protect the same amount of usable facility space.

### Wizard Recommendation

**Build vertically or underground when practical.**

This allows more facility space without proportionally increasing Defensive Wall costs.

## Party Bastions

Party members can combine their individual Bastions into a single larger headquarters.

Each character still controls their own Special Facilities.

This can be particularly effective for an optimized party because characters can specialize their facility choices.

### Example

**Wizard**
- Arcane Study
- Library
- Scriptorium
- Teleportation Circle
- Observatory
- Demiplane

**Martial Character**
- Defensive facilities
- Defenders
- Military infrastructure

**Other Characters**
- Crafting
- Resource production
- Social facilities
- Healing/support infrastructure

### Advantage for the Wizard

The Wizard does not need to spend valuable Special Facility choices covering every function of the headquarters.

Other party members can handle:

- Physical defense
- Resource generation
- Military infrastructure
- Social functions

while the Wizard concentrates on magical infrastructure.

## How an Attack event actually works

The default Attack event is not a tactical siege simulation: the hostile force is defeated, then **6d6** are rolled and each 1 kills a Bastion Defender. Complete Defensive Walls reduce this to **4d6**. A stocked Armory replaces each d6 with a **d8**, making each loss less likely; its stock is expended after the event even if no defender dies. Stocking costs **100 GP + 100 GP per defender**, halved if the Bastion has a Smithy.

If the Bastion has no defenders, a randomly determined Special Facility shuts down for the next Bastion Turn, then repairs for free. A Barrack recruits up to **four defenders** per Recruit order for no money, housing **12**, or **25** after its 2,000 GP enlargement. Combined Bastions can allocate defender losses between them, but do not merge their facility slots or hirelings.

**Optimization:** for the normal Attack event, a modest defender reserve can protect facility uptime more cheaply than an enormous wall. Walls, wards, and layouts may matter much more if the DM runs an actual adventure at the Bastion. Do not assume a spell defense changes the event dice unless a rule or the DM says it does.

## Magical Defense

A Wizard can supplement ordinary Bastion defenses with permanent or long-duration magical effects.

## Useful Defensive Spells

| Spell | Main Use |
|---|---|
| Arcane Lock | Secure important doors, containers, and entrances |
| Alarm | Detect unauthorized entry |
| Glyph of Warding | Prepared magical defenses and triggers |
| Magic Circle | Protection and containment against specific creature types |
| Mordenkainen's Private Sanctum | Privacy, anti-divination, anti-teleportation, and planar protection |
| Guards and Wards | Large-scale magical protection for buildings |
| Programmed Illusion | Persistent deception, warnings, and misdirection |
| Symbol | Powerful high-level magical ward |
| Antipathy/Sympathy | Long-duration creature attraction or repulsion |
| Sequester | Hide and protect important creatures or objects |

The Wizard's main advantage is **downtime preparation**.

Many defensive effects can be established before an attack ever happens, allowing spell slots, gold, and downtime to be converted into long-term security.

## High-Level Wizard Security

At higher levels, a Wizard can build several layers of security rather than relying on walls alone.

## Layer 1 — Information Security

Prevent enemies from easily observing or locating the Bastion and its important contents.

Useful spells:

- Mordenkainen's Private Sanctum
- Nondetection
- Sequester
- Mind Blank

---

## Layer 2 — Physical Security

Control how creatures physically enter and move through the Bastion.

Useful options:

- Defensive Walls
- Limited entrances
- Arcane Lock
- Glyph of Warding
- Guards and Wards
- Bastion Defenders

---

## Layer 3 — Magical Security

Protect against magical intrusion and dangerous supernatural creatures.

Useful spells:

- Magic Circle
- Glyph of Warding
- Symbol
- Antipathy/Sympathy
- Mordenkainen's Private Sanctum

---

## Layer 4 — Transportation Security

A high-level Wizard should not depend entirely on normal entrances and roads.

**Avoid blocking the escape route with the ward.** Private Sanctum can block teleportation into/out of its area and planar travel within it, affecting friendly travel too. Keep a destination circle outside those selected protections or plan a lawful way to remove/change them; the spell offers selectable protections, not an ally whitelist for its teleport restriction. [2024 Private Sanctum](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/phb-2024/spell-descriptions#MordenkainensPrivateSanctum).

Useful options:

- Teleportation Circle
- Teleport
- Plane Shift
- Demiplane

This allows the Wizard and party to enter or leave the Bastion without relying entirely on its physical entrance.
